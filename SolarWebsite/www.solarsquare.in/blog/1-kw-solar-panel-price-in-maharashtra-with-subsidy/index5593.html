TL;DR Summary Box: What is the Subsidy for 1 kW Solar Panels in Maharashtra?

Homeowners installing a 1 kW grid-connected solar system in Maharashtra can receive a subsidy of Rs. 30,000 under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana. You, too, can benefit from this financial assistance offered by the central government if you fulfill the simple eligibility criteria listed below:

  • You are an Indian citizen installing a grid-tied solar system for personal use. Off-grid and commercial rooftop systems do not qualify for a subsidy under this scheme.
  • You choose made-in-India, ALMM-approved solar panels.
  • You have an electricity connection in your name with your local DISCOM.
  • You own the property where the solar system is supposed to be installed.
  • You haven’t ever received a solar subsidy before for systems up to 3 kW.

How to Apply for a Solar Subsidy in Maharashtra?

Eligible homeowners can register an account on the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana National Portal and follow the steps listed below to receive a solar subsidy straight into their bank accounts within 30 days of the subsidy claim approval:

  • Step 1 – Visit the National Portal: Access the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana National Portal and open the section where rooftop solar applications are submitted.
  • Step 2 – Create the consumer account: Select the state, district, and DISCOM, then register by entering the Customer Account Number linked to the property where solar installation will happen.
  • Step 3 – Fill out the rooftop solar form: Provide the required personal details and enter the electricity consumption information taken from your latest electricity bill.
  • Step 4 – Enter bank details: Upload the documents requested on the portal, add the bank account information, and attach a scanned copy of a cancelled cheque.
  • Step 5 – Choose an empanelled installer: Select a company, such as SolarSquare, from the DISCOM-approved installer list to complete the rooftop solar installation at your home.
  • Step 6 – Upload the signed agreement: Review the agreement shared by the installer, sign it, and upload the signed document to the portal.
  • Step 7 – Net meter installation: After installation of the solar system, the DISCOM replaces the existing energy meter with a bi-directional or smart meter.
  • Step 8 – Receive the commissioning certificate: Once the DISCOM verifies that the system is functioning correctly, it issues the Solar Commissioning Certificate.
  • Step 9 – Submit the subsidy request: Log in again, upload the commissioning certificate, and submit the subsidy claim.
  • Step 10 – Subsidy disbursement: After approval, the subsidy amount is transferred to the registered bank account within 30 days.

What are the Types of 1 kW Solar Systems and Their Prices in Maharashtra?

On-grid, off-grid, and hybrid solar systems are the most common photovoltaic systems homeowners choose between when installing rooftop solar at their homes. The right system type largely depends on how reliable the grid connection in your area is. 

  • If the grid is reliable: Choose on-grid solar systems in this case because when power outages are not an issue, solar energy storage is not a necessity. Since it does not require lead-acid or lithium batteries to store solar energy, an on-grid PV system is extremely economical and offers the highest ROI. 
  • If the grid is unavailable or unreliable: When there’s no grid supply, off-grid solar becomes the obvious choice, as it uses a battery bank to store solar energy, which can keep the load running at night. In areas where the grid is unreliable and power outages are frequent, off-grid or hybrid solar systems are better investments, as they store solar energy in batteries that can be used at night or wherever needed.

Is a 1 kW Solar Panel Sufficient for Homes?

A 1 kW solar system generates just enough electricity to light up a few fans, lights, and small appliances such as a mixer/grinder, laptops, phones, and an induction cooktop. Since it can generate only 1,400 to 1,450 units of electricity annually, it can’t power appliances such as a washing machine or an electric geyser, which are pretty common household appliances. Hence, a 1 kW solar system is usually not sufficient to meet all energy needs in a home.

How to Determine the Right Solar System Size for Your Home?

If you want your electricity bills to drop to zero or near zero with solar, it’s important to choose a system size that can generate as much electricity as your home consumes annually. 

Here are some simple steps you can follow to get an idea of the approximate annual energy consumption in your home and how you can use it to find out the right solar system capacity:

  • Step 1 – Gather electricity bills from the past year: Collect all monthly bills issued over the last 12 months.
  • Step 2 – Add the unit readings from each bill: Write down the units mentioned on every bill and total them to find the yearly consumption.
  • Step 3 – Compare the yearly total with the output of a 1 kW system: A 1 kW solar installation generates around 1,400 to 1,450 units annually. Divide the yearly unit total by 1,400 to estimate the required kW capacity.

FAQs

Q1. How many ACs can run on a 1 kW solar system in Maharashtra?

Ans. A 1 kW solar system is not suitable for running an AC because air conditioners need a high surge to start, which a 1 kW system cannot supply reliably. A single 1.5-ton AC requires a 3 kW solar system, while two 1.5-ton ACs usually need a 5 kW system.

Q2. Which company’s solar panel is best?

Ans. Some of the most reliable solar panel manufacturers in India that supply latest-technology panels include ReNew Solar, Premier Energies, and Rayzon Solar.

Q3. How much space is needed for a 1 kW solar panel system?

Ans. It takes around 100 square feet of shadow-free area on a roof to install a 1 kW on-grid solar system.

Q4. What is the lifespan of a 1 kW solar panel system?

Ans. With professional cleaning and maintenance, a 1 kW solar system can last for at least 25 years, and continue generating power at ~80% of its original efficiency after that.
