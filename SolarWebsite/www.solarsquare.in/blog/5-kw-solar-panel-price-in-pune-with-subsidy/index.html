What’s the 5 kW Solar System Price in Pune without Subsidy in 2026?

The cost of a 5 kW solar system in Pune without subsidy in 2026 for on-grid PV systems is ~₹ 3.23 lakh*. However, as a homeowner, you can install rooftop solar in Pune at much cheaper rates if you meet certain eligibility conditions set by the government, as listed below:

  • You are an Indian citizen with an active electricity connection in your name with your local DISCOM.
  • You’ve never received a subsidy for systems up to 3 kW.
  • You choose only ALMM-approved, made-in-India solar panels.
  • Your solar system is grid-connected and for personal use, because off-grid and commercial rooftop systems are not eligible for a subsidy under the PM Surya Ghar scheme.
  • You have the legal ownership of the property or roof where the system will be installed.

How to Apply for a Solar Subsidy in Pune?

Homeowners in Pune can have the solar subsidy amount credited to their bank account by registering on the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana National Portal and following the steps listed below.

  • Step 1 – Open the official portal: Visit the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana National Portal and navigate to the section where rooftop solar applications are submitted.
  • Step 2 – Create your consumer profile: Select the state, district, and DISCOM. Then, create a profile by entering the Customer Account Number from the electricity bill.
  • Step 3 – Fill in the rooftop solar application form: Enter the required personal information, and enter the latest bill’s unit details exactly as shown.
  • Step 4 – Upload bank details and documents: Provide your bank account information, attach a cancelled cheque, and upload the documents listed on the portal. This account is where the subsidy will be sent after the claim is processed and approved.
  • Step 5 – Select an empanelled installer: Choose an installer from the DISCOM-approved list available on the portal. This installer will complete the rooftop solar setup in your home.
  • Step 6 – Upload the signed installer agreement: Go through the agreement shared by the selected installer, sign it, and upload the signed copy to the portal.
  • Step 7 – Net meter installation: After the solar system is installed, the DISCOM installs a bi-directional or smart meter that records imported and exported electricity.
  • Step 8 – Issuance of the commissioning certificate: When the DISCOM verifies that the system is functioning properly, it issues the Solar Commissioning Certificate.
  • Step 9 – Submit the subsidy claim: Log in again and upload the commissioning certificate to move the subsidy claim forward.
  • Step 10 – Receive the subsidy amount: After the claim is approved, the central subsidy is deposited into the registered bank account within 30 days of the approval.

What are the Types of 5 kW Solar Systems and Their Prices in Pune?

Choosing between on-grid, off-grid, and hybrid solar systems is one of the most important decisions when going solar. Each type has specific strengths, and the right option depends on how reliable the grid is and your budget. 

  • When should you choose an on-grid solar system? On-grid systems are the most economical because they do not use lead-acid or lithium batteries to store solar energy. They’re connected to the grid, and the system can easily exchange power with the grid (both import and export). They are best suited for locations with stable grid power and minimal outages.
  • When should you choose an off-grid solar system? Off-grid systems operate independently of the grid and store solar energy in a battery bank for use at night. They are ideal in regions where grid supply is unavailable or frequently disrupted.
  • When should you choose a hybrid solar system? Hybrid solar systems are connected to the grid and a battery bank. They’re most recommended in commercial, residential, and industrial installations where battery backup is non-negotiable.

Why Choose SolarSquare’s 5.40 kW Solar System?

Most new home installations in India use high-wattage and high-efficiency solar panels, such as 540-watt mono-PERC or TOPCon modules. If you do the math to find the number of 540-watt panels you’ll need in reality to build a 5 kW solar system, it won’t add up. That’s because you would require 9.25 540-watt solar panels (5,000 ÷ 540) to reach exactly 5 kW capacity. But panels can only be installed in whole numbers, not fractions. 

Therefore, the real choice is between 9 and 10 panels. 

If you use 9 540-watt solar panels, the resulting system will be 4.86 kW, not 5 kW. A 4.86 kW PV system will not meet all your home’s energy requirements. Due to lower power generation, this downsized system won’t be able to bring your bills to near zero or deliver the massive savings expected from solar.

To resolve this issue, SolarSquare designed a 5.40 kW solar system using 10 540-watt solar panels. Since this results in higher daily power generation, the solar savings you receive are massive, and your electricity bills drop to zero or near zero.
