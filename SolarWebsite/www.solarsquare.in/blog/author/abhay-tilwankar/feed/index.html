Why Pigeons Nest Under Solar Panels

Pigeons usually nest under solar panels because it is a perfect hidden place that looks like their natural habitat. The gap under the panels also provides shade, warmth, and a dry environment good for them and their squabs.

  • The gap under the panel is a perfect nest site: You may have noticed solar panels sit slightly raised off the roof, usually to allow airflow and the right tilt angle. This creates a shallow, sheltered gap, which is an ideal place for any bird to live. It’s warm, dry, shielded from wind and rain, and mostly out of the predators’ reach.
  • Roofs and terraces already attract pigeons: In most places in India, pigeons are very common on roofs and terraces. Even if you have not installed solar panels, rooftops already attract pigeons. Open water tanks, nearby food sources, ledges, and parapets all make a terrace attractive. When you install a rooftop solar system with a sheltered gap under solar panels, you’ve essentially built them a ready-made home.
  • Once pigeons nest, they tend to return: Pigeons are creatures of habit. A pair that successfully raises chicks under your panel will often come back to the same spot for future broods. This is why early prevention matters so much more than dealing with it after the fact.

How to Prevent Pigeons Under Solar Panels: 6 Methods

Use mesh with non-penetrating clips around the panel edges, paired with spikes on nearby perching spots to stop pigeons from nesting under solar panels. Solar panel skirts and removing food or water sources help too. Below are all 6 methods explained in detail.

1. Solar Panel Mesh with Clips (Best Option)

Using solar panel mesh with clips is one of the most effective and recommended methods to prevent pigeons from nesting under solar panels. A mesh barrier is fitted around the panel’s perimeter, closing off the gap underneath. The clips are used to attach this mesh. These non-penetrating clips grip the panel frame without drilling into it.

You must make sure you use PVC-coated stainless-steel mesh, which holds up far better over years of sun and rain compared to cheap galvanised wire mesh, which tends to rust and sag.

2. Solar panel skirts

Skirts are a low-visibility alternative to mesh. They run along the edge of the panel array, closing the gap in a way that’s barely noticeable from the ground. They work on a similar principle to mesh but tend to blend in more visually.

3. Bird spikes

Spikes work well on ridges, edges, parapets, and any flat surface where pigeons like to perch before moving in. They’re not a substitute for closing off the gap under the panels, though. Spikes stop birds from landing on a surface; they don’t stop birds from entering an open gap. You can use them alongside mesh, not instead of it.

4. Bird netting

If you have a large terrace or open areas with a lot of exposed roof space, netting can cover a wider area than mesh or skirts alone. You must use bird netting for bigger installations or terraces where pigeons have multiple potential landing and nesting zones, not just the space under the panels.

5. Decoys and predator statues

You can add fake owls or predator decoys that can provide short-term deterrence. The catch is pigeons are smart enough to figure out a decoy isn’t a real threat once it stays in the same spot for a while. You can treat this as a minor, temporary measure at best, not a real solution.

6. Remove food, water, and perches

If you want to keep pigeons away from your panels, start by removing what draws them there in the first place. Clean up any open waste or food scraps lying around. Cover up standing water in open tanks. And trim back any overhanging tree branches, since they give birds an easy landing spot right next to your roof.

Problems Pigeons Cause Under Solar Panels

Pigeons nesting under your panels brings dirty droppings, a real fire risk from nesting material near wires, possible cable damage and hot spots, plus noise, mess and health concerns from the droppings. Here’s a closer look at each one.

  • Droppings and soiling: Pigeon droppings build up on and around the panels over time. This affects the overall efficiency of the solar panels. A panel covered in droppings simply can’t soak up as much sunlight as a clean one, which means it generates less power than it should.

Signs Pigeons Are Nesting Under Your Panels

Not sure if you already have a problem? If you notice two or more of these, it’s worth checking under the panel directly, or calling in a professional to look. Look out for:

  • Droppings or feathers collecting at the lower edge of the panel
  • Twigs or nesting debris visible under the frame
  • Cooing sounds coming from under the panels, especially early morning
  • Birds repeatedly flying in and out of the same gap

Why Airflow and Warranty Matter When Proofing

If you have solar panels at home, you should know the panels need airflow underneath to stay cool. A panel that runs hotter than it should loses efficiency. So you should avoid sealing the gap completely with a solid sheet or board, even if it seems like the simplest fix.

Instead of using a solid sheet or board, mesh allows air to pass through while still keeping birds out, which is exactly why it’s the preferred method.

Also, make sure you never drill into the panel frame, and never fix anything directly to the glass. Both of them can void your manufacturer’s warranty immediately. Before doing any DIY proofing work, check your installer’s warranty terms first. Some installers include specific conditions about what modifications are and aren’t allowed on the mounting structure.

Already Have Pigeons Nesting? Do This First

Before disturbing the nest, you must check the legal position first, wait until it’s abandoned if there are eggs or chicks, clean the area safely, and seal the gap right after so the pigeons can’t come back.

  • Check the legal position: Pigeons in India fall under general bird protection provisions, and interfering with an active nest isn’t something to do casually. You must check with a local pest control or wildlife professional about your specific situation before removing any nest, rather than assuming it’s fine to clear it out yourself. Rules and local interpretation can vary, so this is one area where it’s worth getting it right rather than guessing.
  • Wait until the nest is abandoned: If there are eggs or chicks present, the safest and most humane approach is to wait until the nest is naturally abandoned, or the chicks have fledged.
  • Clean and disinfect the area safely: Once the nest is empty, clean the area properly. Wear gloves and a mask, and use a damp cloth or spray to wipe down droppings rather than dry-sweeping them, which can send dust and particles into the air.
  • Install mesh straight afterwards: Don’t leave the gap open once you’ve cleaned it. Pigeons will return to a spot that worked for them before. Installing mesh or a skirt immediately after cleanup is what actually stops them from coming back.

Prevent It from Day One: What to Ask Your Solar Installer

The cheapest and easiest time to pigeon-proof your system is during installation itself, before any bird has had the chance to settle in. You must ask your installer directly whether bird-proofing can be included as part of the original setup.

Also ask about your solar mounting structure height and gap size. Flush-mount systems, which sit closer to the roof with a narrower gap, tend to see this problem less than elevated mounting structures with a wider clearance underneath.

You must ask your solar installer about their standard approach to this issue, whether proofing is offered as part of the installation package, and what material and clip type they use.

Frequently Asked Question

Can pigeons damage solar panels?

Not directly; they’re not going to crack your panel glass or anything like that. But their droppings pile up and make the panels dirty, any nesting material sitting near your wiring can be a fire risk, and if debris builds up long enough, it can create shaded patches that turn into hot spots.

Does mesh reduce power output of the solar panels?

No, mesh sits around the edges of the panel, not across the surface, so it’s not blocking any sunlight or airflow. It shouldn’t affect your system’s output if installed correctly by someone who understands panel placement.

Does proofing void the solar panel warranty?

It can, but only if it’s done the wrong way. If you are drilling into the panel frame or sticking anything onto the glass, that can void your warranty. You can use non-penetrating clips; they hold the mesh in place without touching the panel itself.

Is it legal to remove a pigeon nest?

This depends on local rules and whether the nest is active with eggs or chicks. You must check with a local pest control professional or your municipal authority before removing any nest, rather than assuming it’s permitted.

Do spikes alone work for pigeons nesting under the solar panels?

Spikes are great for stopping pigeons from perching on ledges and flat edges, but they won’t do anything about that open gap under your panels. You really need mesh alongside the spikes if you want this handled properly.

How long does mesh last?

This depends heavily on material quality. PVC-coated stainless-steel mesh tends to last several years with minimal degradation, while cheaper galvanised wire mesh can rust and weaken much sooner, especially in humid or coastal regions.

What Is PM Surya Ghar 2.0?

PM Surya Ghar 2.0 (also searched as PM Surya Ghar Yojana 2.0 or PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana 2.0) is an informal label. It describes the possible next phase of India’s residential rooftop solar programme. It is not an official scheme name. Here is how that label came about, and how much of it is confirmed.

Where Things Stand Today

PM Surya Ghar 2.0 has not been officially launched. The original scheme began in February 2024 with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore, and it is meant to run until the end of the financial year (FY) 2026-27, as MNRE’s scheme guidelines and Press Information Bureau (PIB) material describe. Today the solar subsidy in India depends on installed capacity alone; batteries get no subsidy of their own, and apartment residents largely sit outside the scheme. The 2.0 talk began after MNRE reportedly held a brainstorming session on 23 July 2026 on the next roadmap. Trade media said a discussion document would follow, but nothing has been notified since, so treat every 2.0 detail as a proposal.

PM Surya Ghar 2.0 Subsidy: What You Can Count On Today

People search “PM Surya Ghar 2.0 subsidy” hoping for a new number. There isn’t one. The numbers below apply to the current scheme and are in effect now.

The central financial assistance (CFA) is calculated per kilowatt (kW) and capped at 3 kW, as listed on state government scheme pages and the national portal.

So a 5 kW system gets the same central subsidy as a 3 kW one. The subsidy is credited by Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) to your bank account through the portal.

Subsidy for Housing Societies

Group Housing Societies (GHS) and Resident Welfare Associations (RWA) get ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities, including EV charging, up to 500 kW (calculated at 3 kW per house), as per official scheme listings.

PM Surya Ghar Yojana 2.0 Eligibility: Who Can Apply?

No separate eligibility list exists for 2.0. What you can apply under today is the existing scheme, so here is who it covers and what the proposals might widen.

Eligibility Under the Current Scheme

According to MNRE’s reply in the Lok Sabha on 29 July 2026, all residential consumers with a grid-connected electricity connection from their local distribution company (DISCOM) can apply on the National Portal for PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana. In practice, you will need:

  • A residential electricity connection in your name
  • A rooftop with enough shadow-free space
  • A system installed by a vendor registered on the portal
  • Equipment that meets the scheme’s domestic content rules

Commercial and industrial buildings are not covered by the residential subsidy.

Could Eligibility Expand Under 2.0?

Possibly. Apartment residents and renters without their own roof are the obvious gap, which is why shared solar keeps coming up in the reports. Until MNRE says so officially, don’t assume you qualify through a route that doesn’t exist yet.

What Has Changed in PM Surya Ghar Rules in 2026?

While 2.0 stays a proposal, the existing scheme has been tightened and simplified. These are the changes that matter to a homeowner.

Easier Approvals and Cheaper Loans

MNRE’s Lok Sabha reply lists the measures taken to speed things up:

  • Collateral-free loans from nationalised banks at repo rate plus 50 basis points (5.75% a year at present) for 10 years
  • Technical feasibility requirement waived, with auto load enhancement up to 10 kW
  • Net metering agreement built into the portal application, so no separate paperwork
  • A grievance helpline, 15555, running in 12 languages

Equipment and Quality Rules

Domestic content matters. Under the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) List-II, solar cells must be on the approved list for projects commissioned after 1 June 2026, as industry guides report. MNRE has also set a 15-day window for vendors to fix defects flagged in third-party inspections. In plain words, a poor installation can delay your subsidy.

Progress and Deadline

As of 27 July 2026, MNRE reported 40,45,298 systems installed, 48,80,796 households benefited, and ₹27,343.9 crore released as CFA. The official timeline still runs to FY 2026-27. Reports of an extension exist, but we found no notification.

Should You Wait for PM Surya Ghar 2.0?

For most households, no. The scheme you can use is open, funded, and paying out. Waiting means paying higher electricity bills each month for a scheme with no date. A few things worth weighing:

  • Apartment dwellers: You may be better off watching for official shared-solar rules, or checking whether your society can apply as a GHS or RWA.

PM Surya Ghar 2.0 is, for now, a reported idea with no notification, no subsidy amount, and no launch date. The scheme that pays today is the existing one: up to ₹ 78,000 in central subsidy, collateral-free loans, simpler approvals, and a fixed window that runs to 31 March 2027. What is a Solar Investment Plan?

A solar investment plan is a financing arrangement that lets a homeowner install rooftop solar without paying the full cost from personal savings at once. The homeowner repays the financed amount through monthly installments while the system helps reduce electricity purchases from the grid.

Why is It Called an SIP?

SIP usually stands for Systematic Investment Plan, a method commonly associated with mutual funds. In this case, it describes a structured way of investing in solar energy systems through regular payments.

  • A conventional SIP requires you to invest fresh money periodically to build a financial corpus.
  • A solar investment plan redirects part of an existing household expense towards a physical asset. The system generates electricity, and the resulting bill savings act as its financial return.

What Makes Solar an Investment Rather Than an Expense?

A normal electricity bill pays for power already consumed. A solar investment, on the other hand, creates a physical asset that can generate free electricity from solar radiation for many years. Its value comes from lower electricity bills and, where applicable, credits for surplus electricity exported to the grid (in case of on-grid or hybrid solar systems).

However, a rooftop solar system generates returns differently from a traditional deposit. As this comparison of solar vs. fixed deposit returns explains, a Fixed Deposit (FD) earns interest on the money deposited. In contrast, rooftop solar creates value by reducing a recurring household expense.

How Does a Solar Investment Plan Work?

Accounts that are dormant, frozen, KYC-incomplete, or subject to transaction restrictions can fail this check even if every document you uploaded was correct. A basic savings account with a low transaction cap can also create problems when the subsidy amount is being credited.

Fix: Confirm with your bank that the account is active, fully KYC-compliant, and able to receive the credit. If it’s been inactive for a while, complete re-KYC and run a small transaction before you reapply.

17. Bank name or IFSC changed after a merger

Several public sector banks have merged in recent years, and the IFSC codes of the merged branches changed as a result. If your passbook or cancelled cheque still carries the old bank name and IFSC, the details won’t validate against current records, even though the account itself is perfectly active.

Fix: Check your current IFSC with your bank or on your bank’s official website, and use the updated bank name and IFSC in the application. Upload a fresh cheque leaf or passbook page carrying the revised details.

18. Missing undertaking form or government ID proof

The application requires a signed undertaking along with a valid government-issued ID proof. Applications are commonly sent back when the undertaking is unsigned, incompletely filled, uploaded in the wrong format, or submitted without the accompanying ID document. An ID whose name or address doesn’t line up with the rest of the application creates the same problem.

Fix: Download the current undertaking format from the portal rather than reusing an old copy, fill it completely, sign it, and upload it with a clear scan of your ID proof.

19. Builder, developer, or promoter undertaking not provided

If the rooftop isn’t solely yours, such as in an apartment building, a housing society, or a property where the builder still holds possession or roof rights, you need written consent from the builder, developer, promoter, or society before the installation can be approved. Applications from flat owners and society members are frequently held up here because this document was never arranged.

Fix: Get the undertaking or NOC from the builder or the society’s managing committee in writing before you apply. In a society, this usually means a resolution passed at a committee meeting, so start the process early.

20. Geo-tagged photo requirement

Installation photographs uploaded after commissioning need to carry geo-tagging, showing the location coordinates of the installed system. Photos taken on a camera without location services enabled, images cropped or edited after capture, or screenshots of photos can strip out this data and delay the claim.

Fix: Ask your installer to capture the photographs using a geo-tagging camera app with location enabled, and to upload the original files without editing or compressing them.

Permanent vs Fixable Rejections

Not every rejection is the end of the road. Knowing which bucket your case falls into saves you time and false hope.

Permanent (cannot be reversed after the fact):

  1. Non-empanelled vendor: Any installation done by a vendor who is not registered on the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana portal will not be eligible for solar subsidy and, the subsidy for that property is permanently lost. The system may still function fine, but the solar subsidy is gone.
  2. Non-ALMM-listed panels: If the panels installed aren’t on the approved list, there’s no way to retroactively qualify that installation for CFA (Central Financial Assistance).
  3. DCR non-compliance: If the cells/modules installed don’t meet the applicable Domestic Content Requirement, including the ALMM List-II requirement for cells, the installation cannot be retroactively made compliant. Verification has to happen before installation.

Fixable (before or after rejection):

  1. Bank document issues (blurry, handwritten, wrong account type, invalid document type): Correctable by uploading fresh, machine-printed documents from an active savings account.
  2. Name, account number, or IFSC mismatches between the form and your documents: Correctable by updating records and resubmitting.
  3. PFMS name mismatch: Fixable by confirming the exact name held in your bank’s core banking records and entering that version, or by getting the bank record itself updated.
  4. Bank account validation pending or rejected: Fixable by activating a dormant account, completing re-KYC, or clearing any restriction with your bank, then resubmitting.
  5. Bank name or IFSC changed after a merger: Fixable by obtaining the revised IFSC from your bank and uploading a fresh cheque leaf or passbook page.
  6. Missing undertaking form or government ID proof: Fixable by downloading the current undertaking format, completing and signing it, and uploading it with a clear ID scan.
  7. Builder, developer, or promoter undertaking not provided: Fixable by obtaining written consent from the builder or the society’s managing committee. Arrange this early, as society approvals can take time.
  8. WCR/DCR certificate mismatches: Fixable by having the vendor re-verify and re-upload the correct serial numbers.
  9. Geo-tagged photo requirement not met: Fixable by having the installer recapture the photographs with location services enabled and upload the unedited original files.
  10. Wrong connection category (commercial/industrial/agricultural instead of residential): Fixable by getting the connection category corrected with your DISCOM, then reapplying.
  11. Incorrect consumer number or DISCOM selected: Fixable by re-entering the correct consumer number and DISCOM as printed on your electricity bill.
  12. Incomplete installation or commissioning details: Fixable by having the vendor upload the missing certificates, correct equipment details, or clearer photographs.
  13. Pending net meter or commissioning: Not a rejection at all; resolves once the pending step (inspection, meter replacement, commissioning, vendor upload, etc.) is completed.
  14. Flagged as duplicate or previously suspended: Fixable by getting any prior application against the same consumer number or account formally closed, then reapplying.

The golden rule: All three permanent rejection causes, which relate to your vendor and the equipment they install, are also 100% preventable before installation begins. They’re never discovered by accident. Verifying the vendor’s empanelment, the panel model, and DCR/ALMM compliance in writing at the start prevents every one of them.

The Pre-Installation Checklist: Prevent Subsidy Rejection Before It Happens

Before you finalize a solar company for your home rooftop solar installation, make sure you go through the following checklist:

  1. Before choosing a vendor:
  • Verify the vendor’s MNRE empanelment at pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Do not accept verbal confirmation.
  • Confirm the vendor’s proposed panels are ALMM-listed. Ask for the MNRE certificate number.
  1. Before registration on the portal:
  • Verify the name on your electricity bill and bank account match exactly, including spelling, initials, and order.
  • Upload a clear, machine-printed cancelled cheque or passbook copy from an active savings account. Avoid handwritten corrections, blur, or hidden IFSC/account numbers.
  • Re-check every manually typed bank detail (account number, IFSC) against the uploaded document before submitting.
  • Confirm your account is an active savings account, not current, NRE, PPF, loan, or cash credit.
  1. After installation:
  • Ask your installer to verify every module serial number on the DCR certificate matches the portal entry before it’s uploaded.
  • Cross-check the final application form against every uploaded document, account number, name, and IFSC code before final submission.

What to Do If Your PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana Application Is Rejected

A rejected PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana online application doesn’t always mean you’ve lost your subsidy. In many cases, the issue can be identified, corrected, and the application resubmitted or escalated through the right channels.

Here’s a checklist on how to proceed once the application is rejected:

  1. Get the rejection reason in writing: Log in to pmsuryaghar.gov.in and check your application status for the specific failure message. If it’s unclear, visit your local DISCOM section office in person.
  2. Identify the problem: Whether the rejection is permanent or fixable using the breakdown above.
  3. For fixable rejections: Rectify the specific issue, correct the name mismatch, re-upload a clear bank document, fix the DCR serial number mismatch, and resubmit the application as appropriate.
  4. For DBT failure after commissioning: Raise a grievance directly at pmsuryaghar.gov.in under the grievance section, or call the PM Surya Ghar helpline at 15555.
  5. If the DISCOM is unresponsive: Escalate to your state electricity regulatory commission, or file a consumer complaint through the DISCOM’s consumer grievance forum.
  6. For complex disputes: Contact MNRE’s (Ministry of New and Renewable Energy) national helpline at 1800-180-3333, and keep a written record of all correspondence in case you need to escalate formally.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I reapply for PM Surya Ghar subsidy after rejection?

Yes, for fixable rejections like bank document issues, name/account mismatches, DCR certificate errors, or incomplete portal steps, you can correct the issue and resubmit or continue the same application. Permanent rejections (non-empanelled vendor, non-ALMM panels) cannot be reapplied for.

Why is name mismatch such a common PM Surya Ghar rejection cause?

Because the subsidy is credited via DBT (Direct Bank Transfer), the system cross-checks your name across your electricity bill and your bank account. Even small differences in spelling or initials can cause an automatic mismatch flag, since these are separate databases that don’t always sync.

What does “non-ALMM panels” mean, and why does it cause the solar subsidy rejection?

ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) is MNRE’s official list of panel makers and models eligible for subsidy schemes. If a vendor installs panels that aren’t on the approved list, the system may not qualify for CFA, even if the installation meets quality standards.

My PM Surya Ghar subsidy was not credited even after commissioning. What should I do?

First, check that your bank account is an active savings account and that the account number and IFSC code exactly match what you entered on the portal. If that’s confirmed and the amount still hasn’t arrived, raise a grievance on pmsuryaghar.gov.in or call the helpline at 15555.

Can a non-empanelled vendor’s work be “regularized” after installation to claim the subsidy?

No. Empanelment status is verified against the vendor at the time of installation, and there is no process to retroactively qualify a non-empanelled installation for CFA. This is why vendor verification has to happen before any contract is signed.

How long does it take to receive the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

The time it takes to receive the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy depends on whether your application and documents are complete and accurate. Once your request is processed, the central subsidy is credited directly to your bank account via a direct transfer, usually within 30-45 days of the plant being commissioned. No cheque collection or office visit is needed.

What should I do if my solar subsidy application is returned for correction?

If your solar subsidy application is returned for correction, first check the reason mentioned on the PM Surya Ghar portal. Common issues include mismatched names, incorrect bank details, blurry document uploads, or errors in the DCR certificate or Work Completion Report (WCR). Correct the highlighted issue, upload the revised documents if required, and resubmit the application.
