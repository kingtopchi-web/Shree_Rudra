FAQs

Q1. How many solar panels are required for 5kW?

Ans. To generate 5 kW of power using SolarSquare’s bifacial Monoperc half-cut solar panels, which have a peak capacity of 545 watts each, you’d require about 10 to 11 panels. The calculation is straightforward:

5,000 Watts / 545 Watts per panel = 9.25 panels, rounding up to 10 panels.

Older panel technologies would need more modules to reach the same capacity.

Q2. How much electricity does a 5kW Solar system generate?

Ans. A 5 kW solar system can generate approximately 6,500 to 7,000 units of electricity each year, depending on the amount of sunlight in your location. This output is usually enough to cover the energy needs of a medium to large household, particularly those with higher electricity consumption.

Q3. How many appliances can be powered by a 5kW rooftop solar?

Ans. This rooftop solar capacity can comfortably power the following:

  • 4-5 ceiling fans
  • 3-4 LED bulbs
  • 1-2 tube lights
  • 2 ACs (1.5 tons each) or 1 AC along with other heavy appliances
  • Refrigerator
  • Washing machine
  • Television
  • 2 geysers
  • Kitchen appliances like a microwave, mixer, or induction cooktop
  • Laptops and charging devices

Q4. How many ACs can run in a 5kW solar system?

Ans. A 5 kW solar system can typically power two 1.5-ton air conditioners simultaneously. Alternatively, you can run one AC along with other heavy appliances like a refrigerator, washing machine, or microwave, ensuring efficient energy use without overloading the system.

Q5. Is 5 kW enough to run a house?

Ans. Whether a 5 kW solar system is enough to power your home depends on your energy usage. For most average-sized Indian households, a 5 kW system can typically cover daily energy needs, including lighting, fans, a refrigerator, and other basic appliances.

However, if your household has higher energy requirements, like multiple AC units or heavy-duty appliances, you may need a larger system to meet your needs.

#1. Electrical Safety and Fire Hazards

Having an on grid solar system means your roof is accommodating powerful machinery that can have up to 1000 volts of electricity running through it. We’re not kidding! 

Factually speaking, solar panels produce DC electricity –

  1. – When the connection in your home is single-phase, that DC power can be as high as 600 volts
  2. – If the connection is 3-phase, the same DC power can be as high as 1000 volts

And there’s a reason households strictly run on AC (alternating current) power, not DC (Direct Current) — the let-go threshold of Direct Current is higher than that of Alternating Current. It means that AC current is less risky than DC current. While withdrawing from a DC shock is almost impossible, AC current still gives a chance! 

This is why alternating current is a safer choice for household devices! Therefore, a solar inverter is an integral part of solar PV systems as it converts DC power into AC power. 

Now, imagine for yourself — 

  • – What if the connection between the solar panels and the inverter is not secured properly? 
  • – What if the connectors are not standard MC4 connectors? 
  • – What if the cables aren’t crimped with the proper standard crimping tools?
  • – What if the DC cables used by the vendor are not standard cables that can handle the high outdoor DC voltage? 
  • – What if the screws come loose? 

In fact, loose connections can cause arcing, which can lead to fire accidents at the installation site. 

We can’t further insist on why solar installation should not be treated as a DIY project. You might find many videos on how to install solar PV systems yourself, but none of those videos will ever foretell the safety risks we just stated. #2. The Risk of Water Leakage on the Roof

Installing an on grid solar system on a rooftop requires drilling on the roof to erect the mounting structure. If those holes aren’t sealed to perfection, they will lead to water leakage issues after installation. And the situation will eventually get worse during the rainy season. 

Although it might not look that risky an affair to start with, do not be fooled — water leakage through cracks that mechanical anchoring cannot seal slowly weakens the infrastructure. It could bring down the entire roof. And not to forget, water is an exceptional conductor of electricity, which poses a risk of electric shocks.

  • – Cyclonic winds loosen the connections and joints of weak structures that can uproot the panels as well as the solar panel stands

]]> When researching the commercial solar panel cost, you should also understand how a solar panel generates electricity. PV cells in a solar panel are made of silicon. When the panel receives sunlight, the light particles get trapped in the thin silicon cells. This causes electrons to be knocked out of the silicon atoms. The electrons move and create a photovoltaic charge that generates DC electricity, which then gets converted into usable AC electricity.

If you want to know more about commercial solar panel cost and the subsidy available in India, dig in deeper.

Types of Commercial Solar Panels

When planning for a commercial solar installation, you should keep in mind that commercial solar panel cost varies depending on the type of solar panels you use. The two most commonly used solar panels in PV systems are discussed below:

  • Monocrystalline solar panels

Monocrystalline solar panels are produced using a single silicon crystal; hence, the name. They are known for their black colour and high efficiency. 

Mono panels have a lifespan of 25 years and a high-efficiency range of 19-20% due to the low-temperature coefficient of 0.35% per degree Celsius. Besides, the annual degradation rate of these panels is just 0.5% per year. 

The monocrystalline commercial solar panel cost is approximately ₹31 per watt.

  • – Polycrystalline solar panels 

Also called multicrystalline panels, polycrystalline panels are cheaper than mono panels. However, they have a lower efficiency rate of 16-17%. They also have a degradation rate of 0.7% per year, which is higher than for mono panels.

Notably, the polycrystalline commercial solar panel cost is around ₹25.5 per watt.

Types of solar systems available

There are three types of solar systems available that you should be aware of. These include

  • On-Grid Solar System: This is the most popular option. It is connected to the grid and doesn’t have a battery. It requires less maintenance and is more economical. Also, subsidies (under specific conditions) are offered only for on-grid rooftop solar installations.
  • Off-Grid Solar System: This type of solar system is mostly used in rural areas. It comes with solar batteries to store energy for later use. It’s an independent solar plant since it’s not connected to the grid. But because of the batteries involved, it’s expensive.
  • Hybrid Solar System: This system has the features of both on-grid and off-grid solar plants. It uses battery backup and is also connected to the grid. Although it tackles the issue of power outages, the involvement of batteries again makes it an expensive option.
