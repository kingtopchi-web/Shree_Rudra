  • The PV (photovoltaic) system must be correctly sized.
  • The roof and shading need professional assessment.

As a homeowner, you are not expected to perform these technical assessments yourself. Your job is to make sure a qualified solar installer completes them and explains the findings before you approve the system.

This solar installation checklist covers what to verify before selecting your rooftop solar system, before installation begins, during installation, and before accepting the completed rooftop solar plant.

Solar Installation Checklist at a Glance

A complete rooftop solar installation checklist should cover the entire project, from electricity consumption and roof assessment to commissioning, monitoring, and handover. Checking only the panels or total installation price can leave important design, safety, and service requirements overlooked.

Here’s a snapshot of the rooftop solar installation checklist that every homeowner must follow:

Let us look at every stage in detail.

What Should Be Assessed Before Planning a Rooftop Solar Installation?

Before deciding the system capacity or comparing quotations, your electricity use, connection details, and rooftop need to be assessed. While some information can be collected from your electricity bill, roof measurements, shading, and the final panel layout should be assessed by a rooftop solar installer.

Here’s a step-by-step overview of the process –

1. Review Your Electricity Consumption

Your electricity consumption is one of the first inputs used to determine the suitable solar system capacity. Steps to check that –

Step 1. Review the electricity units consumed over the previous 6 to 12 months rather than looking only at the bill amount.

Step 2. Account for seasonal changes and any major loads you expect to add later, such as another air conditioner or an electric vehicle.

The objective is to design the plant around realistic annual electricity requirements rather than simply installing as many panels as the roof can accommodate.

2. Check Your Sanctioned Load and Electricity Connection

Your electricity bill contains information such as the sanctioned load and consumer connection details. Share these with the installer before the system is designed.

The installer should assess how the proposed solar capacity fits with your existing electrical connection and the applicable requirements of your DISCOM (distribution company).

Requirements can vary between states and distribution companies. So, this should be checked for your specific connection.

3. Get Your Roof Condition and Structural Suitability Assessed

A qualified installer should assess whether the roof is suitable for the proposed rooftop solar system before installation begins.

The assessment should identify issues such as:

  • Cracks or visible structural damage
  • Existing water leakage
  • Weak or aging roof sections
  • Waterproofing problems
  • Roof areas that may require repair
  • The type of roof and suitable mounting method

Any important roof repair should ideally be completed before the mounting structure is installed.

4. Get the Usable Shadow-free Roof Area and Panel Layout Assessed

The total size of your terrace differs from the area that can actually be used to install solar panels. Keep in mind that every 1 kW of rooftop solar needs at least 100 square feet of shadow-free area for installation.

During the site survey, the installer should account for:

  • Shadows from nearby buildings, trees, water tanks, staircase rooms, parapet walls, antennas, and other roof obstructions
  • Changes in shading by the hour and across seasons
  • Space required between panel rows
  • Safe access for cleaning and maintenance
  • Suitable panel orientation and tilt

In India, south-facing panels generally receive favorable solar exposure. However, east- or west-facing layouts may also be considered depending on the roof conditions. The installer should use the shading assessment and available roof area to create a site-specific panel layout instead of applying the same design to every home.

SolarSquare’s installation process, for example, includes a detailed site survey, software-based shadow assessment, and system layout design rather than relying only on how sunny the roof appears during a single visit.

Checklist to Follow Before Choosing a Solar Installer

A solar installer should be evaluated on project execution, equipment quality, engineering capability, service support, and the complete scope of work. Comparing quotations solely on the lowest price per kW can mask differences in components, structure, warranties, and after-sales service.

Here’s a simple checklist that you can follow to land a reputable installer –

1. Verify the Installer’s Eligibility and Track Record

For a subsidy-linked installation, confirm that the installer meets the applicable PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana and DISCOM requirements.

You should also assess the company’s experience with residential rooftop installations, service presence, and after-sales support.

Ask who will take responsibility if the rooftop solar system develops an inverter fault, generation problem, roof-related issue, or electrical problem after installation. Look for genuine Google reviews and pay close attention to how the company responds to past customers.

2. Compare the Complete Project Scope

Ask for a quotation that clearly identifies what is included in the proposed system.

It should provide clarity on items such as:

  • Solar system capacity
  • Panel technology and model
  • Inverter model and capacity
  • Mounting structure
  • DC and AC cables
  • Electrical protection
  • Earthing
  • Installation work
  • Monitoring
  • Metering and DISCOM-related scope
  • After-sales service

Two systems with the same stated kW capacity can differ substantially in equipment, engineering, and service quality.

3. Check Warranties and After-Sales Support

Solar panels are designed to operate for decades, while other system components may require service or replacement earlier. Check who supports each component after installation.

  • Review the panel’s product and performance warranties, inverter warranty, installation or workmanship coverage, and the maintenance support included with the project.
  • The warranty conditions should also be available in writing rather than only being explained verbally.

What Equipment Should Be on Your Solar Panel Installation Checklist?

A solar panel installation checklist should cover the entire system, not just the module. Panels, inverter, mounting structure, cables, connectors, earthing, and electrical protection all affect how safely and reliably the rooftop plant operates.

1. Solar Panels

Confirm the exact panel technology, wattage, manufacturer, model, and quantity specified in the quotation.

For a subsidy-linked residential system, the installer should also confirm that the modules satisfy the applicable DCR (Domestic Content Requirement) and ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) requirements.

When the equipment reaches your home, the supplied panel model and quantity should match what you approved.

2. Solar Inverter

The best solar inverter must be selected as part of the overall system design. Ask the installer to explain why the proposed capacity and configuration are suitable for your solar array.

Also check:

  • Inverter model
  • Warranty
  • Monitoring capability
  • Installation location
  • Manufacturer service support

The inverter should be installed in a suitable, protected, and ventilated location that remains accessible for monitoring and servicing.

3. Mounting Structure

The mounting structure holds the solar array on the roof and must be engineered for the building and local conditions. The installer should account for the roof type, structure material, corrosion protection, anchoring method, panel height, and wind loads applicable to the site.

4. Cables, Connectors and Electrical Protection

The installer should design the DC and AC electrical system using components suited to a solar installation.

Your quotation and final system should account for solar-grade cables and wires, suitable connectors, isolation and circuit protection, surge protection, earthing, and any required lightning protection.

Cables should also be properly secured and protected, rather than left loose or unnecessarily exposed.

Solar Installation Checklist for PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana Subsidy and Net Metering in 2026

If you plan to claim the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy, complete the applicable portal and DISCOM process before installation. The system must also meet the scheme’s equipment and vendor requirements. Installing first and checking subsidy eligibility later can put the subsidy claim at risk.

1. Complete the Required PM Surya Ghar Online Registration Process Before Installation

For subsidy-linked residential installations, register and apply through the official PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana portal and complete the applicable feasibility stage before proceeding with installation.

  • After installation, plant details are submitted, and the applicable net metering and DISCOM inspection process follows before commissioning.
  • The exact process can change. So, check the National Portal and your DISCOM requirements when you apply.

2. Check DCR and Current ALMM Requirements

For a subsidy-linked grid-tied PV system, ask your installer to confirm the applicable DCR (Domestic Content Requirement) and ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) requirements before ordering the panels.

  • ALMM List-I contains approved solar PV module models and manufacturers. MNRE (Ministry of New and Renewable Energy) states that modules covered by List-I are required for categories including government-assisted and net-metering projects. The current List-I was updated on August 3, 2026.
  • ALMM List-II covers solar PV cells. Its applicability has been subject to several updates and clarifications during 2026. So, the latest MNRE notification relevant to the project and installation date should be checked rather than relying on an older list.

3. Check Your DISCOM and Metering Requirements

Grid-connected rooftop solar must follow the process applicable to your local DISCOM.

  • Depending on the state and connection, this can include feasibility approval, submission of plant details, inspection, meter replacement, or configuration and final grid commissioning.
  • Your installer should explain which steps it handles and which actions, if any, require your participation.

What Documents Should You Keep Ready Before Solar Installation?

Keeping the required documents ready can prevent avoidable delays in the subsidy, net metering approval, and commissioning process. The exact list varies by DISCOM and scheme, but homeowners commonly need electricity connection, identity, banking, and property-related information.

Keep these documents or details accessible:

  • Latest electricity bill
  • Identity details like Aadhaar card or voter card
  • Bank details or cancelled cheque for subsidy transfer
  • Roof/property details
  • Approved quotation/agreement with the installer
  • Solar equipment details
  • DISCOM/portal documents

What Should You Check Before the Installation Team Starts Work?

Before work begins, ask the installer to walk you through the final approved design. Confirm that the panel layout, inverter location, cable route, mounting plan, and any required roof repairs match what was agreed during the site assessment. This final check helps prevent unapproved changes or on-site improvisation during installation.

What Should You Check During Solar Panel Installation?

During installation, the homeowner does not need to inspect the electrical engineering details personally. Instead, verify that the installer follows the agreed design, uses the approved equipment, and completes the mounting, waterproofing, wiring, protection, and earthing work before commissioning.

1. Mounting Structure and Roof Work

Confirm that the mounting structure being installed matches the agreed project specification.

  • Pay particular attention to visible roof damage and waterproofing around any penetrations.
  • The structure should also be installed cleanly without creating unnecessary obstructions across the terrace.

2. Solar Panel Installation

Verify that the delivered panel model and quantity match the quotation and that panels do not have obvious physical damage.

  • Panels should be lifted, positioned, and secured by trained installers using methods compatible with the manufacturer’s instructions.
  • Improper handling or installation can create cell-level damage and contribute to performance loss over time.

3. DC and AC Wiring

Cables should be properly routed, supported, and protected.

  • Look for obvious workmanship issues such as hanging cables, exposed connections, or untidy routing.
  • Detailed electrical connections should be inspected and tested by the installation team rather than by the homeowner.

SolarSquare’s installation process, for example, routes solar cabling through suitable protective conduits or cable management systems and uses purpose-built MC4 connectors on the DC side.

4. Earthing and Electrical Protection

Ask the installer to confirm that the required earthing and protection systems have been completed before the plant is energized.

  • Depending on the system design, this can include equipment earthing, AC and DC protection, surge protection, and lightning protection.
  • Proper electrical design and earthing also help reduce avoidable long-term performance problems.

5. Inverter Installation

Confirm that the installed inverter is the model specified in your quotation.

The installation team should complete the DC and AC connections, communication setup, and required electrical checks before the system is switched on.

What Should You Check Before Accepting the Completed Solar Installation?

Panel mounting does not mean the rooftop solar project is complete. Before final handover, the system should be inspected, tested, commissioned, and connected to monitoring.

You should also receive the documents and credentials needed to operate, monitor, and claim service for the system.

1. Inspect the Completed Installation

Do a visual walkthrough with the installer after the work is finished.

Check the panels, structure, roof, inverter location, electrical boxes, and visible cable routes. Ask about anything that appears damaged, incomplete, or different from the approved layout.

2. Confirm Testing and Commissioning

The installation team should complete the required electrical checks before making the plant operational.

  • The process includes checking DC string output and AC-side parameters before inverter start-up.
  • The inverter is then checked to confirm that the system is generating correctly.

You do not need to carry out these tests yourself. Ask the installer to confirm that commissioning has been completed and explain the system status.

3. Set Up Solar Monitoring

Before handover, make sure you can access the system’s monitoring platform.

  • You should be able to see current and historical generation and identify when the inverter reports an error or stops generating.
  • Monitoring is especially important because an electrical or inverter fault may not be obvious from simply looking at the panels.

4. Complete Metering and Grid Commissioning

For a rooftop on-grid solar system, confirm that the applicable DISCOM inspection, metering, and commissioning process has been completed.

  • The meter arrangement tracks electricity exchanged between your home and the grid in accordance with applicable state regulations.
  • Do not confuse completion of the physical installation with completion of the entire grid-connection process.

5. Collect the Final Handover Documents

Before closing the project, collect the records that may be required for future service, warranty, or regulatory purposes.

These can include:

  • Final invoice
  • Panel warranty documents
  • Inverter warranty
  • Installation or workmanship warranty
  • Equipment datasheets
  • Final system details
  • Commissioning documents
  • Monitoring login credentials
  • DISCOM and metering documents
  • Service contact details

Common Solar Installation Mistakes to Avoid

Most avoidable rooftop solar problems begin with poor planning, unclear quotations, or weak installation practices. A good solar installation checklist helps identify these issues before they affect solar generation, safety, or the roof itself.

Avoid these common mistakes:

  • Choosing system capacity only from available roof area
  • Ignoring future electricity requirements
  • Installing over a roof that requires repair
  • Relying on a visual shade check instead of professional analysis
  • Comparing installers only by pricing
  • Accepting a quotation without exact panel and inverter details
  • Ignoring mounting structure and electrical protection
  • Installing before completing applicable subsidy approvals
  • Treating panel mounting as final project completion
  • Finishing the project without monitoring access or handover documents

]]> Electricity bills rising by 3-6% annually and unpredictable power cuts have pushed many homeowners in India to consider solar energy adoption in 2026. But one key decision creates a lot of confusion: off-grid vs hybrid solar system, which one is actually better for your home?

On the surface, both types of solar systems look quite similar because both store solar energy in lead-acid or lithium batteries. However, the major difference between the two lies in how they interact with the electricity grid.

A hybrid solar system is connected to the grid and, at the same time, it has a battery bank that stores solar energy for later use during outages or periods of low solar generation, and at night. An off-grid solar system, on the other hand, is a completely standalone solar system. It is connected only to a battery bank, not the grid.

Since off-grid solar systems are not connected to the grid, they must be designed for full autonomy. Autonomy is preparing for a low-solar day when solar production is~12-15% of what happens on a sunny day. In such cases, the system must produce enough energy and store it in the battery the day prior, so it can power the home on a rainy day. If it powers the home through 1 day, it’s called 1 day of autonomy, and so on.

It’s achieved by:

  • Adding more solar panels than needed to meet the energy needs of a home.
  • Using a bigger battery to store solar energy that can power the house on its own for that many days of autonomy.

Since a hybrid solar system is connected to the grid, it does not require adding more panels than necessary. Only the battery should be sized to provide backup power for the entire duration of the power outage.

A hybrid solar system is therefore cheaper than an off-grid solar system and is most preferred in urban areas or rural/semi-rural areas where power outages are frequent and last for 2-3 hours. On the other hand, an off-grid solar system is the only practical option in remote and rural areas where grid supply is either unavailable or so poor that electricity comes for only 2-3 hours a day.

In this blog on off-grid vs hybrid solar systems, we will unpack many such differences between the two, including their cost, availability of the PM Surya Ghar solar subsidy, and which of the two is better and under what scenarios.

What is an Off-Grid Solar System, and What are its Benefits for Homeowners?

An off-grid solar system is a standalone PV system that converts solar radiation into DC power, which is used to power the home and solar batteries during the day. Stored solar power is used at night or when solar generation is completely absent.

Here’s how it works:

  • During the day, solar cells in solar panels absorb photons present in the sunlight.
  • The photons transfer their energy to electrons present in the solar cells, exciting them.
  • Excited electrons begin to flow in a single direction. This unidirectional flow of current is direct current (DC) electricity.
  • This DC power is sent to a solar inverter, which converts it to alternating current (AC) to run household appliances.
  • Excess DC energy generated during the day is used to charge the lithium battery bank.
  • The battery’s stored power is used at night or during periods of low or no solar generation.

Some of the biggest benefits of off-grid solar systems are as follows:

  • They are the only power source in remote locations with no grid power.
  • They’re the only practical choice in rural areas with a highly unreliable grid that gives electricity supply for just 2-3 hours a day.
  • They offer complete energy independence with no reliance on the grid.
  • You don’t have to worry about electricity bills, since all power comes from solar.

What are the Disadvantages of an Off-Grid Solar System?

Off-grid solar systems have to be designed with full autonomy in mind. It makes them quite expensive. And their high upfront cost is their biggest disadvantage.

Here’s a look at all the disadvantages in detail:

  • The government does not offer the PM Surya Ghar subsidy for installing off-grid systems.
  • They’re costlier than hybrid solar systems. The higher upfront cost is a result of large battery storage and additional panels.
  • Once the grid supply becomes available, the system becomes much larger than required.

What is a Hybrid Solar System, and What are its Benefits for Homeowners?

A hybrid solar system is a grid-connected PV system that also includes battery storage. It allows your home to use solar power during the day, store excess energy in batteries, use stored power during outages, and draw power from the grid when needed.

Here’s a step-by-step breakdown of how a hybrid solar system works:

  • Solar panels convert sunlight into direct current (DC) power.
  • A hybrid solar inverter converts DC power to AC power, keeping your household appliances running.
  • Excess DC power is stored in the solar rechargeable batteries and used at night or during power cuts.
  • If there’s any additional power left after the batteries are charged, it is sent to the grid.

Here are some of the biggest benefits of using a hybrid solar system for homes:

  • It offers a battery backup that ensures an uninterrupted power supply even during outages.
  • If the DISCOM permits net metering for hybrid systems in your city, you will be eligible for the PM Surya Ghar solar subsidy, which reduces the overall system cost.
  • Since hybrid systems prioritize solar energy, they reduce electricity bills by reducing reliance on grid power.
  • The grid support acts as a safety net, eliminating the need for full autonomy, as is the case with off-grid systems.

What are the Disadvantages of a Hybrid Solar System?

Despite benefits like uninterrupted power supply during outages and the availability of a solar subsidy if net metering is allowed, the biggest limitation of a hybrid solar system is that it costs more than an on-grid solar system.

Having said that, their cost is extremely justified in areas where power cuts are frequent. If you use an on-grid solar system in such areas, you will either be left in darkness during power outages or rely on UPS-based systems that increase electricity bills by drawing power from the grid to charge themselves.

Therefore, a hybrid solar system is the best option for urban areas where power outages happen almost daily for 2-3 hours.

How Do the Best Solar Panels in India Work?

Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity through the photovoltaic effect. Photons are energy particles present in sunlight. When sunlight falls on solar cells (building blocks of solar panels), photons transfer their energy to electrons within the cells.

The energized electrons leave their positions and begin to flow in a single direction, producing direct current (DC) electricity.

How to Install Solar Connectors? Step-by-Step Guide on Connecting MC4 Solar Connectors

It is highly recommended not to try to install MC4 connectors and solar systems on your own. DC electricity can arc, burn, and cause serious injuries. How to Connect MC4 Solar Connectors in Simple Steps

Professionals follow proper MC4 connector installation steps and use specific tools and techniques to ensure reliable and weatherproof connections that will last decades. 

Let’s check out all the steps professionals follow to connect MC4 solar connectors:

  • Step 1 – Power is made safe: The inverter and DC isolators are turned off before any work begins. Panels are shaded or covered to reduce live voltage. Personal protective equipment is worn, and live connections are never made or broken under load.
  • Step 2 – The cable run is planned: Cable lengths, routing, and polarity are confirmed on paper before the cable is cut. 
  • Step 3 – The cable is cut cleanly: The cable is cut with a proper cutter, ensuring the end is square and the strands remain undamaged. Damaged or flattened ends are trimmed.
  • Step 4 – Insulation is stripped to meet specifications: Only the length specified by the connector manufacturer is stripped. Copper strands are not nicked. If strands are nicked accidentally, the end is cut off, and the strip is repeated.
  • Step 5 – The contact is crimped correctly: The stripped conductor is fully inserted into the MC4 contact, which is a pin for the male side and a socket for the female side. 
  • Step 6 – A pull test is performed: A gentle pull is applied to confirm the conductor does not slip from the crimped contact. Any movement indicates a failed crimp, and the step is repeated with a new contact.
  • Step 7 – The contact is inserted into the housing: The crimped contact is pushed into the matching housing until an audible click sound. The pins are inserted into male housings, and the sockets are inserted into female housings. 
  • Step 8 – The seal is checked and the gland is tightened: The O-ring is checked to ensure it is seated and not twisted. The gland nut is tightened with MC4 spanners. This creates the seal and strain relief.
  • Step 9 – The matching connector is prepared: The same process is followed for the mating cable end, ensuring a complete, matched pair is ready.
  • Step 10 – Connectors are mated and locked: Keys are aligned, and the two halves are pushed straight until they click. If locking clips are provided, they are fitted.
  • Step 11 – Electrical checks are done: A multimeter is used to confirm polarity. Open-circuit voltage and short-circuit current are checked against the solar panel’s specifications. Any mismatch is investigated before energizing.
  • Step 12 – Cables are secured: Cables are secured with UV-rated clips or ties, kept away from sharp edges, and routed to prevent pooling water. 

Why Did MC4 Connectors Replace MC3 Connectors?

MC3 connectors were popular in the 90s, but they were completely discontinued in 2016. The reason these first-generation Multi-Contact solar connectors were discounted is that, as solar became more popular, high-efficiency solar panels started producing output much higher than the MC3 solar connectors could safely handle. Under load, MC3 connectors began to open on their own, posing a safety issue. 

To address safety concerns, Multi-Contact AG introduced the MC4 solar connectors, which are touch-safe. They completely replaced MC3 connectors because they had enhanced safety features with positive locking mechanisms, superior weatherproofing with IP67/IP68 ratings, increased current capacity from 20A to 50A, and improved long-term reliability. 

Here are all the major reasons that made MC4 connectors replace MC3 connectors and become standard in rooftop solar system installation all across the world:

  • They have a safer locking mechanism: MC3 connectors use a simple snap-fit that can come apart if the cable is pulled or if parts expand and contract due to heat. MC4 solar connectors, on the other hand, use a positive lock that clicks into place and requires a small tool to release. This prevents accidental unplugging.
  • They’re sealed to withstand harsh outdoor weather conditions: MC3 connectors have basic weather resistance. As a result, water, dust, and rust affect the cables and components. MC4 connectors are designed for IP67 or IP68 protection when assembled correctly. It means dust is kept out and the joint can handle temporary water immersion, which greatly reduces water-related failures.
  • They have a higher current handling capacity, suitable for high-efficiency panels: The 3 mm pin in MC3 connectors can handle a current of 20 A. On the contrary, an MC4 connector uses a 4 mm contact that is commonly rated at around 30 A, with higher variants available to withstand up to 50 A. This supports higher-wattage solar panels and helps cut heat at the connection point.
  • They use multiple contact points for reliability: MC3 relied more on a single contact area that could degrade with heat cycles and oxidation. MC4 uses a multi-contact design that distributes pressure across multiple points. As a result, the connection remains stable for many years with a lower risk of hot spots.
  • They have a touch-safe design for people working on systems: With MC3, live metal could be exposed during mating and unmating. MC4 connectors, on the other hand, keep the live parts inside the housing. This ensures that fingers do not come into contact with energized metal during normal handling.
  • They use stronger plastics that last on the roof: MC3 housings could turn brittle after years in strong sunlight. MC4 housings are made from UV-stabilized engineering plastic, which holds up better in high temperatures and direct sunlight.
  • They offer higher voltage options for larger arrays: MC3 was generally limited to systems with a voltage of up to about 600 V DC. However, modern MC4 solar connectors are available with ratings up to 1,000 V and 1,500 V, which allows longer strings.

What is Solar Equipment?

Solar equipment is the set of multiple components that work together in a solar photovoltaic system to turn sunlight into usable electricity (AC) through the photovoltaic effect. 

The main solar power equipment components include: 

  • Solar panels: They are made up of solar photovoltaic cells that absorb sunlight and convert it to DC power.

  • Bi-directional net meter: This meter is used in grid-tied systems to record the electricity units exchanged between the grid and the solar system.
  • A lithium battery bank: A battery is a solar PV equipment used in off-grid or hybrid solar systems. The stored battery power is then used at night or whenever needed.

#1. Solar Panels: The Most Visible Solar System Equipment

Solar panels, also known as solar modules, are the components that receive sunlight and use the photovoltaic effect to turn it into DC electricity. Out of the various types, the best solar panels that are used in most rooftop solar systems for housing societies, homes, and commercial complexes are monocrystalline bifacial solar panels with PERC half-cut cells.

The two main kinds of solar panels include:

  • Monocrystalline solar panels: The latest technology monocrystalline solar panels are made of half-cut mono-PERC cells. Their efficiency shoots up to 22.5%. How Does Solar Panel Equipment Work?

Solar panels work using the photovoltaic principle that converts solar energy into direct current. When photons present in sunlight fall on the solar cells present inside the panels, they knock electrons loose. The flow of electrons is known as direct current. 

Let’s understand how this particular solar equipment works to convert sunlight into direct current:

  • Step 1 – Solar panels absorb photons: Solar cells in solar panels are made of silicon, which is a semiconductor. When sunlight falls on them, the photons present in sunlight transfer their energy to the electrons inside the silicon.
  • Step 2 – Energized electrons begin to move: When electrons absorb the energy of photons, they dislodge from their original position and start to move. The space they leave behind is called a hole, which acts like a positive charge.
  • Step 3 – The solar cell’s built-in electric field pushes the electron-hole pair in opposite directions: Every solar cell has a built-in electric field created by joining two types of silicon (p-type and n-type). This field pushes electrons and holes in opposite directions, so they don’t recombine immediately.
  • Step 4 – Electrons flow in one direction: The electrons are collected by thin metal lines on the front of the cell and move towards the back contact layer. This creates a unidirectional flow of electrons, known as direct current (DC).

#2. Solar Inverter: The Solar Energy Equipment For DC to AC Conversion

An inverter is a crucial piece of solar energy equipment, as it converts the DC output from the panels into alternating current. Without a solar inverter, a solar system is not a functional model for homes because households receive only AC power, as, unlike DC, alternating current is safer.

The three main types of solar inverters are string inverters, microinverters, and power optimizers. 

  • String inverters: These are the most common type of inverter used in rooftop solar systems. All the solar panels in a string send their DC power to one central string inverter, which converts it into AC. They are the most affordable solar inverter type; however, if one panel underperforms, the whole string is affected.
  • Microinverters: These are small inverters attached to each solar panel individually. Since each panel operates independently, shading or dust on one panel does not impact the performance of the others. While microinverters are more efficient, they can be up to 90% more expensive than string inverters.
  • Power optimizers: These devices are fitted to each panel, but they still send power to a central string inverter. They help each panel work at its best by adjusting voltage and current, which reduces losses from shading or mismatch. However, they’re still costly and complicated to install. 

Out of these types, string solar inverters with MPPT technology are most commonly used in residential and commercial rooftop systems, as they’re up to 90% cheaper than microinverters and are highly efficient when nearby objects do not shade the roof during peak sunshine hours.

How Do Solar Inverters Work?

Solar inverters work by converting the DC input they receive from solar panels into AC. 

Let’s check out the step-by-step working process:

  • Step 1 – DC power enters the inverter: Solar panels send direct current (DC) to the inverter through strings. A DC isolator and internal checks make sure the input is safe before conversion begins.
  • Step 2 – MPPT finds the best operating point: The inverter measures the panel voltage and current, and adjusts them to ensure the panels operate at their maximum power point (MPPT). This maintains high output even when sunlight or temperature changes occur. 
  • Step 3 – DC is converted into smooth AC: Inside the inverter, the incoming DC power is first stabilized, then shaped and filtered to produce smooth AC power, the same type used by home appliances.
  • Step 4 – Output is synchronized: For on-grid solar systems, the inverter continuously measures the grid’s voltage and frequency, and sends power into the grid when the panels produce excess current. If the grid goes down, the output stops immediately. For hybrid or off-grid systems, the inverter also manages battery charging and discharging.
  • Step 5 – Protection and monitoring run all the time: The inverter monitors over-voltage, over-current, DC leakage, ground faults, and high temperatures, and shuts down if necessary.

#4. Bi-directional Net Meter: The Solar Electric Equipment Mandatory for On-Grid Solar Systems

A bi-directional net meter is one of the most important solar equipment in grid-connected solar systems, as it records the exchange of electricity between the solar system and the utility grid. 

On-grid solar systems do not have lithium batteries to store solar energy. Instead, any excess energy produced by the panels that isn’t consumed in real-time is sent to the utility grid, and the net energy meter/smart meter keeps a record of the exported units. Similarly, at night or when the panels produce fewer units than needed, the required electricity units are imported from the grid. The net meter also records the import. 

The process of unit exchange, as recorded by the net meter, is known as the solar power net metering process. Based on the exchange, you either get credits from your DISCOM or a solar bill. 

  • If your system exports more units than it imports: Since you’ve supplied more power, you receive credits from the DISCOM, which are either settled at the end of the month or the financial year, depending on your DISCOM’s rules.
  • If your system imports more units than it exports: In this case, your home has consumed grid electricity. The net meter has a record of the extra units consumed, and you pay a solar bill only for those extra units.

#5. Solar Accessories: Solar Equipment Necessary to Connect Other Components

Solar accessories are the small components that are used to connect other components of a solar system together. The most important solar accessories include MC4 connectors, AC and DC cables, AC and DC combiner boxes, conduit trays, and a lightning arrester.

Let’s understand the exact role of all solar accessories in detail:

  • MC4 solar connectors: These are industry-standard waterproof solar connectors that create secure, weatherproof connections between solar panels and DC wiring. These locking connectors use a positive-engagement mechanism that prevents accidental disconnection. They’re critical for preventing corrosion in outdoor environments exposed to rain, snow, and UV radiation.
  • DC cables: These are specialized photovoltaic wires built to carry direct current safely. They use insulation designed to withstand temperature extremes, UV radiation, and moisture. Proper gauge selection minimizes voltage losses while safely carrying panel output current to inverters or charge controllers.
  • AC cables: These are standard electrical wires that carry alternating current from inverters to electrical panels and the grid. 
  • DC combiner box: It’s a weatherproof box that gathers the DC power from several panel strings and sends it to the inverter through one or a few thicker DC cables. Most DC combiner boxes also include surge protection and a DC on-off switch
  • AC combiner box: It’s a weatherproof box located on the AC side that combines the output from one or more inverters and sends it to the building’s main panel or the grid. 
  • Electrical conduit and fittings: These are protective tubes and connectors that safely carry solar wires. They shield cables from water, dust, and sunlight.
  • Lightning rod: This is a metal rod fixed at the highest point of the roof. It safely conducts a direct lightning strike to the ground through thick metal strips and an earth pit. It protects the roof and panels from a direct hit, but it does not stop the voltage surge that can travel inside the wires. A lighting arrester, another important solar equipment, is needed for that.
  • Lightning arresters/surge protection devices (SPDs): These are small devices installed near the inverter on both the DC and AC sides of the system. They divert sudden high voltage from lightning into the earth before it can damage the inverter and other electronics. They only work well with proper earthing and short, direct ground wires. 

#6. A Lithium Battery Bank: The Solar Power Equipment for Off-Grid and Hybrid Solar Systems

A lithium battery bank is a type of solar power equipment used only in off-grid and hybrid solar systems, rather than on-grid systems. Lithium batteries are expensive and require periodic replacement. That’s the reason why off-grid and hybrid solar systems are so costly. Since on-grid solar systems use the grid to store excess energy, they do not require batteries. 

Let’s check out how lithium batteries are charged in off-grid and hybrid solar systems: 

  • Excess solar energy is directed into the solar charge controller: This charge controller is either built into the inverter or used as a separate device. It ensures that the DC being sent to the batteries is in a safe range. Once batteries receive the controller-directed DC during the daytime, they store solar energy. 
  • Stored energy is used at night or during a power outage: The inverter converts the battery’s DC power into AC, ensuring the essential load remains operational at night or whenever needed.

How Does Solar Equipment Work to Generate Electricity? Step-by-Step Process

All components of the solar PV equipment work simultaneously to ensure that sunlight is converted into usable electricity, which can be sent to the appliances in a home as well as the grid. 

Let’s check out the complete process of how solar equipment components come together to work as one single solar system in simple steps:

  • Step 1 – Solar panels capture sunlight: Solar cells in the panels convert the captured sunlight into direct current (DC). 
  • Step 2 – DC cables carry DC power from the panel into the DC combiner box: MC4 connectors and DC cables, located inside the conduit, take DC power from each string to a DC combiner box, where strings are grouped.
  • Step 3 – DC from the combiner box is sent to the solar inverter: The string solar inverter converts direct current into alternating current. This AC solar electricity is sent to the main distribution board, where it is distributed to the connected appliances. If microinverters are attached to each panel individually, their outputs are combined in an AC combiner box and then sent to the main distribution board.
  • Step 4 – Excess energy, if any, is sent to the grid or stored in batteries: In an on-grid solar system, the bidirectional net meter records the extra energy that flows directly to the grid. In off-grid systems, the extra energy is used to charge the solar batteries. In hybrid systems, if the DISCOM allows net metering, any remaining current after the batteries are fully charged is sent to the grid.
  • Step 5 – Protection and earthing run all the time: DC and AC isolators, breakers, and surge protection devices keep the system safe. Proper earthing and a rooftop lightning rod protect against electrical faults and lightning strikes.

What Are the Types of a 4 kW Solar System? Cost Comparison Included

On-grid, off-grid, and hybrid solar systems are the three primary kinds of solar systems used in India. On-grid solar systems are more economical than off-grid and hybrid solar systems as they do not require lithium batteries to store excess solar energy. Before we understand the exact cost difference that makes on-grid the most preferred choice for homes in India, let’s learn how these systems work:

  • On-grid solar systems: They are connected to the utility grid. When the solar panels produce excess solar energy, a bi-directional net meter sends extra current to the grid for storage. At night, energy is imported from the grid to keep the house powered. This unit exchange is recorded by a net meter, and the process is called solar net metering.
  • Off-grid solar systems: They are not connected to the grid like on-grid PV systems. Instead, they use a lithium battery bank to store excess solar energy generated by the solar panels. Since lithium batteries are expensive and require replacement, the off-grid systems are way more expensive. Plus, the government does not provide homeowners with a subsidy for installing off-grid solar systems.
  • Hybrid solar systems: They are connected to the grid as well as the lithium battery bank. Excess solar energy first charges the battery; if anything remains after that, the net meter sends it to the grid. These are the most expensive of the three types.

What Are the Types of 5 kW Solar Systems?

The three main kinds of 5 kW solar systems are on-grid PV systems, off-grid PV systems, and hybrid PV systems. On-grid solar systems are connected to the grid. Off-grid solar systems are connected to a lithium battery bank, not the grid. Hybrid solar systems are connected to both the grid and the lithium battery bank. 

  • On-grid solar panel systems: The grid serves the function of batteries in on-grid systems. Excess solar electricity generated by the panels is sent to the grid through a bidirectional net meter. At night, the same meter imports electricity from the grid to keep the household running. The entire process of electricity exchange is called the solar net metering process. 
  • Off-grid solar panel systems: Excess energy generated by the panels during the day is used to charge the batteries through a built-in solar panel charge controller in the solar inverter. 
  • Hybrid solar panel systems: These are connected to both the grid and the lithium battery bank. Excess energy is first used to charge the lithium batteries. If any extra current still remains, it is sent to the grid through the net meter.

What is a Solar-Powered Air Conditioner?

How Does a Solar AC Unit Work?

Solar panel air conditioners operate through a straightforward energy conversion process, but their operation depends on whether they include battery storage or not.

  • Battery-free solar-powered air conditioners: These are grid-tied solar AC units where solar panels capture sunlight and convert it into DC electricity through photovoltaic cells during the daytime.   • Subsidy for housing societies is capped at Rs. 90 lakh.

This subsidy, however, is not offered for installing off-grid solar systems, ground-mounted solar systems, or solar systems for commercial purposes. 

Conclusion 

Using solar panels for AC can offer substantial savings on electricity bills as solar panel systems produce free solar electricity for at least 25 years, which can be used to keep air conditioners and other home appliances running. There are many solar-powered air conditioners available in the market, but instead of investing a few lakhs in buying them, you can invest that same money into installing an on-grid solar system that can run all household appliances, including conventional air conditioners.

Buying a solar AC unit instead of installing a rooftop solar system makes sense if net metering is not allowed by your discom. Otherwise, using a solar panel for AC in rooftop solar offers the best return on investment.
