]]> A solar investment plan (SIP) lets homeowners install rooftop solar through manageable monthly payments instead of paying the full system cost from personal savings at once. The system then reduces the electricity you buy from the Distribution Company (DISCOM), while the government subsidy lowers your effective investment.

For example, the 3 kW system may cost ~₹2.50 lakh before subsidy. Eligible residential consumers can receive up to ₹78,000 under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, reducing the effective investment in this case to ~₹1.72 lakh. The estimated payback period in this case can be ~4.5 years. If financed, the monthly installment depends on the loan amount, interest rate, and repayment tenure.

That is what makes a solar SIP different from simply paying an electricity bill. Instead of repeatedly paying for grid electricity, you’re paying toward a rooftop solar system that can continue generating solar electricity even after the financing period ends.

This guide explains how a solar investment plan works, where its returns come from, how return on investment (ROI) is calculated, and what risks homeowners should consider before choosing it.

What is a Solar Investment Plan?

A solar investment plan is a financing arrangement that lets a homeowner install rooftop solar without paying the full cost from personal savings at once. The homeowner repays the financed amount through monthly installments while the system helps reduce electricity purchases from the grid.

Why is It Called an SIP?

SIP usually stands for Systematic Investment Plan, a method commonly associated with mutual funds. In this case, it describes a structured way of investing in solar energy systems through regular payments.

  • A conventional SIP requires you to invest fresh money periodically to build a financial corpus.
  • A solar investment plan redirects part of an existing household expense towards a physical asset. The system generates electricity, and the resulting bill savings act as its financial return.

However, a solar SIP is not a mutual fund, deposit, or market-linked financial product. It is a rooftop solar ownership and financing plan.

What Makes Solar an Investment Rather Than an Expense?

A normal electricity bill pays for power already consumed. A solar investment, on the other hand, creates a physical asset that can generate free electricity from solar radiation for many years. Its value comes from lower electricity bills and, where applicable, credits for surplus electricity exported to the grid (in case of on-grid or hybrid solar systems).

However, a rooftop solar system generates returns differently from a traditional deposit. As this comparison of solar vs. fixed deposit returns explains, a Fixed Deposit (FD) earns interest on the money deposited. In contrast, rooftop solar creates value by reducing a recurring household expense.

Since electricity tariffs in India rise by 3-6% annually, solar is more of an investment instead of an expense. With proper maintenance and regular servicing, it lasts 25 years or more.

How Does a Solar Investment Plan Work?

A solar investment plan combines roof assessment, system sizing, financing, the government subsidy, and electricity savings. The system is installed first, and the homeowner uses the electricity it generates while repaying the financed amount through monthly installments.

The process generally works as follows:

  • The homeowner’s electricity consumption and roof are assessed.
  • The appropriate rooftop solar capacity is selected.
  • The system cost and eligible PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy are calculated.
  • The customer pays the full system cost, with the required amount financed through monthly installments.
  • The rooftop solar system is installed and connected through net metering.
  • The approved subsidy is later credited to the customer’s bank account.
  • Solar generation reduces the household’s grid electricity purchases.
  • The household uses these monthly savings to offset the solar payment.
  • After the repayment period, the system continues generating electricity and monthly savings.

How Can You Install Solar Without a Large Upfront Payment?

Solar financing divides the system cost into manageable installments, reducing the need to use a large amount of personal savings at once. The government subsidy further reduces the homeowner’s effective investment once it is credited after installation and approval.

How the Government Subsidy Reduces Your Initial Cost

Eligible homeowners can receive a subsidy under the applicable government rooftop solar scheme. The maximum subsidy under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is capped at ₹78,000 for solar systems of 3 kW or higher capacity.

However, as already explained, the central subsidy should not be treated as an advance discount. It remains subject to the customer, system, equipment, installer, and documentation meeting the applicable requirements.

How Solar Financing Covers the Remaining Cost

Financing helps the customer pay the system price without using the entire amount from personal savings. The financed amount is repaid through monthly installments over the agreed tenure.

Before accepting a financing offer, the customer should review:

  • Loan amount
  • Interest rate
  • Monthly installment
  • Repayment tenure
  • Processing charges
  • Credit eligibility
  • Prepayment conditions
  • Penalties for delayed payments
  • Treatment of the subsidy after it is received

Once the system starts operating, its financial value appears in several ways.

Where Do the Returns from a Solar Investment Plan Come From?

The main return from a solar power investment comes from reducing the amount of electricity purchased from the DISCOM (distribution company). Additional value may come from net metering credits, protection against future tariff increases, and savings after the solar loan is repaid.

The returns can come from the following sources:

  • Savings from using solar electricity at home: Every solar unit consumed at home replaces a unit that the household would otherwise purchase from the grid. A properly sized rooftop solar system can lower your electricity bills by up to 90% or more.
  • Credits for surplus solar electricity: When the system generates more electricity than the home consumes, surplus units may be exported to the grid.
  • Protection against future electricity tariff increases: Grid tariffs rise by 3-6% annually. Since solar now caters to all your home energy needs, you’re automatically shielded from tariff hikes.
  • Savings after the monthly payments end: Once you repay the loan, the rooftop photovoltaic (PV) system continues generating electricity without the monthly solar installment.

The actual value of these returns depends on solar generation, household consumption, electricity tariffs, net metering rules, maintenance expenses, system performance, and financing terms.

How is the ROI of a Solar Investment Plan Calculated?

Return on Investment (ROI on solar panels) compares the financial value you receive from rooftop solar with the amount you invest. An accurate solar ROI calculator (also known as a solar return on investment calculator) should account for the subsidy, electricity bill savings, financing expenses, maintenance, and other applicable costs.

The basic formula is:

Solar ROI (%) = [(Annual electricity savings − Annual operation and maintenance cost) ÷ Net solar investment] × 100

Here:

  • Annual electricity savings means the amount saved on electricity bills in one year.
  • Annual operation and maintenance cost includes the applicable yearly expenses for maintaining the system.
  • Net solar investment is the system cost remaining after deducting the government subsidy.

What is the Solar Payback Period?

The solar payback period shows approximately how long the system takes to recover its net cost through electricity bill savings. A shorter payback period means the homeowner begins receiving post-recovery savings sooner.

The formula is:

Solar Payback Period = Net solar system cost ÷ Annual net electricity savings

In the 3 kW example, the net investment is ₹1,72,000, and the annual savings are ₹36,000. The estimated payback period is 4.5 years. After this period, the continuing electricity bill savings contribute to the homeowner’s net financial benefit.

What is Included in Annual Net Savings?

Annual net savings represent the financial value the system produces after deducting applicable recurring solar expenses. This provides a more realistic result than treating the value of all generated electricity as a return.

The formula is:

Annual net electricity savings = Value of solar electricity used at home + Value of exported electricity − Annual maintenance and recurring charges
  • The value of solar electricity used at home depends on the applicable grid tariff.
  • The value of exported electricity depends on the state’s net metering or settlement rules.

Why ROI on Solar Panels is Different for Every Home

Two homes with systems of the same capacity may receive different returns because their roofs, electricity consumption, tariffs, financing terms, and solar generation can differ.

The main factors affecting ROI include:

  • System size
  • Roof orientation and available sunlight
  • Shading
  • Local electricity tariff
  • Daytime electricity use
  • Net metering rules
  • Subsidy eligibility
  • Loan interest and tenure
  • Equipment performance
  • Maintenance and system downtime

Why Can a Solar Investment Plan Deliver a High ROI?

A solar investment plan can offer an attractive ROI because the government subsidy reduces the effective investment while the system produces electricity for many years.

Several features support the potential return:

  • Government subsidy reduces the cost base: With government subsidy available, less capital is tied up in the solar system. A lower net investment lets the same electricity savings deliver a higher percentage return.
  • Solar replaces expensive grid electricity: Solar electricity consumed at home reduces the number of units purchased from the DISCOM.
  • Solar panels have a long operating life: Modern high-efficiency TOPCon panels keep generating electricity after the original investment is recovered.
  • Operating costs are relatively low: Rooftop solar generally requires cleaning, monitoring, periodic inspections, and occasional repairs.

Why Is the Risk in Solar Investment Plan (SIP) Lower Than Many Market-Linked Investments?

A solar SIP has limited exposure to daily financial-market movements because its return comes mainly from electricity generation and bill savings. However, lower exposure to market volatility does not make it risk-free. Its performance still depends on technical, financial, and regulatory conditions.

Its risk is lower in some respects for the following reasons:

  • Returns do not depend on stock-market prices: Electricity bill savings do not rise or fall with daily equity-market movements.
  • Electricity is an existing household expense: Homeowners already need electricity, so solar replaces part of a recurring monthly household expense.
  • Generation can be estimated before installation: Roof conditions, local sunlight, system capacity, and previous electricity use can help estimate potential output of rooftop solar systems.
  • Warranties reduce some equipment risks: Applicable panel, inverter, structure, and workmanship warranties may cover specified faults or performance issues.

These factors can make potential savings more predictable than market-linked returns, but they cannot guarantee a fixed result. What Risks Should You Check Before Choosing the SIP (Solar Investment Plan)?

The plan’s results can be affected by solar generation, financing, subsidy approval, roof conditions, equipment quality, and changes in electricity regulations. Homeowners should evaluate these factors before comparing projected electricity savings with the monthly loan payment.

The main risks include:

  • Lower-than-expected electricity generation due to shade, dust, poor design, equipment faults, or downtime
  • Electricity savings that are lower than the monthly loan installment
  • Subsidy rejection or delay if the applicable requirements are not met
  • Loan interest, repayment obligations, and late-payment penalties
  • Poor-quality equipment, unsafe installation, or inadequate after-sales service

Who Can Benefit Most from a Solar Investment Plan?

An solar investment plan (SIP) is highly suitable for:

  • Homeowners with an unshaded and structurally suitable roof
  • Households with consistently high electricity bills
  • Consumers paying higher electricity tariffs
  • Families expecting to remain in the property for several years
  • Homes with high daytime electricity consumption
  • Residential consumers eligible for the subsidy
  • Customers who qualify for the required financing

When May a Solar Investment Plan Not Be Suitable?

A solar SIP plan may be less suitable in the following situations:

  • Very low or heavily subsidized electricity bills
  • A shaded or structurally unsuitable roof
  • A rented property without the owner’s approval
  • Plans to demolish, or reconstruct the property soon
  • Inability to meet monthly loan payments

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a solar investment plan the same as a mutual-fund SIP?

No. A solar investment plan finances a physical rooftop solar system, while a mutual-fund SIP purchases units in a market-linked fund. Solar returns mainly come through electricity bill savings, whereas mutual-fund returns depend on the changing value of the underlying investments.

Does a solar investment plan require any down payment?

It depends on the system cost, approved loan amount, customer eligibility, and lender’s terms. The customer repays the financed portion through monthly installments and pays only the required upfront contribution, if any. The approved subsidy is credited to the customer’s account after commissioning.

Is the solar investment plan really a zero-investment plan?

It may reduce or remove the need for a large upfront payment under an eligible financing arrangement, but the system is not free. The customer must repay the financed amount, interest, and applicable charges, even when electricity savings help offset the monthly installment.

Can electricity savings completely cover the EMI?

Electricity savings may cover or exceed the EMIs in suitable cases, but this is not guaranteed. The result depends on solar generation, electricity tariffs, daytime consumption, system size, interest rate, repayment tenure, and net-metering rules.

Is the return from solar guaranteed?

In most cases, no. Solar returns depend on actual electricity generation, household consumption, grid tariffs, system performance, maintenance, downtime, financing costs, and applicable policies.

What happens after the solar loan is repaid?

Once the solar loan is fully repaid, the lender cancels the hypothecation on the solar asset. The solar system then remains with the homeowner and can continue generating electricity bill savings, subject to its performance, maintenance, and operating condition.

Can tenants apply for a solar investment plan?

A tenant would generally require the property owner’s approval because the system is permanently installed on the roof. Financing eligibility, electricity connection details, roof rights, and responsibility for future repayments must also be resolved before installation.

Is solar investment income taxable?

Residential electricity bill savings are generally a reduction in household expenses rather than a regular investment payout. Savings from solar are not taxable.

Are Rooftop Solar and Financial Investments Directly Comparable? 

No. Rooftop solar and financial investments are not directly comparable because they create value in different ways. Solar reduces a recurring household expense through electricity savings, while fixed deposits, gold, and mutual funds create financial assets that can generally be redeemed or sold.

They can still be compared on a common basis by looking at how much financial value the same investment creates over the same period, while accounting for the different forms of returns, costs, taxes, liquidity, and residual value.

  • Rooftop solar creates value through electricity savings: It reduces the amount of grid electricity a household needs to purchase and may also earn credits for surplus electricity exported to the grid.   • Each option requires a suitable return metric: Solar can be assessed using ROI (return on investment), payback period, and IRR (Internal Rate of Return), which is the annualized rate of return that accounts for the timing of cash flows over the investment period. Gold and mutual funds are commonly compared using CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) and their net value after costs and taxes.

Therefore, the comparison between solar vs gold vs FD vs mutual funds should examine the total value created after costs and taxes rather than placing solar’s annual bill savings directly against the advertised return percentage of a financial product.

How Do Solar, FDs, Gold, and Mutual Funds Generate Returns? 

These four options generate value differently.

  • Solar reduces electricity expenses by up to 90% or more
  • Fixed deposits pay interest
  • Gold benefits from price appreciation
  • Mutual funds gain or lose value according to the performance of their underlying investments.

Let’s understand each of the four in detail.

Returns From Rooftop Solar 

Rooftop solar generates returns through electricity bill savings. The photovoltaic (PV) system reduces grid purchases, while surplus generation may earn credits under applicable DISCOM regulations.

Here’s a simple formula that can be used to calculate returns from rooftop solar:

Annual solar savings = Value of solar electricity consumed at home + value of exported electricity − annual maintenance service expenses

The payback period shows how long savings take to recover the installation cost. Long-term solar panel investment return (ROI) also considers maintenance, panel degradation, and component replacement.

Returns from Fixed Deposits 

A fixed deposit generates returns through bank interest. The maturity amount depends on the initial deposit, interest rate, tenure, and compounding frequency.

Here’s a simple formula that can be used to calculate returns from FDs:

FD maturity value = Initial deposit + accumulated interest

The advertised rate is not the final return. Interest is generally taxable at the applicable slab, while inflation can reduce the maturity amount’s purchasing power.

Returns From Gold 

Gold produces a return when its selling price exceeds its total purchase cost. It does not ordinarily generate regular interest or monthly income.

Here’s a simple formula that can be used to calculate returns from gold:

Net gold return = Sale value − purchase cost − transaction, holding, and tax costs

Physical gold may involve purchase, storage, and selling costs, while market-linked gold products may have other charges.

Returns from Mutual Funds

Mutual funds produce returns when the value of their underlying investments rises. Their performance is market-linked, which means the value can also fall.

Here’s a simple formula that can be used to calculate returns from mutual funds:

Net mutual fund return = Redemption value − investment amount − expenses and applicable tax

The outcome depends on the fund category, period, market performance, expenses, and tax. Historical returns do not guarantee future performance.

Solar and gold serve different purposes.

  • Gold is a transferable financial asset whose return depends on future market prices.

Gold is easier to sell, requires no suitable roof, and supports diversification. However, it produces no electricity savings or interest, and its value can fall.

A solar vs mutual fund comparison involves two very different assets.

  • Solar creates household savings through free solar electricity generation.
  • A mutual fund creates a liquid, market-linked financial corpus that you can partially or fully redeem.

Solar has limited direct exposure to stock market movements and reduces electricity expenses. Mutual funds offer diversification, liquidity, and potential long-term wealth creation without requiring property ownership.

How Does Inflation Affect the Four Options? 

Inflation affects each option differently. Solar can benefit when electricity tariffs rise, while tax and inflation can weaken the real return from an FD. Gold and equity-oriented mutual funds may outpace inflation, but their market-linked performance is uncertain.

  • Solar: Rising electricity tariffs increase the value of every unit consumed at home. In most Indian states, tariffs rise by 3-6% annually.
  • Fixed deposit: The principal and interest may grow, but tax and inflation can reduce the real purchasing power of the maturity amount.
  • Gold: Gold can support diversification during inflation or economic uncertainty, but it does not increase consistently every year.
  • Mutual funds: Equity-oriented funds may outperform inflation over long periods, but short-term losses remain possible.

What Is the Solar Payback Period? 

The solar payback period is the time required for electricity savings to recover the net cost of a rooftop solar system. It is calculated by dividing the net solar installation cost by annual savings.

Factors That Affect the Payback Time for Solar Panels

Two homes in the same city can have a different payback time for solar panels because their electricity use, roof conditions, system design, and financing choices may not be the same.

Here are the main factors that affect the solar PV payback period :

  1. Total system cost: Higher installation costs increase the amount you must recover. Panel quality, inverter type, mounting structure, wiring, installation complexity, and brand selection all affect the final price.
  2. Solar subsidy: The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy reduces the upfront cost. A larger subsidy lowers the net investment and shortens the payback period.
  3. Electricity tariff: Solar delivers greater savings when it replaces expensive grid electricity. Homes in higher tariff slabs usually recover their investment faster than homes with lower or subsidized electricity rates.
  4. Annual solar generation: A system that generates more units each year produces higher savings.
  5. Roof direction and shading: South-facing roofs with limited shade generally produce more electricity. Trees, nearby buildings, water tanks and poorly positioned panels can reduce output and extend the solar PV payback period.
  6. Net-metering rules: The treatment of exported electricity differs by state and DISCOM (distribution company). Export credits, settlement periods, carry-forward rules, and billing methods can all influence annual savings.
  7. Financing cost: A solar loan reduces the upfront payment, but interest and processing fees increase the total cost. A longer loan tenure or higher interest rate can extend the solar payback period.
  8. Maintenance and equipment replacement: Routine cleaning and maintenance costs are usually limited, but inverter repairs or replacement can reduce lifetime savings. These expenses should be included when calculating a more realistic solar panel payback period.
  9. Panel degradation: Solar panels produce slightly less electricity as they age. The reduction is small each year, but it affects total generation.
  10. Battery storage: Adding a battery energy storage system (BESS) increases the initial system cost and usually extends the payback period.

On-Grid vs Off-Grid vs Hybrid Solar Payback

The type of solar system you choose strongly affects the payback period because each configuration has different upfront costs, savings potential, and dependence on batteries. In most cases, on-grid solar systems recover their cost fastest, while battery-based systems take longer.

  • On-grid payback period: On-grid PV systems have the shortest solar power payback time because they do not require batteries. The home uses solar power during the day, imports electricity from the grid when required, and can export surplus generation under the applicable net-metering rules.
  • Hybrid payback period: Hybrid solar systems combine a grid connection with battery backup. The battery increases the installation cost. So, the solar energy payback period gets longer than that of an on-grid system.
  • Off-grid payback period: Off-grid solar systems depend on batteries to supply electricity when solar generation is unavailable. Their higher battery cost, limited export options, and future replacement expenses result in the longest financial payback period. They are usually chosen for energy independence or locations without a reliable grid connection, rather than for the fastest return on investment.

How to Reduce Your Solar Payback Period?

These steps can move your solar payback time toward the lower end of the expected range while improving the system’s lifetime savings –

  1. Claim the full subsidy: The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy directly reduces your net system cost.
  2. Choose the right system size: Match the system to your electricity consumption to avoid unnecessary oversizing.
  3. Increase self-consumption: Use major appliances during solar-generation hours to replace more grid electricity.
  4. Understand net-metering rules: Check how your DISCOM credits exported units and settles surplus generation.
  5. Avoid shading: Install high-efficiency solar panels where trees, tanks, and nearby buildings do not block sunlight.
  6. Choose reliable equipment: Efficient panels, a high-quality solar inverter, and proper installation help protect long-term generation.
  7. Maintain the system: Regular solar panel cleaning and timely repairs plus servicing help prevent avoidable generation losses.

Common Solar Payback Calculation Mistakes

Small calculation errors can make the solar payback period look much shorter or longer than it really is.

So, avoid these common mistakes –

  • Using the pre-subsidy cost: Calculate solar payback using the final amount you actually pay after subsidy.
  • Ignoring self-consumption: The share of solar power used within the property has a major effect on annual savings.
  • Forgetting recurring costs: Include maintenance, insurance, loan interest, and possible inverter replacement.
  • Ignoring panel degradation: Solar generation reduces gradually over the system’s life.
  • Assuming tariffs remain unchanged: Electricity tariff increases can shorten the actual payback period.
  • Using unrealistic generation estimates: Shading, dust, roof direction, and system losses can reduce output.

A reliable estimate should use your actual system cost, local tariff, expected generation, self-consumption, and applicable net-metering rules.

#System Sizing: How Many kW Does Your Facility Need?

The sizing of solar panel depends on two things, including the amount of electricity consumed by you and the available roof space.

For a warehouse consuming 30,000-1,00,000* units per month, it would cover roughly 65–80%* of daytime consumption.

The right way to size a system properly is through an energy audit. It looks at your actual consumption data month by month, identifies your peak load hours, and maps that against the solar generation potential of your specific location and roof orientation.

#CAPEX vs RESCO vs Open Access: Which Model Works for You?

One thing that has changed over the last few years is solar financing. You no longer have to pay for the entire capital at once. There are various solar models through which you can finance warehouses:

Three ways you can finance your solar for warehouses:

  • Capex model: For the CAPEX solar model, you or your business has to pay for the system upfront. The best part is you own it and keep all the savings. The upfront cost is high in this case, but you get the best long-term returns as every rupee saved on electricity belongs to you.
  • RESCO model: For the RESCO model, a solar company installs the system on your roof at their cost. You pay them per unit for the electricity generated, at a rate lower than your current grid tariff. The best part of this model is zero upfront investment. The solar company recovers its investment over time through the per-unit charges.
  • Open Access: If you are looking for a facility with an above ~1 MW consumption, you can source solar power directly from a solar plant or through the grid. You need to pay only for transmission and wheeling charges. Open access is best for facilities where roof space is limited, but power consumption is very high.

For most warehouse operators exploring solar for the first time, the RESCO model is the easiest entry point. It has no capital outlay, immediate savings, and no operational responsibility for the system.

Government Subsidies and Open Access: What’s Changed

For commercial and industrial solar users, two policy frameworks have significantly improved the economics over the last three years.

  • Accelerated Depreciation (AD): Businesses installing solar can claim 40% depreciation in Year 1 under Section 32 of the Income Tax Act. For a ₹3 crore installation, that’s ~₹ 1.2 crore of depreciation benefit in the first year alone, substantially reducing the effective capital outlay.
  • Green Energy Open Access Rules (2022): This is the bigger game-changer for large facilities. Under the Green Energy Open Access framework, any commercial or industrial consumer using more than 100 kW can now directly purchase renewable energy from producers via a Power Purchase Agreement, paying only wheeling and transmission charges. For warehouses that can’t install enough rooftop solar to meet their full demand, either due to roof space constraints or very high consumption, open access allows them to source the balance from nearby solar parks at fixed, below-grid rates.
  • Net Metering: For rooftop systems, most states now allow net metering, where excess solar generation gets exported to the grid and credited against future bills. This effectively means a well-designed rooftop system can achieve near-zero electricity cost for the hours when generation exceeds consumption.

The Cold Chain Opportunity Is Still Largely Untapped

Compared to standard warehouses, cold storage opportunities are still not being touched for solar.

Cold chain operations are among the most energy-intensive in the logistics sector. Refrigeration compressors, blast freezers, temperature-controlled docking areas. These run continuously, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. If we look at the total electricity bill, 20-30% represents the total operating cost for a cold storage facility.

The only drawback with cold chain solar is that consumption doesn’t stop at night. This means battery storage or the grid is required at night. But during the day, a well-sized solar system can cover a substantial fraction of the refrigeration load, directly reducing the highest-cost power consumption.

In addition, the payback periods are longer than standard warehousing due to the battery component.
