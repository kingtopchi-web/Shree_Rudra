Why Pigeons Nest Under Solar Panels

Pigeons usually nest under solar panels because it is a perfect hidden place that looks like their natural habitat. The gap under the panels also provides shade, warmth, and a dry environment good for them and their squabs.

  • The gap under the panel is a perfect nest site: You may have noticed solar panels sit slightly raised off the roof, usually to allow airflow and the right tilt angle. This creates a shallow, sheltered gap, which is an ideal place for any bird to live. It’s warm, dry, shielded from wind and rain, and mostly out of the predators’ reach.
  • Roofs and terraces already attract pigeons: In most places in India, pigeons are very common on roofs and terraces. Even if you have not installed solar panels, rooftops already attract pigeons. Open water tanks, nearby food sources, ledges, and parapets all make a terrace attractive. When you install a rooftop solar system with a sheltered gap under solar panels, you’ve essentially built them a ready-made home.
  • Once pigeons nest, they tend to return: Pigeons are creatures of habit. A pair that successfully raises chicks under your panel will often come back to the same spot for future broods. This is why early prevention matters so much more than dealing with it after the fact.

How to Prevent Pigeons Under Solar Panels: 6 Methods

Use mesh with non-penetrating clips around the panel edges, paired with spikes on nearby perching spots to stop pigeons from nesting under solar panels. Solar panel skirts and removing food or water sources help too. Below are all 6 methods explained in detail.

1. Solar Panel Mesh with Clips (Best Option)

Using solar panel mesh with clips is one of the most effective and recommended methods to prevent pigeons from nesting under solar panels. A mesh barrier is fitted around the panel’s perimeter, closing off the gap underneath. The clips are used to attach this mesh. These non-penetrating clips grip the panel frame without drilling into it.

You must make sure you use PVC-coated stainless-steel mesh, which holds up far better over years of sun and rain compared to cheap galvanised wire mesh, which tends to rust and sag.

2. Solar panel skirts

Skirts are a low-visibility alternative to mesh. They run along the edge of the panel array, closing the gap in a way that’s barely noticeable from the ground. They work on a similar principle to mesh but tend to blend in more visually.

3. Bird spikes

Spikes work well on ridges, edges, parapets, and any flat surface where pigeons like to perch before moving in. They’re not a substitute for closing off the gap under the panels, though. Spikes stop birds from landing on a surface; they don’t stop birds from entering an open gap. You can use them alongside mesh, not instead of it.

4. Bird netting

If you have a large terrace or open areas with a lot of exposed roof space, netting can cover a wider area than mesh or skirts alone. You must use bird netting for bigger installations or terraces where pigeons have multiple potential landing and nesting zones, not just the space under the panels.

5. Decoys and predator statues

You can add fake owls or predator decoys that can provide short-term deterrence. The catch is pigeons are smart enough to figure out a decoy isn’t a real threat once it stays in the same spot for a while. You can treat this as a minor, temporary measure at best, not a real solution.

6. Remove food, water, and perches

If you want to keep pigeons away from your panels, start by removing what draws them there in the first place. Clean up any open waste or food scraps lying around. Cover up standing water in open tanks. And trim back any overhanging tree branches, since they give birds an easy landing spot right next to your roof.

Problems Pigeons Cause Under Solar Panels

Pigeons nesting under your panels brings dirty droppings, a real fire risk from nesting material near wires, possible cable damage and hot spots, plus noise, mess and health concerns from the droppings. Here’s a closer look at each one.

  • Droppings and soiling: Pigeon droppings build up on and around the panels over time. This affects the overall efficiency of the solar panels. A panel covered in droppings simply can’t soak up as much sunlight as a clean one, which means it generates less power than it should.

You can’t really control what the birds do. But you can control how clean your panels stay, either by handling it yourself or bringing in a professional.

Signs Pigeons Are Nesting Under Your Panels

Not sure if you already have a problem? If you notice two or more of these, it’s worth checking under the panel directly, or calling in a professional to look. Look out for:

  • Droppings or feathers collecting at the lower edge of the panel
  • Twigs or nesting debris visible under the frame
  • Cooing sounds coming from under the panels, especially early morning
  • Birds repeatedly flying in and out of the same gap

Why Airflow and Warranty Matter When Proofing

If you have solar panels at home, you should know the panels need airflow underneath to stay cool. A panel that runs hotter than it should loses efficiency. So you should avoid sealing the gap completely with a solid sheet or board, even if it seems like the simplest fix.

Instead of using a solid sheet or board, mesh allows air to pass through while still keeping birds out, which is exactly why it’s the preferred method.

Also, make sure you never drill into the panel frame, and never fix anything directly to the glass. Both of them can void your manufacturer’s warranty immediately. Before doing any DIY proofing work, check your installer’s warranty terms first. Some installers include specific conditions about what modifications are and aren’t allowed on the mounting structure.

Already Have Pigeons Nesting? Do This First

Before disturbing the nest, you must check the legal position first, wait until it’s abandoned if there are eggs or chicks, clean the area safely, and seal the gap right after so the pigeons can’t come back.

  • Check the legal position: Pigeons in India fall under general bird protection provisions, and interfering with an active nest isn’t something to do casually. You must check with a local pest control or wildlife professional about your specific situation before removing any nest, rather than assuming it’s fine to clear it out yourself. Rules and local interpretation can vary, so this is one area where it’s worth getting it right rather than guessing.
  • Wait until the nest is abandoned: If there are eggs or chicks present, the safest and most humane approach is to wait until the nest is naturally abandoned, or the chicks have fledged.
  • Clean and disinfect the area safely: Once the nest is empty, clean the area properly. Wear gloves and a mask, and use a damp cloth or spray to wipe down droppings rather than dry-sweeping them, which can send dust and particles into the air.
  • Install mesh straight afterwards: Don’t leave the gap open once you’ve cleaned it. Pigeons will return to a spot that worked for them before. Installing mesh or a skirt immediately after cleanup is what actually stops them from coming back.

Prevent It from Day One: What to Ask Your Solar Installer

The cheapest and easiest time to pigeon-proof your system is during installation itself, before any bird has had the chance to settle in. You must ask your installer directly whether bird-proofing can be included as part of the original setup.

Also ask about your solar mounting structure height and gap size. Flush-mount systems, which sit closer to the roof with a narrower gap, tend to see this problem less than elevated mounting structures with a wider clearance underneath.

You must ask your solar installer about their standard approach to this issue, whether proofing is offered as part of the installation package, and what material and clip type they use.

Frequently Asked Question

Can pigeons damage solar panels?

Not directly; they’re not going to crack your panel glass or anything like that. But their droppings pile up and make the panels dirty, any nesting material sitting near your wiring can be a fire risk, and if debris builds up long enough, it can create shaded patches that turn into hot spots.

Does mesh reduce power output of the solar panels?

No, mesh sits around the edges of the panel, not across the surface, so it’s not blocking any sunlight or airflow. It shouldn’t affect your system’s output if installed correctly by someone who understands panel placement.

Does proofing void the solar panel warranty?

It can, but only if it’s done the wrong way. If you are drilling into the panel frame or sticking anything onto the glass, that can void your warranty. You can use non-penetrating clips; they hold the mesh in place without touching the panel itself.

Is it legal to remove a pigeon nest?

This depends on local rules and whether the nest is active with eggs or chicks. You must check with a local pest control professional or your municipal authority before removing any nest, rather than assuming it’s permitted.

Do spikes alone work for pigeons nesting under the solar panels?

Spikes are great for stopping pigeons from perching on ledges and flat edges, but they won’t do anything about that open gap under your panels. You really need mesh alongside the spikes if you want this handled properly.

How long does mesh last?

This depends heavily on material quality. PVC-coated stainless-steel mesh tends to last several years with minimal degradation, while cheaper galvanised wire mesh can rust and weaken much sooner, especially in humid or coastal regions.

Types of Rooftop Solar Servicing

Rooftop solar servicing falls into two broad categories: preventive and corrective service.

Preventive Service

This is planned, routine service carried out to prevent faults and maintain system performance. It typically includes module cleaning, visual inspections, checking electrical connections, monitoring generation, and inspecting inverters and mounting structures.

For a rooftop solar system in India, preventive services may include:

  • Module cleaning: Removing dust, bird droppings, and other soiling that can reduce generation, particularly in dusty or high-pollution locations.
  • Visual inspection: Checking modules, cables, connectors, mounting structures, and junction boxes for visible damage, corrosion, loose connections, or signs of overheating.
  • Electrical checks: Inspecting connections, earthing, protection devices, and other electrical components as per the system’s O&M requirements.
  • Inverter inspection: Checking inverter performance, error logs, ventilation, and operating conditions.
  • Generation monitoring: Reviewing plant performance to identify unusual drops in generation or deviations from expected output.
  • Vegetation and rooftop checks: Ensuring surrounding vegetation, debris, or new rooftop installations do not cause shading or obstruct access to the system.

Corrective Service

This is repair work carried out after a fault or issue is detected. It may involve replacing damaged modules, cables, connectors, inverters, or other faulty components to restore normal system operation.

Common corrective service activities include:

  • Replacing damaged solar modules, cables, connectors, or junction boxes
  • Repairing or replacing faulty inverters and protection equipment
  • Fixing loose or damaged electrical connections
  • Addressing earthing or insulation faults
  • Repairing damaged mounting structures or components
  • Troubleshooting repeated inverter alarms or generation losses
  • Restoring communication and monitoring systems when plant data is unavailable

In order to ensure this doesn’t happen with your installation, professional service becomes critical. The following are some other things that can increase your factory rooftop solar performance:

  • Panel cleaning: For commercial and large industrial plants, solar panels should ideally be cleaned twice a month to prevent dust buildup. In highly dusty or polluted environments, such as MIDCs, cleaning frequency should be increased to 2-3 cycles per month.
  • Once-a-month preventive maintenance (PM): This includes visual inspections of electrical terminals, cables, and connections, along with parameter checks at each termination point to ensure the system is operating within expected limits.
  • Quarterly preventive maintenance (PM): This involves more in-depth technical checks, such as inspecting cooling fans, verifying termination tightness, using thermal imaging to detect hotspots, and EL imaging to detect early-stage microcracks that may not be visible during routine inspections.

Red Flags: When Your Factory Solar System Needs Immediate Attention

A factory solar system needs immediate attention when generation drops persistently, panels show mineral deposits, inverters display faults, roof leaks appear, or vegetation and debris cause shading.

  • Generation drop of 10-15% on the monitoring dashboard sustained over 2-3 weeks; check soiling first, then strings.
  • Visible mineral spots (white scaling) on panels; hard-water deposits require chemical descaling, not just a rinse.
  • Inverter fault codes or unusual fan noise: don’t wait for the next quarterly visit.
  • Water stains on the factory ceiling near mounting points: address waterproofing failures immediately; production downtime from a roof leak costs far more than a maintenance call.
  • Vegetation or debris at roof edges causing partial shading on perimeter panels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need professional service for a factory solar system, or can I manage maintenance in-house?

For systems above 50 kW, professional maintenance is often more cost-effective than in-house management; not because the tasks are highly complex, but because consistency matters. In-house maintenance typically involves basic cleaning without specialised equipment or technical checks. Professional services, on the other hand, include thorough panel cleaning, along with inspections of wiring, mounting structures, and other system components. Regular professional maintenance helps keep the system performing efficiently and reduces the risk of issues going unnoticed.

Does rain clean solar panels sufficiently?

Partially, and only for loose surface dust. Rain can wash away light debris, but it cannot remove bird droppings, hard-water mineral deposits, industrial soot, or oily particulates common near factories. Relying solely on rain is not recommended for optimal production; it can extend intervals between manual cleans, but it doesn’t replace them.

What happens if you don’t clean solar panels for months?

Output drops, silently and progressively. Research on Indian PV installations shows that dust and soiling can cause significant generation losses, with the impact varying widely by location, season, and cleaning frequency. Studies in India have recorded daily soiling losses of around 0.3–0.6% in some conditions and monthly losses exceeding 20% when panels remain heavily soiled.

Can I use tap water to clean solar panels?

Not advisable, especially at factory scale. Tap water contains dissolved minerals (calcium, magnesium) that leave white residue on the glass surface after the water evaporates. Over multiple cleaning cycles, this mineral scaling builds up and becomes a persistent barrier to sunlight, one that a regular rinse won’t remove. DM (demineralised) water is the correct choice for panel cleaning.

How long do solar panels last, and does maintenance extend their lifespan?

Most quality solar panels carry a 25-30 year performance warranty, with output typically guaranteed at 80-85% of rated capacity at the end of that period. Maintenance does not significantly change the panel’s natural degradation rate, which mainly depends on the materials used. However, regular maintenance prevents avoidable power losses from dust and dirt and helps protect the inverter and mounting structure, which typically last 10-15 years and 25+ years, respectively, when properly maintained.

How much does it cost to maintain a 500 kW solar system in India?

The annual maintenance cost of a 500 kW solar system in India depends on factors such as site location, system design, cleaning frequency, vegetation, equipment condition, and the scope of the O&M contract. A typical O&M contract may cover module cleaning, preventive inspections, inverter checks, monitoring, and minor corrective work. For an accurate estimate, the system size alone is not enough; the site and required service scope should also be considered.

What direction should factory solar panels face?

In India, factory solar panels are generally oriented towards the south to maximise annual solar generation, as India is in the Northern Hemisphere. However, the ideal orientation depends on the factory’s location, roof structure, available space, shading, and whether the system uses a fixed or adjustable mounting structure. On a factory roof, a slightly different orientation may sometimes be more practical if it allows better roof utilisation without significantly affecting generation.

Can I lose my solar panel warranty due to poor maintenance?

Poor maintenance of solar panels on a roof does not automatically void a solar panel warranty. However, manufacturers’ warranty terms may exclude damage or performance issues caused by neglect, improper cleaning, unauthorised modifications, physical damage, or failure to follow specified installation and maintenance requirements. It is therefore important to follow the panel manufacturer’s warranty conditions and maintain records of inspections, cleaning, and any repairs carried out on the system.

What is Solar Panel Degradation?

Solar panel degradation is the gradual reduction in a panel’s ability to convert sunlight into solar electricity over time. Every type of solar panel goes through degradation year after year.

As solar cells are exposed to real-world conditions such as heat, sunlight, and electrical load, their internal materials begin to wear and tear. It leads to a slow but steady decline in efficiency and power output.

Here are the key factors that result in degradation of PV modules:

  • Continuous heat generation within cells
  • Exposure to sunlight and UV radiation
  • Material aging under electrical stress
  • Environmental impact due to dust, humidity, and temperature
  • Handling and installation-related micro-damage
  • Long-term operational wear and tear

Although it can’t be eliminated, solar panel degradation can certainly be lowered through proper maintenance and handling.

Degradation Factor of Solar Panels vs Derating Factor of Solar Panels: Are They the Same?

These two terms are often confused, but they refer to very different aspects of a rooftop solar panel system for homes.

  • Solar panel degradation is related to the panels themselves: It represents the gradual loss of their ability to generate electricity as they age, driven by material and environmental factors.
  • Derating factor of solar panels: Derating is linked to the solar inverter. It occurs when the inverter is unable to convert the full input power from the panels into usable output. This usually happens due to inverter overloading. A conversion loss of around 1.5% to 2% is considered normal, while in higher-capacity inverters, it can increase to 3% to 4%.

In simple terms, PV module degradation rate reduces how much power the panels can produce, while derating factor of solar panels affects how much of that produced power is actually converted and delivered.

What Causes Solar Panel Degradation?

Annual degradation of solar panels occurs due to internal material behavior and external operating conditions. Once panels are installed in the real environment outside the labs, they are exposed to heat, sunlight, electrical stress, and installation-related factors that gradually reduce their efficiency.

Some of these causes are natural and unavoidable, while others depend on how well the system is handled and maintained.

Here are the key factors that contribute to solar panel yearly degradation:

  • First-time exposure to sunlight and other environmental factors: PV panels are tested under Standard Test Conditions in a controlled lab environment. Once installed outdoors, exposure to real weather conditions such as heat, dust, and humidity initiates the degradation process, especially in the first year.
  • Thermal stress from electrical flow: Semiconductor cells allow electric current to pass through them, which generates heat. Continuous heating over long periods degrades the cell structure and reduces solar panel efficiency.
  • Light-induced degradation (LID): Initial exposure to sunlight causes a performance drop due to material-level reactions within the cells. This varies based on the type of module and materials used.
  • Potential-induced degradation (PID in solar panels): Voltage differences within the module, especially when charge is not properly grounded after sunset, can lead to leakage currents and performance loss over time.
  • Material aging and electrical stress: Constant exposure to sunlight and electrical load can cause slow degradation of internal materials, even under normal operating conditions.
  • Poor handling and installation practices: Rough transportation, improper panel mounting, or stress during installation and maintenance can cause micro-damage to cells, accelerating solar panel degradation over time.

  • Inadequate maintenance practices: Lack of professional solar panel cleaning, loose connections, or delayed issue detection can worsen performance decline and increase photovoltaic degradation rates over time.

What is Potential-Induced Degradation of Solar Cells and Panels?

Potential-Induced Degradation (PID), also known as PID, is a performance loss caused by voltage differences within the solar module. After sunset, if the electrical charge in the modules is not properly grounded, it can lead to leakage currents. Over time, this results in a reduction in power output and overall panel efficiency.

What is Light-Induced Degradation of Solar Panels?

Light-Induced Degradation, or LID, refers to the initial drop in performance that occurs when solar panels are first exposed to sunlight within the first few hours to days of operation.

It happens due to internal reactions within the silicon cells, where defects form and trap charge carriers, reducing the panel’s efficiency. The extent of this loss depends on the type of module and the materials used in manufacturing. That’s why different solar cell technologies show varying levels of LID.

Normal Annual Degradation of Solar Panels vs Preventable Performance Loss

Not all performance loss in a solar system is due to panel degradation. While some solar panel degradation per year is natural and unavoidable, a portion of the loss is preventable and depends on how well the system is designed, installed, and maintained.

Let’s understand both:

  • Natural solar panel degradation per year: This is the unavoidable decline in efficiency that occurs as solar panels age. It’s a result of heat generation within semiconductor cells, prolonged exposure to sunlight, material aging under electrical stress, and real-world conditions such as temperature, dust, and humidity.
  • Preventable performance loss in solar systems: This refers to efficiency losses caused by system design, poor installation quality, and maintenance gaps. For example, wiring and cable losses can reduce output if not properly designed or maintained. Poor execution during installation, loose connections, or lack of regular maintenance can further increase these losses. The good news is that all these losses can be prevented through proper system design, high-quality solar installation, regular maintenance, and ongoing performance diagnostics.

What are Anti-PID Solar Panels?

An anti-PID module marketed to have PID-free solar cells is either a marketing gimmick or a miscommunication. No solar panel can be 100% free from Potential Induced Degradation, as PID is influenced not only by module design but also by system voltage, grounding quality, and operating conditions over time.

What solar panel manufacturers actually mean by anti-PID is that the module is designed to be more resistant to PID. This is achieved through improvements in materials, cell structure, and protective layers that reduce the likelihood and severity of leakage currents.

In simple terms, anti-PID panels can reduce the impact of PID but cannot eliminate it.

How to Reduce PID in Solar Panels?

While PID in solar panels cannot be fully avoided, proper system design, handling, and maintenance can significantly reduce its impact.

Let’s find out all the practices that can lower PID damage in solar panels:

  • Regular system maintenance: Routine checks help ensure the system operates within safe electrical limits and reduce the chances of PID-related issues.
  • Module-to-module earthing: Proper earthing between panels prevents unwanted voltage differences from building up across modules.
  • High-quality DC-side earthing: The system’s DC earthing should be properly implemented. If grounding is weak, PID effects can increase over time.

Signs Your Solar Panels Are Degrading Faster Than Normal

Certain signs, like a drop in solar generation and cell discoloration, are tell-tale signs that your panels might be degrading faster than normal. Identifying them early can help you take corrective action and avoid long-term performance loss.

Here are the most critical indicators to watch for:

  • Noticeable drop in power generation: If your system is producing significantly less electricity than expected for your location for that particular season, it could indicate accelerated performance decline.
  • Uneven output across panels: When some panels generate less power than others in the same system, it may point to localized degradation or damage.
  • Visible cell discoloration: Changes in cell color can signal internal issues. In Mono-PERC panels, for example, degraded cells appear darker compared to surrounding cells.
  • Frequent system performance fluctuations: Inconsistent output without clear external reasons such as weather changes may indicate degradation.
  • Hotspots or localized heating: Certain areas of the panel heating more than others can be a sign of cell damage or electrical faults, which can accelerate solar panel degradation.

Solar generation refers to the amount of solar electricity produced by a solar system over a given period. It is measured in units or kilowatt-hours (kWh) and showcases how effectively your system converts solar energy into usable AC electricity.

Interestingly, this solar panel output is not fixed. It changes based on factors such as sunlight availability, system efficiency, and seasonal conditions. For example, a 1 kW solar system in one city may generate more electricity than the same system in another city within the same state due to differences in solar irradiance and weather patterns.

How Do Solar Panels Generate Electricity?

Solar panels use the photovoltaic effect to convert sunlight into electricity. Direct sunlight striking the panels is absorbed by solar cells and converted into DC power.

Let’s have a look at the simple steps that change sunlight into DC power:

  • Sunlight falls on solar panels: Solar cells absorb photons from sunlight, which carry energy.
  • Photons excite electrons in solar cells: The energy from photons frees electrons from their atomic bonds and causes them to move.
  • An electron-hole pair is formed: When an electron leaves its position, it creates a hole with a positive charge. The internal electric field at the p-n junction separates these charges.
  • Electrons flow in one direction: The movement of electrons in a single direction creates direct current (DC) electricity.
  • Current flows through the circuit: Metal contacts on the solar cell collect the flowing electrons and transfer the current through wires.
  • Inverter converts DC to AC: The generated DC electricity is converted into alternating current (AC) by the solar inverter, making it usable for homes and appliances.

Why Actual Generation/Power and Rated Generation/Power May Not Match Exactly?

The power rating of a solar panel is based on ideal test conditions. However, in real-world installations, these conditions are not met, which is why actual solar generation differs from the rated solar output.

The key reasons for this difference are:

  • Standard Test Conditions (STC): Solar panels are rated under laboratory conditions known as STC, which assume a cell temperature of 25°C, specific sunlight intensity of 1,000 W/m², and clear skies. These conditions are almost never present in real installations. Thus, actual solar generation tends to vary from the rated one.
  • Temperature-related losses: While higher sunlight increases generation, it also raises the panel temperature. Solar panels lose about 0.3% to 0.4% of their efficiency for every degree Celsius above 25°C, reducing overall solar output during hot conditions.

Why Your Solar Generation May Be Low?

A drop in solar panel generation does not always mean something is wrong, but it should never be ignored. If your solar system is producing less electricity than expected, the cause could range from simple maintenance issues to technical faults within the system.

The most common reasons for low solar system output are:

  • Dusty panels: Dust, dirt, and pollution accumulate, blocking sunlight from reaching the solar cells and reducing overall energy production. A solar panel that hasn’t been cleaned for 3 months can reduce output by 10-18%.
  • Bird droppings: Even small patches of bird droppings can block entire sections of a panel, leading to noticeable drops in output and hotspots if left unchecked.
  • Shadows due to poor design: Shadows from nearby buildings or trees can significantly reduce solar generation, especially if the system is not designed to handle shading effectively.
  • Incorrect panel orientation and tilt: Panels that are not positioned at the appropriate tilt angle facing south may not receive maximum sunlight throughout the day.
  • Loose electrical connections: Faulty or loose wiring can interrupt the flow of electricity, reducing the system’s output.
  • Incorrect inverter configuration: If the inverter is not properly configured, it may not convert power efficiently, leading to lower usable output.
  • Panel defects like hotspots and microcracks: Manufacturing defects or aging can create hotspots or microcracks, reducing solar panel efficiency and long-term performance.

To understand whether low solar generation is a concern, it’s important to evaluate it in the right context rather than reacting to short-term changes.

Here’s how you can interpret a drop in solar output:

  • When low generation is completely normal: Seasonal changes, cloudy days, monsoon periods, or temporary shading naturally reduce solar output without indicating any system issue.
  • When low generation is a warning sign: A consistent drop compared to past performance or expected benchmarks for your location may indicate problems such as dirt buildup, inverter faults, or system damage.
  • How much drop should concern you: A drop of up to 10-15% can occur due to seasonal change, but anything beyond this range over a sustained period should be investigated.

Why do Mumbai Solar Systems Need Regular Maintenance?

Mumbai monsoons are no mystery to anybody. Long spells of monsoon rains result in high humidity that, when combined with the salt-laden air from the Arabian Sea, is highly corrosive to even the best solar panels.

Mumbai throws a mixture of coastal salt exposure, extreme monsoon cycles, and dense pollution on your panels. That combination is why maintenance here is not optional.

Common Rooftop Solar Issues in Coastal and Monsoon Conditions

Besides year-round high humidity and salty, corrosive air, Mumbai is also an urban city that sees as much pigeon activity as construction, making bird droppings and pollution among the main causes that, if not addressed through periodic solar panel cleaning, can result in lower electricity output.

Here’s a closer look at all the major issues that make frequent panel cleaning and maintenance important:

  • Salt deposits from the Arabian Sea: This salt settles on panel glass, frames, connectors, and mounting hardware. It reacts with moisture to form a mildly acidic solution that corrodes metal much faster than normal water exposure.
  • Post-monsoon grime and mineral residue: Rainwater in polluted urban areas has pollutants and minerals that leave behind stains on the panel surface. After months of Mumbai’s monsoon, panels end up with hardened deposits that regular rain or DIY cleaning on your own cannot wash away.
  • Humidity-related moisture damage: High air moisture levels can affect metallic components, solar connectors, and electrical wiring in solar systems. If the seal around junction boxes or cable entry points has weakened, moisture can get in, causing internal corrosion and resulting in generation drops.
  • Dust and pollution: Construction dust and smoke from vehicles settle on panels daily, forming a layer that blocks sunlight from reaching the panels directly. This reduces the solar radiation absorbed by the panels, in turn, lowering power generation.
  • Bird droppings and hotspot formation: Bird droppings can reduce panel performance and permanently damage affected solar cells. Unlike dust, bird droppings stick to the glass, resist rain, and, under intense sunlight, the acid in them burns into the surface, creating hotspots.
  • Corrosion between metals: When an aluminium panel frame comes into contact with steel mounting hardware, corrosion eats away at the aluminium where the two metals meet. Mumbai’s salty air and humidity make this form of corrosion very aggressive and hard to spot early.

FAQs

How much does it cost to clean one solar panel?

While it can cost upwards of Rs. 50 to clean one solar panel; panel-level cleaning services are not recommended. These services will only remove visible dirt, leaving structural and electrical problems with panels, connectors, inverters, and mounting structures. That’s why you should opt for cleaning and maintenance services that offer inspection as a part of the package.

What is the best time to clean solar panels?

Early morning or late evening, when the panels are cool, are the best two time slots to clean solar panels. Cleaning panels during peak sun hours can cause water to evaporate too quickly, leaving behind mineral deposits. It can also cause thermal shock, where cold water hitting hot glass can lead to microcracks in the panel surface.

How much water is required to clean solar panels?

You might need 3 to 7 litres of water per panel when using water alone to clean the modules. Professional cleaning services with specialized equipment can bring this down significantly.

Is tap water ok to clean solar panels?

Using tap water to clean solar panels is usually not recommended because tap water with high mineral content leaves behind limescale and white residue when it dries, which blocks sunlight the same way dust does. Therefore, RO or distilled water is used to properly clean solar panels.

Can dirty solar panels cause problems?

Dirty panels can lose up to 40% of their output if not cleaned for a year. In such a long span, issues such as bird droppings can create hotspots that can permanently damage cells. Over months, the financial loss from reduced generation adds up far beyond the cost of a cleaning visit.

Can I pressure wash solar panels?

Pressure washing solar panels is not recommended at all. High-pressure water can force its way through the seals around panel frames and reach the sensitive electrical components inside. It can also crack the tempered glass and damage the anti-reflective coating.

Understanding Main Solar Safety Concerns: How Solar Systems Can Become Dangerous?

When installed and maintained by trained solar professionals, rooftop solar systems are extremely safe, as they comply with all Indian electrical and fire safety standards. However, several technical and environmental factors can turn a solar installation into a safety risk if it is not maintained by professionals on a periodic basis.

High-voltage DC electricity, outdoor exposure to weather, rooftop work conditions, and multiple electrical connections mean that faults can create dangerous situations, such as fires, electrical shocks, and complete equipment damage, resulting in loss of savings and even loss of life.

Here’s an overview of how rooftop solar can become dangerous when handled by people not trained to work with solar energy systems:

  • Electrical shock risks in solar installations: All types of solar panels generate DC electricity whenever sunlight falls on them. This means DC cables, connectors, and panels may remain live during the day even if the grid supply is turned off. Electrical shocks can occur when exposed wires, damaged cables, or loose MC4 connectors allow contact with live DC circuits.
  • DC arc faults and solar fire hazards: A DC arc fault occurs when electricity jumps across a gap between damaged or loose electrical connections. This can happen if MC4 connectors are not properly locked, cables are cut or worn out, or wiring joints are loose. Unlike AC arcs, DC arcs can sustain longer because DC current flows continuously. This can generate intense heat, melt insulation, and ignite nearby materials, leading to fires.
  • Lightning and surge risks for rooftop solar: Solar panels are installed on rooftops and are the highest metal structures on a building. During thunderstorms, lightning strikes or voltage surges can travel through solar panels, mounting structures, or connected cables. Without proper earthing, lightning arrestors, and surge protection devices, these surges can damage inverters, cables, and electrical appliances connected to the system.
  • Monsoon, humidity, and corrosion risks in India: Solar systems in India operate through extreme weather conditions, including heavy rain, humidity, and heat. Moisture can enter connectors if they are not sealed properly, leading to leakage current or short circuits. Humidity and rain can also cause corrosion of mounting structures, bolts, and cable joints. Over time, rusted structures or degraded wiring can increase safety risks.
  • Working-at-height hazards during installation and maintenance: Installing and maintaining rooftop solar requires technicians to work at height. Accidents can occur if untrained people climb rooftops without proper ladders, harnesses, helmets, and fall protection systems. Slippery roofs, uneven surfaces, and strong winds can increase the risk of falls. This is why trained technicians use safety belts, fall arrest systems, and proper access equipment while working on solar installations.
  • Risk to human life: Solar installations involve working with live electrical systems and performing tasks on rooftops. Accidents can occur if untrained people work without insulated tools, electrical gloves, or other protective gear. Failure to follow solar plant safety protocols during installation or maintenance can result in electrocution and carries the risk of falling from height.

Top Solar Safety Precautions You Must Follow

Most safety incidents in rooftop solar systems happen because basic precautions are ignored during installation or maintenance. Loose connectors, improper earthing, damaged cables, poorly routed wiring, or unstable mounting structures can turn a solar plant into a safety hazard.

Following the right precautions helps prevent electrical shocks, arc faults, fire hazards, equipment failure, and rooftop accidents.

For clarity, we’ve divided solar panel safety precautions into two parts: installation precautions and maintenance precautions.

What are the Top Solar Safety Precautions to Follow Before Installation?

Solar installation mistakes such as poor system design, incorrect wiring, or weak mounting structures can lead to electrical hazards, equipment failure, or rooftop damage later.

Here are the key solar panel safety precautions to prevent issues after installation.

  • Conducting a proper roof safety survey and structural assessment: Before installing solar panels, technicians check whether the roof can safely support the weight of the solar system. They inspect the roof condition, slope, load-bearing capacity, and available space.
  • Using certified solar components only: Solar panels, inverters, cables, solar connectors, and mounting structures should come from certified manufacturers that meet BIS standards. Certified components are tested for electrical safety as well as durability. Using uncertified or low-quality components increases the risk of electrical faults, overheating, or premature equipment failure.
  • Planning system layout and panel connections to keep voltage within safe limits: Solar panels generate DC electricity, and multiple panels are connected together to form a string. When panels are connected in series, the voltage increases. If too many panels are connected in one string, the voltage can exceed the safe input limit of the solar inverter. In rooftop solar panel systems for homes, installers must keep the DC voltage below 800 volts and choose inverters that can safely handle up to 1,000 volts. Proper system design is critical to ensure the voltage remains within safe limits and prevents electrical faults.
  • Installing proper earthing and grounding: Solar panels start generating electricity immediately upon exposure to sunlight, making proper grounding essential. Earthing provides a safe path for fault currents and helps protect the system from electrical shocks and lightning damage. Without proper grounding, fault currents may pass through equipment or structures, creating dangerous conditions.
  • Installing proper isolation and protection devices: Solar systems require electrical protection devices to isolate the system when necessary. Isolation devices such as MCBs help disconnect parts of the system during faults or maintenance. Surge protection devices, or SPDs, protect the system from sudden voltage spikes caused by lightning or grid disturbances.
  • Installing cables properly and routing them safely to prevent damage: Solar installations use DC cables that carry electricity from the panels to the inverter. These cables must be routed carefully to avoid sharp bends, exposure to mechanical stress, or contact with sharp surfaces. Poor cable routing can lead to insulation damage, exposed wiring, or electrical faults.
  • Using only trained installers with proper safety gear: Solar installation requires technicians who understand electrical systems, rooftop safety, and installation standards like the back of their hands. Trained solar professionals use safety gear such as helmets, harnesses, insulated gloves, and safety shoes while working on rooftops. Proper training and equipment help prevent accidents and ensure the solar system is installed correctly.

What are the Top Solar Plant Safety Precautions to Follow During Maintenance?

Once a grid-tied solar system becomes operational, regular maintenance is necessary to keep it safe and functioning properly.

Maintenance activities such as panel cleaning, cable inspection, or equipment checks must be carried out only by trained professionals wearing protective gear, as parts of the system may have DC power when exposed to sunlight, even if the grid has been shut down.

Following the proper safety procedures listed below helps prevent electrical hazards, equipment damage during maintenance, and solar safety concerns.

  • Always shut down the system before maintenance: Before any inspection or repair work begins, the solar system must be safely shut down using the appropriate isolation switches. This helps prevent contact with live electrical circuits and reduces the risk of electric shock while technicians handle cables, connectors, or electrical components.
  • Follow safe solar panel cleaning practices: Solar panels require periodic cleaning to maintain performance, but cleaning should be done using the right tools and methods. Technicians use proper ladders, safety gear, and appropriate cleaning equipment. Panels should not be cleaned with excessive pressure or unsafe methods that could damage the modules, void the warranty, or create electrical hazards.
  • Inspect cables and connectors regularly: DC cables and connectors, such as MC4 connectors, should be checked for damage, loose connections, or wear. Damaged insulation, exposed wiring, or poorly connected cables can cause electrical faults, overheating, or arc faults if left unattended.
  • Monitor inverter warnings and system performance: The inverter continuously monitors the health of the solar system. Warning messages, fault codes, or unusual drops in solar output indicate wiring issues, grounding problems, or component faults. Regular monitoring helps identify problems early, before they become solar safety risks.
  • Check mounting structures and panel stability: The mounting structure holds solar panels securely on the roof. Technicians inspect clamps, nuts, bolts, and frames to ensure the panels remain firmly secured. Loose mounting hardware can allow panels to shift during strong winds or storms.
  • Inspect earthing and lightning protection periodically: Earthing systems and lightning protection devices help protect the solar plant from electrical faults and lightning strikes. A regular solar inspection ensures that grounding is proper and that lightning arrestors and surge protection devices are functioning properly.
  • Keep the rooftop area safe and accessible: The area around the PV system should remain clear and free of obstructions for maintenance work. Walkways should not be blocked, tools or debris should not be left near cables or panels, and a proper parapet wall should surround the roof. An organized rooftop reduces the risk of accidental damage or worker injury during maintenance activities.

Things that Professionals Do Differently for Solar Panel Installation Safety

Professionals follow various safety procedures before and during installation to ensure the system operates safely for many years. These steps include checking the roof condition, designing the system within safe voltage limits, properly routing cables, installing proper earthing, and adding protection devices to prevent electrical faults and fire hazards.

Let’s explore everything that solar professionals follow to make sure that the solar system is safe for you and generates maximum possible power to maximize your solar savings:

  • Pre-installation safety checks and site assessment: Installers inspect the roof condition, available space, structural strength, and surrounding environment before installing panels. This helps ensure the roof can safely support the weight of the solar system.
  • Safe system design and component selection: Installers determine how many panels will be connected in each string so that the DC voltage remains within the inverter’s safe input range.   • Earthing requirements for solar systems: Proper earthing ensures that fault currents or lightning surges have a safe path to the ground. Installers connect panels, mounting structures, and electrical components to the ground to reduce the risk of electric shock and equipment damage.
  • Isolation and protection devices in solar installations: Professionals install isolation switches, MCBs, and surge protection devices to help disconnect the system during faults and protect equipment from voltage spikes or electrical disturbances.

Fire Safety Checklist in Solar Systems

Most fire incidents in solar systems happen due to loose MC4 connectors, damaged DC cables, improper wiring joints, overloaded inverters, faulty components, or poor earthing. These issues can cause overheating or arc faults, leading to fires. Needless to say, preventive checks can help identify these issues before they become dangerous.

The following checklist highlights key precautions to prevent a fire from breaking out near or around a solar system.

  • Perform regular electrical inspections to ensure cables, connectors, and wiring joints are tight and undamaged.
  • Conduct mechanical checks to confirm panels, clamps, and mounting structures remain secure.
  • Monitor plant generation and inverter alerts to detect abnormal system behavior early.
  • Install the inverter in a well-ventilated location to prevent overheating.
  • Ensure MC4 connectors are properly locked and tightened to avoid loose electrical connections.
  • Use proper lugs for wiring connections so electrical joints remain secure.
  • Do not pour water on a broken solar module during the day, as it may still be generating electricity.
  • Check that the lightning arrestor and earthing system are functioning properly.
  • Replace damaged cables, connectors, or modules immediately instead of continuing to operate the system.
  • Conduct periodic maintenance inspections to identify potential fire hazards early.

Checklist for Solar Safety in Monsoon and Extreme Weather

Rooftop solar systems in India face relentless rain, humidity, strong winds, and lightning during the monsoon season every year. These conditions can loosen mounting structures, damage cables, cause corrosion in metal parts, and increase the risk of lightning-related electrical faults.

Without proper inspection before and after the monsoon, these issues can affect both system safety and performance.

The following checklist highlights the key checks that professionals must carry out to help keep solar systems safe during monsoon and extreme weather conditions.

  • Conduct electrical inspections before the monsoon to ensure cables, connectors, and protection devices are functioning properly.
  • Check that modules, mounting structures, and clamps are tightly secured.
  • Inspect lightning arrestors and ensure they are functioning properly.
  • Verify that MCBs and other protection devices are in proper working condition.
  • Ensure there are no loose objects around the rooftop that could hit the solar panels during storms.
  • Check welded joints and mounting frames for structural strength.
  • Inspect mounting structures for rust and apply zinc spray if corrosion is visible.
  • Perform a full inspection of the system after the monsoon season to identify any damage caused by weather.
  • Ensure earthing connections remain intact and capable of handling lightning surges.

Emergency Solar Panel Safety Precautions When Something Goes Wrong

Some solar system faults require immediate attention because they can quickly become dangerous. Warning signs of an unsafe solar plant include a burning smell from electrical components, visible smoke or sparking near cables or connectors, loose or exposed wiring, malfunctioning electrical equipment, unusual heating of the inverter, repeated inverter tripping, or sudden system shutdown.

If any of these signs appear, you should take immediate precautions, listed below, to reduce the risk of fire, electric shock, or equipment damage.

  • Turn off the main power supply: If it is safe to do so, switch off the home’s main MCB from the distribution board to disconnect the system from the electrical supply.
  • Keep people away from the system: Restrict access to the rooftop area, inverter location, and electrical panels until the issue is professionally inspected.
  • Leave the area if there are signs of fire or smoke: Smoke, sparking, or a burning smell can indicate an electrical fault. Moving away from the area helps prevent injury.
  • Avoid touching solar components or wiring: Do not handle panels, cables, connectors, or inverter equipment during an emergency because the system may still be generating electricity.
  • Contact emergency services if fire is present: If you notice flames or rapid overheating near the solar installation, call the fire brigade immediately.

FAQs

Are rooftop solar panels safe for homes in India?

Yes, rooftop solar panels are safe for homes when they are designed, installed, and maintained according to proper safety standards. Trained solar professionals use correct wiring, earthing, protection devices, and mounting structures to ensure the system operates safely. Regular inspection and maintenance reduce the risk of electrical faults or equipment damage.

Can solar panels cause electrical fires?

Yes, electrical faults in the panels and nearby components can create fire risks. Loose connectors, damaged cables, improper wiring joints, or faulty components can produce heat or arc faults. However, following proper installation practices and conducting regular maintenance checks helps prevent such issues.

Why do solar inverters trip during rain or storms?

Inverters trip during rain or storms due to earthing faults or leakage current in the system. This can happen when cables are damaged and come into contact with metal structures, when moisture enters MC4 connectors, or when cable routing is not done properly. These conditions allow current to leak, which triggers the inverter’s protection mechanism.

What is a DC isolator, and why is it important?

A DC isolator is a switch used to disconnect the DC power coming from the solar panels. It acts as a protection device that allows technicians to safely isolate the solar panels from the rest of the system during maintenance or emergencies.

Are solar panels live even when grid power is off?

Yes, solar panels generate electricity whenever sunlight falls on them. Even if the grid supply is turned off, the panels can still produce DC power during the day. This is why proper isolation and safety procedures are required before handling solar equipment.

What is a Solar Inspection and why is it Important?

A solar inspection is the process of professionally evaluating a solar system to check its physical condition, electrical safety, and power generation performance. Importance of Solar Panel Inspection

Regular solar panel inspection plays a crucial role ensuring the PV system is safe and the output over the long-term is maximum.

Here’s what makes inspection of solar panels and other components essential:

  • Detects hidden faults early: Many solar panel defects develop internally and remain invisible to the human eye. A professional solar panel assessment can identify issues such as hotspots, damaged cells, or loose electrical connections before they start affecting system performance.
  • Prevents energy loss: Even small problems, such as partial shading from surrounding objects, dust buildup, or faulty wires, can reduce solar electricity generation. Routine solar PV inspection detects these issues early and ensures your long-term savings are not affected.
  • Improves system lifespan: Solar modules and other electrical components are constantly exposed to heat, dust, and environmental stress. Regular solar inspections allow technicians to identify signs of serious wear and tear and take corrective actions to extend the system’s operational life.
  • Ensures warranty compliance: Many solar manufacturers require professional inspection and maintenance records to keep warranties valid. Professional inspection of solar panels helps homeowners maintain proper documentation and avoid warranty disputes.
  • Ensures plant and household safety: Electrical faults, poor earthing, or damaged wiring pose serious risks of electrical shocks and fires. A thorough solar inspection verifies that the system meets safety standards and operates without electrical hazards that can threaten human life.

What are the Most Critical Solar Panel Inspection Services?

A professional solar panel inspection involves several diagnostic checks that evaluate the condition, safety, and overall performance of the solar system. Some services look for visible signs of wear or damage, while others help detect hidden electrical or thermal faults that cannot be seen during a basic inspection.

Let’s take a look at the most critical solar panel inspection services performed during a professional solar assessment.

  • Visual inspection of solar panels and other components: In this process, technicians physically examine the solar panels, mounting structure, cables, connectors, and the surrounding installation area. This check identifies visible issues such as cracked glass, damaged frames, corrosion, loose clamps, accumulated dust, bird droppings, shading, and poorly managed cable routing.
  • Electrical inspection: An electrical inspection determines whether the solar system’s electrical connections and safety components are working properly. Technicians inspect wiring, connectors, junction boxes, earthing, DC and AC connections, and protective devices. This test is used to find loose connections, burnt terminals, insulation damage, voltage irregularities, or grounding problems that could affect safety and system operation.
  • Performance diagnostics: This service checks whether the solar energy system is generating the electricity it is supposed to produce. Technicians study inverter data, generation records, and monitoring system readings. Then, they compare actual plant output with the expected output. This helps identify underperformance caused by issues such as soiling, shading, wiring losses, or module degradation.
  • Infrared thermography (IR scan): It uses a thermal imaging camera to detect unusual heat patterns in solar panels. This test is used to find hotspots, faulty connectors, overheated cells, and stressed components that are not visible during a normal visual inspection. It is especially useful for detecting hidden thermal defects early, before they lead to permanent module damage.
  • IV testing: During this test, technicians use a device called an IV curve tracer to measure the panel’s current and voltage output. The results are compared with the manufacturer’s expected performance. If the panel is producing less power than expected, the test helps identify problems such as internal cell damage, module degradation, or weak panels in the system.

What are the Most Common Problems Found During a Solar Assessment?

A visual solar assessment can identify issues such as dust buildup, bird droppings, and even corrosion near and around the solar mounts. Advanced testing, such as IR scans, can detect internal cell defects that are invisible to the human eye but cause significant generation loss.

Here’s a snapshot of the most common problems that professionals identify during a solar inspection:

  • Loose or burnt electrical components and connections: Connectors, terminals, or junction box connections may become loose over time. This can cause resistance, overheating, or burnt solar components, potentially affecting system safety.
  • Corroded module mounting structure and its components: Moisture during the monsoon can cause corrosion of rails, bolts, clamps, and other structural parts that support the panels.
  • Dust, bird droppings, and shadow issues: Dirt buildup, bird droppings, and shading from nearby objects block sunlight from reaching the cells. Even small shaded areas can reduce electricity generation across the entire panel string.
  • Earthing issues: Poor grounding or faulty earthing can increase the risk of electrical faults and equipment damage. Proper earthing is essential for electrical safety.
  • Cable dressing issues: Cable dressing refers to how solar cables are routed, secured, and terminated within the system. During a solar PV inspection, technicians check whether cables are properly tied, protected from sharp edges, and safely connected using correctly crimped lugs at terminals. Poor cable dressing can leave wires exposed to weather, rodents, or mechanical stress. Loose or damaged lugs and worn insulation can lead to electrical faults and overheating.
  • Plant offline issues: Sometimes, the solar system stops generating power completely due to inverter faults, tripped protection devices, communication failures, or disconnected wiring. A solar inspection helps identify and resolve the cause quickly.
  • Hotspots on solar panels: These are localized overheating areas on a panel caused by damaged cells, shadows cast by surrounding objects, or electrical stress. Hotspots reduce panel efficiency and can permanently damage the module if ignored.
  • Cell damage, module damage, or cell failures: Solar cells can develop microcracks, internal damage, or degraded sections due to mishandling during transportation, exposure to weather, or manufacturing defects. These issues reduce the panel’s power output.

How Do Professionals Carry Out a Solar PV Inspection?

A professional solar system inspection is a step-by-step process in which trained technicians visit your home to assess your solar system’s condition, safety, and performance.

Here is how a solar PV inspection is carried out in India:

  • Step 1 – System assessment: Technicians begin by understanding the overall system setup. They check the system size, panel layout, inverter details, installation age, and any past complaints, such as low generation or shutdown issues. This step helps them know what to inspect more closely.
  • Step 2 – Visual inspection of solar panels and other components: Technicians examine the panels, mounting structure, cables, connectors, and surrounding area for visible problems. This step helps identify cracks, dirt buildup, bird droppings, shading, loose clamps, corrosion, and poor cable routing.
  • Step 3 – Electrical testing: Technicians inspect wiring, connectors, earthing, voltage levels, and terminals to find faults such as loose connections, burnt components, grounding issues, or damaged insulation.
  • Step 4 – Detailed inspection report: After the initial checks, technicians record all findings in a report. This report usually lists the faults detected, the likely reasons behind them, and the corrective actions recommended.
  • Step 5 – Infrared thermal scanning: If technicians suspect hidden faults, they may perform an infrared (IR) scan using a thermal camera. An IR scan is performed to detect heat patterns that cannot be seen with the naked eye. This step helps locate hotspots, overheating cells, and faulty connectors that may reduce performance or damage the system over time.
  • Step 6 – Performance analysis: If the system shows signs of reduced generation, technicians may carry out a performance analysis. They review the system’s electricity production data and compare it with the expected output based on the system’s size and rooftop sunlight conditions. This helps confirm whether the system is underperforming and whether the detected faults are affecting energy production.

Solar Panel Cleaning Mistakes You Must Avoid For Module Longevity

The most common solar panel cleaning mistake that can ruin the anti-reflective coating is using vinegar or other household cleaners, such as baking soda, to clean the surface. Any sort of DIY (on your own) panel cleaning method can also void the manufacturer’s warranty.

Here’s a breakdown of all the solar panel cleaning mistakes you should steer clear of to ensure your panels generate maximum possible power for more than 25 years:

  • Using vinegar, baking soda, or household cleaning agents: These seem harmless, but are chemically aggressive on solar panel surfaces. They strip the anti-reflective coating that helps panels absorb maximum solar radiation. Once the coating is damaged, the output loss is permanent.
  • Using abrasive cloth, scrubbers, or rough brushes: Steel wool, rough sponges, or any abrasive material scratches the glass surface of your panels. Scratches scatter incoming light, preventing it from reaching the solar cells and reducing generation over time.
  • Cleaning panels in the middle of the day: Panels get extremely hot under direct midday sun. Splashing cold water on a hot panel causes thermal shock, leading to microcracks within the cells. These cracks are invisible to the naked eye but degrade output steadily over time.
  • Using high-pressure water jets: High-pressure water forces its way into panel frames, junction boxes, and connectors. Water seepage into these components can cause corrosion, short circuits, and even electrical fires.
  • Cleaning without switching the system off: Solar panels generate high-voltage DC power even on overcast days. Cleaning a live system without switching it off first is a serious safety risk. Contact with water and live components can cause electric shocks with severe consequences.
  • Treating bird droppings like regular dust: Bird droppings are acidic and concentrated. Unlike dust, they do not wash off easily and actively degrade the anti-reflective coating underneath. They also block concentrated clusters of cells, creating hotspots that damage panels from the inside.
  • Cleaning without proper safety gear: Panel cleaning without a safety harness, electrical hand gloves, and non-slip footwear is dangerous. A slip on a wet roof or accidental contact with a live connector can cause serious injury. Safety gear is not optional.
  • Only cleaning panels and skipping inspections: Cleaning panels removes dust but does not catch loose connections, corroded mounting structures, inverter faults, or hot spots. A panel that looks clean can still be underperforming due to issues that only a trained technician with the right equipment can detect.
