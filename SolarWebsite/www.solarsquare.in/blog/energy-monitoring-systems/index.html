What is a Solar Energy Monitoring System? Types, Working, Benefits, and Features Explained

A solar energy monitoring system is a device that combines hardware and software to track the performance of a solar system in near real time and detect critical issues that might be reducing the energy output from the solar panels. It tracks how much power your panels produce, how much your home or building uses, how much you send to the grid, and, if you have lithium batteries, how much is stored and available. 

These advanced solar energy monitoring devices also flag issues that can reduce your output, such as faulty solar panels, solar inverter errors, wiring problems, shading, or dust buildup. 

A good-quality solar panel monitoring system offers remote monitoring. You can check live and past data on a web dashboard or a simple phone app, get alerts when something looks wrong, and download reports for bills, maintenance, and ROI checks.

Here are a few ways these solar energy monitoring systems are set up:

  • Some watch each panel
  • Some watch each string or inverter
  • Some only watch the whole solar energy system

The monitoring can come built into the inverter, be provided by your installer, or run on a third-party device. No matter the type, most critical components of any solar energy monitoring system are sensors, data loggers, software, and a communication system. 

In this blog, you’ll explore what an energy monitoring system is, how it works, its benefits, types, and features. We will also give you a head-on comparison between on-grid and off-grid solar systems to help you understand which one you should choose (and why) when installing rooftop solar panel systems for homes, housing societies, and commercial buildings. 

TL;DR Summary Box: Why is Energy Monitoring Important?

You installed a rooftop solar system at home. But how do you know it’s giving you the power it should? Energy monitoring checks how your system is performing in near real time. Without it, you’re flying blind. You won’t know if panels are underperforming, if the inverter has an error, or if you’re losing money because of a hidden issue.

Here’s why solar energy monitoring makes so much sense:

  • It protects your investment from failures that would otherwise go unnoticed: Panels and inverters can underperform without any visible signs. Monitoring spots faults early and sends alerts before these issues can significantly impact your electricity bills.
  • It maximizes your energy savings: Energy monitoring helps you understand how much energy your panels produce and when they produce it most efficiently. You can use this knowledge to shift activities that consume more power, like running washing machines, water heaters, or air conditioners, to peak sunshine hours. This allows you to maximize the use of free solar renewable energy instead of expensive grid electricity. 
  • It helps you check your solar bill and net-metering credits: Net metering uses a bi-directional meter to record electricity import and export between the grid and your on-grid solar system. Energy monitoring helps you compare app data with your DISCOM credits so mistakes don’t slip through.
  • It maximizes your ROI: An energy monitoring system will ensure your system produces maximum solar electricity. And the more power your solar system will produce, the sooner its initial investment cost will break even. 
  • It detects maintenance needs before costly repairs: Solar energy monitoring identifies performance issues that indicate maintenance needs, such as panel cleaning, loose connections, or component aging. Addressing these issues early on through proper solar system maintenance costs much less than waiting for complete system failures. 

Please note: Energy monitoring itself doesn’t increase production. It helps you catch issues fast and use more of your solar at the right times, which is how it protects your savings.

Here are the main points covered in this blog in detail:

What is a Solar Energy Monitoring System?

Solar energy monitoring systems are tools that can track the performance of your solar system, detect power generation issues, and maximize your return on investment by offering real-time data analysis and sending automated alerts to your phone.

These systems collect real-time power generation data from solar panels, inverters, and smart energy meters. The collected data is then used to show energy production, how you use the produced energy, electricity import/export exchange between the grid and your system, and maintenance needs like solar panel cleaning.

What Are the Core Components of a Solar Power Monitoring System?

The solar energy monitoring system is a combination of physical sensors, cloud computing technology, data loggers, and a user-friendly interface (a web portal or an app) where customers can view the data collected by the system.  

Here are the main components that work in tandem in a solar power monitoring system:

  • Hardware sensors and meters: They are installed to measure electrical output from individual solar panels or entire arrays.
  • Data logger, often present inside the solar inverter itself: It collects readings, stores them for a while if the internet is down, and prepares them to send.
  • Communication devices: Their role is to transmit data wirelessly or through power line communication to central processors.
  • Cloud-based data processing platforms: They store the data and can also turn it into charts.
  • User interfaces: This could either be a mobile application or a web portal that customers can access for real-time and historical power generation data.
  • Alert systems: These are meant to notify users immediately when performance drops below expected thresholds.

How Does a Solar Energy Monitoring System Work?

Energy monitoring systems use different components to collect, transmit, process, and display the performance of a solar energy system to customers in near real-time. The monitoring process begins at the panel level, where sensors measure the DC output at the panel’s end. This data is further transmitted through communication devices and sent to a centralized processing system. 

A cloud-based platform receives final data. The platform performs complex calculations and presents the results in the form of simple bar graphs or charts on mobile applications and web interfaces.

Here’s how the energy monitoring systems work step-by-step:

  • Step 1 – Power generation data is collected by the sensors: The inverter/sensors read power output from your solar system. If fitted, smart meters also read the power consumption at your home and the unit exchange between your system and the grid. This can happen every few seconds or every few minutes.
  • Step 2 – Local processing follows next: Basic calculations and error checks are done inside the inverter or the monitoring box. Many devices keep a local copy of recent data.
  • Step 3 – The collected data is then transmitted: The energy monitoring system uses communication devices to send information to cloud servers via WiFi, cellular networks, or ethernet connections.
  • Step 4 – Cloud performs advanced data analysis: The data is processed to calculate performance metrics, identify trends, and generate alerts if the output is lower than it should be.
  • Step 5 – The final data is presented to the user: Results are displayed on mobile apps or web portals. They can also be sent to the user as automated email or text notifications.
  • Step 6 – The collected data is stored for historical usage: This is done in order to maintain long-term records for performance analysis and warranty documentation as the solar system gets older, year after year. 

Core Functions of a Solar Power Monitoring System

The primary function of a solar energy monitoring system is to continuously measure and track different parameters of the solar system, including voltage, current, power output, and energy production at different times throughout the day. 

These systems can also monitor environmental factors such as temperature, irradiance levels, and weather conditions like cloud cover that directly impact solar panel performance.

Here’s a snapshot of all the critical core functions that an energy monitoring system performs:

  • Tracks power output: It measures and records the solar electricity generated by the panels through the day and over time.
  • Records consumption pattern: It shows how much power was consumed in your home and how much was imported/exported between the grid and the system.
  • Notifies in case of faults: The system sends prompt alerts when output drops or a fault occurs.
  • Notifies when maintenance is needed: Since the system tracks the overall performance so closely, it’s able to highlight likely maintenance needs, like cleaning dusty panels or checking a loose connection, before the system’s output can drop significantly.

What Are the Benefits of an Energy Monitoring System?

Energy monitoring systems maximize your return on investment and protect your solar systems against panel or inverter malfunction by tracking the output of the solar system and flagging potential issues that might be reducing the power output in real time. Simply put, they identify issues before those issues can drop the panel generation to such an extent that will impact your bills, reducing your savings. 

Here are the main benefits/advantages a solar energy monitoring system offers:

  • It increases the energy generated by the solar panels: Although the energy monitoring system doesn’t do this directly, it does increase the output indirectly by identifying issues early on. Early identification helps remedying the issues before output drops. 
  • It reduces the risk of costly repairs: Since the system sends timely alerts when panels should be cleaned and inverters should be serviced, major faults that might have required complete replacement rarely happen. 
  • It can help optimize energy consumption at home: The data produced gives a record of when the solar system generates most power during the day. You can use that insight to perform all activities that require a lot of power during the maximum power generation time. This will reduce your reliance on grid electricity.
  • You can track your savings: These systems provide you with real power generation figures, which you can simply tally with the power generation that was promised at the time of system installation. 

What Are the Types of Solar Energy Monitoring Systems?

Energy monitoring systems can be categorized by data collection level and installation approach. Based on data collection level, there are three types of energy monitoring systems: panel-level, string-level, and system-level. Based on installation, the energy monitoring systems can be classified as equipment provided by the installer, equipment provided by the manufacturer, and third-party equipment. 

Types of Solar Energy Monitoring Systems by Data Collection Level

These are of three types: panel-level monitoring, string-level monitoring, and system-level monitoring. Panel-level monitoring provides detailed performance data for each individual solar panel in your solar system. The string-level system shows the output of each string. Lastly, the system-level monitoring provides comprehensive oversight of the entire solar array’s performance. 

Let’s check out all the types:

  • Panel-level monitoring: A solar panel monitoring system tracks and monitors the output from an individual solar panel. It shows production data for each panel separately and identifies exactly which panels are underperforming or have failed. It can also identify how shadows affect specific panels throughout the day and ensure every panel operates at peak efficiency levels.
  • String-level monitoring: An array monitoring system shows the output of each string, which is a small group of panels wired together. This system can quickly reveal if one whole string has dropped power output, which is a common fault that results in reduced power generation in solar PV systems.
  • System-level monitoring: A system-level energy monitoring device monitors and tracks overall array performance. It provides data regarding the energy production from the entire solar system, not individual panels or strings. It’s more suitable for rooftops where shading and orientation issues are minimal across all panels. 

Types of Solar Energy Monitoring Systems Based on Installation 

These are of three main types: equipment by the installer, equipment by the manufacturer, and third-party monitoring equipment. Let’s check out how these three types differ: 

  • Equipment by the installer: Some installers give you access through the manufacturer’s app and also use an installer view to watch your system for service. 
  • Equipment by the manufacturer: These are mainly solar inverters that have built-in monitoring capabilities. They can show total energy production, current power output, and system status without the requirement for any additional hardware. One example of these energy monitoring systems is power optimizers by SolarEdge. 
  • Third-party equipment or standalone home energy monitoring solutions: These are add-on boxes or home energy monitors from companies that don’t make your solar inverter. They are great for older solar systems and can add consumption and export tracking if your original app doesn’t show it.

Are Solar Energy Monitoring Systems and Solar Tracking Systems the Same?

Solar energy monitoring systems and solar tracking systems are not the same. Solar tracking systems, or solar trackers, are devices that physically move and adjust the solar panels so they remain perpendicular to the sun throughout the day. These are of two main types: a single-axis solar tracker and a dual-axis solar tracker. 

While their purpose is also to maximize the output from solar panels, solar tracking systems are completely different from energy monitoring systems.

FAQs

Q1. Is solar monitoring worth it?

Ans. Yes. It identifies issues with solar panels and inverters early, safeguards your savings, and allows you to verify that your bill and export credits are accurate.

Q2. Do solar monitoring systems work during a power outage?

Ans. If your Wi-Fi/router or inverter has no power, the app won’t update live readings. Many systems store data locally and sync it when power returns. However, with a battery backup, you might be able to see data on the local display.

Q3. Can I add monitoring to an existing solar system?

Ans. Usually, yes. You can add a small monitor or energy meter that works with your inverter. Ask your installer to check compatibility and set up the internet connection for the app.
