A solar generator is a portable system that stores electricity generated by solar panels in batteries for later use. The main internal components of solar electric generators are a solar charge controller, an inverter, and a battery pack. Solar panels, although mandatory for solar generators to work, are not a built-in part of the generator unit. 

Solar power generators are specifically designed to be used with off-grid applications. Since most of them are portable, they’re suitable for camping or hiking in off-grid locations. They’re also used for powering RVs, boats, and remote cabins. However, by default, they are not compatible with grid-tied solar systems, like rooftop on-grid solar systems, as they lack synchronization capabilities with the utility grid. Hence, solar generators are usually not recommended with on-grid rooftop solar panel systems for homes unless they’re configured properly. 

In this blog, we’ve chalked down the exact working of solar generators, their benefits and drawbacks, when you should invest in them, how they differ from gas generators, their cost, and things you should consider when buying solar power generators for home use.

What is a Solar Generator?

A solar generator is a portable power system that stores the electricity generated by external solar panels in an internal battery pack. It typically includes a solar panel charge controller, a battery pack (usually lithium batteries like lithium-ion or lithium iron phosphate), and an inverter that can convert DC battery power into usable AC electricity. Most models are compatible with different types of solar panels, though the panels themselves are sold separately. 

Solar electric generators operate silently, and they do not emit greenhouse gases or carbon. Hence, they’re eco-friendly. While their upfront investment cost can be higher than that of gas generators, they prove to be much more economical in the long run as they do not require frequent fuel replacement. 

How Does a Solar Power Generator Work?

Inside the generator unit, a solar charge controller regulates the DC electricity received from the solar panels. The controller directs the incoming electricity to the battery for safe and efficient charging. This prevents overcharging and ensures the battery receives optimal voltage and current levels.

Let’s check out how they work step-by-step:

  • Step 1 – External solar panels convert sunlight to electricity: Solar panels contain PV solar cells that are usually made of silicon, a semiconductor material. When photons in the sunlight hit the solar cells, they knock electrons in the silicon loose. These loose electrons start flowing in one direction through a closed circuit, which results in the production of direct current (DC) power. 
  • Step 2 – The DC power from the solar panels is sent to a solar charge controller: DC power from the panels is not always consistent. The panels can sometimes produce more voltage and sometimes less, depending on the sunlight and temperature throughout the day. If this unregulated DC electricity were to go straight to the batteries, it could damage them. Hence, a solar charge controller (usually an MPPT solar charge controller) ensures it always matches the panel’s output with the battery’s voltage. 
  • Step 3 – The regulated voltage from the solar charge controller is sent to the battery: Power coming from the solar charge controller is used to charge the in-built battery pack in the solar generator.    
  • Step 4 – Stored DC power is sent to the inverter for conversion: Most household appliances run on alternating current (AC), not direct current (DC). The inverter inside the solar generator converts stored DC electricity into AC. This AC power can then be used to power lights, fans, refrigerators, laptops, and other appliances.

Gas Generator vs Solar Generator, Which is Better and Why?

Solar generators are costlier than gas generators. However, they prove to be cost-effective in the longer run since, unlike gas generators that require frequent fuel replacement and maintenance charges, solar power generators run on solar renewable energy, which is literally free. Moreover, they’re noise-free, portable, and weigh less than gas generators. 

Whether it’s for the environment or long-term savings and durability, solar generators are better than gas generators. 

Let’s give you a head-on comparison between the two so you can choose the one that aligns with your requirements: 

What Are the Benefits of a Solar Electric Generator?

Solar power generators do not cause noise pollution, and they’re eco-friendly as well. Their operation cost is nil, and they require minimal maintenance. Plus, most models are compact and portable. Hence, you can also carry them to remote locations when hiking or camping in the mountains. 

Let’s check out all the benefits of solar generators in detail:

  • They operate silently: Solar electric generators, unlike gas generators, do not emit noise while they operate. Hence, they do not cause noise pollution. 
  • They’re eco-friendly: These generators run on solar energy, which is infinite and non-polluting. Hence, solar generators do not produce carbon emissions. 
  • They require minimal maintenance: Since they do not have oil or a fuel tank, they require very minimal maintenance. 
  • Zero operation cost: These generators work on sunlight, which costs nothing. 
  • Safe for indoor use: Since they don’t emit carbon monoxide or fumes, solar generators can be safely used indoors if needed.

What Are the Disadvantages of a Solar Generator?

Solar generators tend to have a higher upfront cost when compared to gas generators. It’s their biggest limitation. However, in the longer run, they prove to be more cost-effective because their operating cost is nil and they require minimal maintenance. 

Let’s check out all the drawbacks of solar power generators in detail:

  • Weather dependence: Since solar panels require sunlight to work, the performance of solar generators is weather dependent. 
  • Higher upfront cost: They are costlier to install than gas generators. But in the longer run, they prove to be more economical.
  • Slower response to sudden needs: If the battery is low or empty, you can’t instantly recharge it like refueling a gas generator.

When Should You Invest in a Solar Power Generator?

Solar power generators are cost-effective in the long run, and they’re great for the environment. However, they are not suitable for everyone. If you live in urban areas with a reliable grid, investing lakhs of rupees in solar generators to power your home might be unnecessary for you. 

Installing on-grid rooftop solar for housing societies and homes can meet all your energy needs without the need for solar power generators.  

You should go for a solar generator in the following situations: 

  • You live in remote off-grid locations: If your home, cabin, or workspace is located in a remote area where connecting to the power grid is not feasible or is super expensive.
  • You frequently camp or hike in mountains: In such cases, you can invest in buying cheaper models. They’re perfect for powering LED lights, portable fridges, drones, laptops, and other gear in remote locations where grid access is unavailable.
  • You live in rural areas that witness frequent power cuts: If your area suffers from regular blackouts or grid instability, a solar generator can be a reliable backup solution. It keeps essentials like lights, fans, routers, phones, and medical devices running without any fuel dependency. 
  • You prefer silent and clean power backup: Gas generators are noisy and release harmful emissions. If you want a zero-noise and zero-emission alternative, solar generators are the better choice.  
  • You already have solar panels installed: If you already own solar panels or solar water pumps, you can take professional help to integrate a solar generator to store excess power and use it as an additional backup during blackouts.

Do You Need Solar Generators in Urban Areas?

Solar generators are specifically designed for off-grid applications in remote or rural areas where a grid supply is either patchy or not available at all. If you live in an urban area with a reliable power grid, on-grid solar systems are enough to take care of your power requirements. You don’t actively need solar generators in such cases.

Factors to Consider When Buying a Portable Solar Power Generator?

Choosing the right solar electric generator requires careful consideration of factors such as your power requirements, battery capacity, brand reputation, and even the number of charging ports provided. 

Here’s a detailed list of seven factors that will never let you go wrong:

  • Consider how much power you need: Start by listing the devices you want to run. Do you want to charge just phones and lights or larger items like fans, heaters, or fridges? Smaller devices need less power, while appliances with motors or heating elements require a generator with higher output. This will directly impact your budget because solar generators with higher output are costlier than those with lower output. So, decide carefully how much power you truly need.
  • Check the output rating of the generator: The output of a solar generator is measured in watts (W) or watt-hours (Wh). Watts tell you how much power it can supply at once, and watt-hours tell you how long it can run your devices. The bigger the number, the more it can handle.
  • Look for generators that have expandable battery options: Some solar generators allow adding extra batteries to increase storage. If you’re unsure about future power needs or plan to run more devices later, choose a model that allows expansion.
  • Pick a trusted brand with a great reputation: Go for a manufacturer with a strong reputation and positive user reviews. Well-known brands usually offer better build quality, longer warranties, and reliable after-sales support.
  • Make sure the generator has multiple output ports: If you have multiple gadgets to charge, you’ll need a generator with various ports like USB, AC, and DC outlets. The more types and numbers of ports available, the more devices you can power at once.
  • Compare the charging speeds carefully before making a purchase: Some solar generators charge in a few hours, while others take nearly a day. If fast charging is important to you, look for features like MPPT charge controllers.
  • Choose the right size and weight: If you plan to carry your generator for outdoor use or emergencies, make sure it’s easy to lift and move. A compact, lightweight design is ideal for travel. On the other hand, a heavier solar generator is better suited for home use.

Conclusion

Solar generators are excellent portable options to keep the lights running and gadgets charged in remote and rural areas without a power grid. While their upfront installation cost is higher than traditional gas generators, they’re far more economical in the long run as their operating cost is almost nil. You must pay close attention to your power needs before making a purchase, as the higher the power requirement, the costlier the generator will be.

FAQs

Q1. Can a solar generator run a house?

Ans. To run an entire house, you'd need a high-capacity solar generator with expandable batteries and an inverter. On the other hand, small portable solar power generators can usually power small appliances.

Q2. How long will a solar generator battery last?

Ans. A fully charged solar generator can power devices for a few hours to several days. The actual hours depend on battery capacity and usage. The battery itself can last for up to 15 years based on its type and maintenance.

Q3. Is a solar generator safe?

Ans. Yes. Solar generators do not produce fumes, and they don’t overheat easily. They are much safer than gas generators for indoor use.

Q4. Can you charge a solar generator with electricity?

Ans. Yes, most generators manufactured by reputable brands can be charged via wall sockets in addition to solar panels. This allows you to recharge even when there’s no sunlight.
