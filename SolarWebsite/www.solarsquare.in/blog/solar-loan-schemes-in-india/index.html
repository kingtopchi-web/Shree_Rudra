With electricity bills rising by 3-6% every year in India, rooftop solar has become the go-to solution for homeowners tired of watching their expenses climb. To accelerate solar adoption, the central government launched the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana solar subsidy scheme in 2024, offering generous subsidies to make rooftop solar affordable. But even after the subsidy, the upfront installation cost was a challenge

That’s where solar loan schemes stepped in.

Under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, several Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) now offer dedicated solar loans. Additionally, many Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) also offer loans to homeowners installing rooftop solar. Their goal is to remove the upfront cost barrier so more homeowners can switch to solar.

  • PSUs are banks like SBI and PNB that offer solar loans at very low interest rates. Hence, consumers have to surrender the subsidy.
  • NBFCs are the faster alternatives that trade lower rates for speed, subsidy, and convenience.

PSU financing is government-subsidized and, in the case of SBI, financed by the World Bank. SBI and PNB solar loans of up to ₹ 2 lakh come at an interest rate of just 5.75% per annum. The loan approval process takes 15-30 days, the bank conducts two physical property visits, and registration on the Jan Samarth Portal is mandatory. And since the interest rate is very low, you have to surrender the subsidy once your loan is approved.

On the other hand, NBFCs focus on speed and convenience. The entire process is digital, loan approvals happen within 24 hours, there are no branch visits or physical inspections, and customers are not required to register on the Jan Samarth Portal. The trade-off is an interest rate of 12-13% per annum, but you get to retain the full subsidy amount.

So, which option makes more financial sense for you? Is solar financing actually worth it for homeowners in India? This blog answers all such questions.

Read on to discover the key aspects of solar loan schemes in India, eligibility criteria, documents needed to receive a solar rooftop loan, benefits and challenges of solar financing, and the cost of installing rooftop solar systems in India.

TL;DR Summary Box: Is PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana Scheme a Loan?

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, launched in 2026, is a solar subsidy scheme, not a loan. Under this scheme, the government provides financial assistance to homeowners who install rooftop solar systems

However, even after the subsidy, many households still found the initial investment difficult to manage. To address this gap, the government expanded the ecosystem by bringing in PSUs such as SBI and PNB, with SBI securing financial backing from the World Bank.

These banks offer solar loans at 5.75% per annum for loans up to Rs 2 lakh. However, if you opt for a solar loan scheme through PSUs, the subsidy is adjusted since the loan is offered at a highly reduced interest rate.

Homeowners also have another financing option.

Non-Banking Financial Companies, or NBFCs, offer solar loans at an interest rate of 12-13% per annum through a fully digital process. Approvals can happen within 24 hours, documentation is minimal, and there are no branch visits. The biggest advantage is that you get to retain the full subsidy amount.

So, while the PM Surya Ghar scheme itself is not a loan, it works alongside multiple financing options that help homeowners install rooftop solar without paying the full amount upfront.

What is a Solar Loan Scheme?

A solar loan scheme is a financing option that allows homeowners to install a rooftop solar energy system without paying the entire cost upfront.

Instead of investing the full amount at once, you pay a small down payment and convert the remaining cost into affordable monthly EMIs. These loans are designed specifically for residential rooftop solar systems for homes and are often linked to the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana ecosystem.

In India, solar loans are offered by:

  • Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) banks such as SBI and PNB.
  • Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) that provide faster, digital approvals.

The objective is to remove the upfront cost barrier and accelerate rooftop solar adoption.

Different solar loan schemes come with specific interest rates, tenure limits, eligibility rules, and processing timelines.

Let’s break down their key aspects in detail.

Which are the Best Solar Loan Schemes for Residential Installations?

Residential solar loan schemes in India broadly fall into two categories: PSU loans with low interest rates but a longer approval period, and NBFC loans focused on speed and convenience

  • NBFC solar loan schemes: Offered by private financial institutions, these loans focus on speed and digital convenience. Approvals can happen within 24 hours, documentation is minimal, and the entire journey is online. Interest rates are slightly higher than PSUs, but you retain the full subsidy.
  • PSU solar loan schemes: Offered by public sector banks such as SBI and PNB under the PM Surya Ghar subsidy scheme, these loans have low interest rates starting at 5.75% per annum. However, once the loan is approved, you have to surrender the subsidy.

What are the Benefits of Solar Loan Schemes in India?

Solar loan schemes make rooftop solar financially accessible without requiring homeowners to set aside large upfront savings. Instead of paying the entire system cost at once, you spread it into manageable EMIs while continuing to benefit from lower electricity bills. The result is a smoother transition to solar with reduced financial strain.

First, let’s check out the benefits that apply to both PSU and NBFC solar loans:

  • EMIs offset electricity bills: In many cases, the monthly EMI is close to or lower than your pre-solar electricity bill. This means your cash outflow does not increase after installation.
  • Affordable interest rates: PSU loans offer concessional rates starting from 5.75% per annum, while NBFC loans remain structured and allow homeowners to retain the entire subsidy amount.
  • No foreclosure charges: Most solar loan schemes allow early repayment without penalties, helping you reduce total interest cost if you choose to close the loan early.

In addition to the benefits above, NBFC solar loans have the following advantages:

  • Approval within 24 hours: Faster loan sanction helps speed up installation timelines.
  • No branch visits required: The entire loan journey is digital, eliminating paperwork-heavy bank interactions.
  • The loan can be taken in a third-party name: This adds flexibility in cases where the primary property owner prefers another family member to take the loan.
  • Faster breakeven period: With EMIs aligned to savings and quicker installation, many homeowners achieve system breakeven within 3-5 years.

Are There Any Challenges to Securing PSU Solar Loan Schemes? 

PSU solar loans offer some of the lowest interest rates in the market. However, the process is compliance-driven, which can introduce operational delays and additional steps for borrowers.

Here are the common challenges homeowners may face:

  • Portal glitches and errors: Applications must be routed through the Jan Samarth portal. Technical errors, document mismatches, or portal slowdowns can delay approvals.
  • Longer processing timelines: Loan approval can take 15-30 days in standard cases and may extend further if inspections, documentation, or portal approvals are delayed.
  • Multiple branch visits: Borrowers are required to visit the bank branch at least twice (once for KYC verification and again during the sanction or disbursal stages).
  • Non-digital workflow: Unlike NBFC loans, PSU loans involve physical inspections, manual verification, and in-person documentation, which can slow down execution.
  • Subsidy adjustment requirement: Since PSU loans are offered at concessional interest rates, the subsidy component is adjusted within the loan structure rather than retained by the homeowner.

Factors to Keep in Mind When Choosing a Bank for a Solar Loan

Not all solar loan schemes are created equal. While the interest rate is important, the overall experience, from approval speed to documentation burden, plays a major role in how smoothly your rooftop solar installation process moves forward.

Here are the key factors to evaluate before choosing a solar loan scheme:

  • Approval speed: Faster loan sanction means quicker installation. NBFC loans can be approved within 24 hours, while PSU loans may take 15-30 days or longer.
  • Digital vs physical process: A fully digital journey reduces paperwork, branch visits, and delays. PSU loans typically require portal filings and in-person bank visits, whereas NBFC loans are usually processed online.
  • Ease of documentation: Check the documentation requirements upfront. Loans above certain brackets in PSU banks may require income proofs and bank statements.
  • Processing transparency: Choose lenders that clearly communicate timelines, inspection requirements, disbursement stages, and EMI schedules to avoid surprises during installation.

FAQs

Who is eligible for a solar loan in SBI?

To be eligible for the SBI solar loan scheme, the applicant must:

  • Be within the prescribed age limit. The loan should close before the borrower turns 70. If applying with a co-borrower, the loan must close before the co-borrower turns 75.
  • Own the residential property and the rooftop where the solar system will be installed.
  • Apply through the Jan Samarth Portal.

For how many years can a solar loan tenure be?

The maximum solar loan tenure for PSU banks can be 10 years.

What is a good interest rate for a solar loan?

For PSU banks, 5.75% per annum (up to ₹ 2 lakh) is considered a strong interest rate. NBFC loans generally range between 12% and 13% per annum, offering faster approval and greater convenience.

Can I pay off my solar loan early?

Yes. Most solar loan schemes, including SBI, PNB, and NBFC solar loans, do not charge foreclosure or prepayment penalties.

How long does it take to receive a solar loan in India?

For PSU banks like SBI and PNB, loan approval takes 7-15 working days in the fastest cases, provided all documents and feasibility approvals are ready. However, delays are common, and the process can extend to 1-3 months due to branch processing, portal issues, or inspections.

Once sanctioned, disbursement happens within a few days to 1-2 weeks after vendor agreements, inspections, and branch formalities are completed. Most PSU banks release part payment before installation and the balance after commissioning.

NBFC solar loans, in contrast, are approved within 24 hours since the process is fully digital.
