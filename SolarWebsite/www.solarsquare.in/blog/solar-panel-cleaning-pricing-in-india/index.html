Breakdown of DIY Solar PV Cleaning (Panel Cleaning on Your Own)

Cleaning your own solar panels might seem straightforward. Most homeowners think it only needs a bucket, some water, household cleaning agents like dishwashing gel or soap, and an hour on the roof. But what goes unnoticed is the real cost of taking this task into your own hands.

To clean solar panels correctly, you need a long-handle soft-bristle brush, a low-mineral or RO water supply, a non-abrasive cleaning solution, safety gear and equipment, and a stable ladder.

Sourcing all of this properly will cost around ~Rs. 15,000 before you have cleaned a single panel. And that cost repeats every time something wears out or breaks.

Even with the right equipment, the risk does not go away. Solar panels generate high-voltage DC power even on cloudy days. There is no switch that makes them safe to touch.

Without a safety harness, electrical hand gloves, and proper roof footwear, you are one slip or accidental contact away from a serious injury.

Here’s the hidden cost of DIY (on your own) solar PV cleaning that nobody warns you about:

  • Threat to life: High-voltage DC on a slippery rooftop without safety gear is a life-threatening combination. Unlike AC power, DC does not let go once contact is made.
  • Coating and glass damage: Household items like vinegar, baking soda, or rough cloth permanently strip the anti-reflective coating. Once the coating is gone, any type of solar panel will lose output.
  • Mounting structure damage: DIY (on your own) inspections rarely catch early rust on mounting structures. Ignored long enough, corroded mounting hardware compromises the structural integrity of your entire installation.
  • Fire risk: Chipped or damaged wiring that goes unnoticed during a DIY (on your own) clean can cause short circuits and electrical fires. A professional technician spots and fixes such issues on the spot.
  • Voided warranty: Manufacturers are specific about how panels must be cleaned and by who. One session done wrong is enough to void your warranty and leave you with no cover on a system you spent lakhs on.

In reality, DIY panel cleaning on your own does not save money. It just moves the cost further down the line, and safety of the family as well as the system gets compromised.

Signs that Your Modules Need Professional Solar Panel Cleaning Services

Many homeowners who wait to call professionals until something stops working completely suffer major financial losses. Many times, the damage is irreversible. Just so it does not happen to you, if you notice any of the signs listed below, that’s a clear indication that your system needs professional cleaning and maintenance:

  • Visible drop in units generated: If your system is generating fewer units than usual and the weather has not changed, dirty panels are the most likely cause.
  • Dust and bird droppings visible on panels: If you can see a layer of dust or bird droppings on the panels from the ground, your panels are already losing output. Bird droppings are particularly damaging because they block concentrated areas of cells and create hotspots.
  • Inverter showing error codes: Error codes on your solar inverter indicate that something is wrong with your system. Ignored long enough, these faults compound and cause sustained output loss.
  • Panels not cleaned in over three months: Even if nothing looks wrong, three months of dust, pollution, and bird activity is enough to cause a measurable 10-18% drop in generation.
  • Discolouration or dark patches on panels: Brown or dark patches on your panels are a sign of hotspots or cell degradation. This is not a cleaning issue alone, and requires immediate professional inspection to assess the damage and prevent it from spreading.
  • System running hotter than usual: Panels that run consistently hot are a sign of hotspots, poor ventilation, or dust buildup trapping heat. Left unchecked, excess heat degrades cells more quickly, shortening the lifespan of your panels.

How Often Should You Clean Your Solar Panels?

The frequency of cleaning solar PV panels depends entirely on how dusty and polluted your city is. Naturally, cities with high levels of pollution require more frequent panel cleaning than those with lower levels.

  • Recommended panel cleaning in cities with moderate pollution: Once a month
  • Recommended panel cleaning in cities with low levels of pollution: Once every 45 days or once every month

Solar Panel Cleaning Mistakes You Must Avoid For Module Longevity

The most common solar panel cleaning mistake that can ruin the anti-reflective coating is using vinegar or other household cleaners, such as baking soda, to clean the surface. Any sort of DIY (on your own) panel cleaning method can also void the manufacturer’s warranty.

Here’s a breakdown of all the solar panel cleaning mistakes you should steer clear of to ensure your panels generate maximum possible power for more than 25 years:

  • Using vinegar, baking soda, or household cleaning agents: These seem harmless, but are chemically aggressive on solar panel surfaces. They strip the anti-reflective coating that helps panels absorb maximum solar radiation. Once the coating is damaged, the output loss is permanent.
  • Using abrasive cloth, scrubbers, or rough brushes: Steel wool, rough sponges, or any abrasive material scratches the glass surface of your panels. Scratches scatter incoming light, preventing it from reaching the solar cells and reducing generation over time.
  • Cleaning panels in the middle of the day: Panels get extremely hot under direct midday sun. Splashing cold water on a hot panel causes thermal shock, leading to microcracks within the cells. These cracks are invisible to the naked eye but degrade output steadily over time.
  • Using high-pressure water jets: High-pressure water forces its way into panel frames, junction boxes, and connectors. Water seepage into these components can cause corrosion, short circuits, and even electrical fires.
  • Cleaning without switching the system off: Solar panels generate high-voltage DC power even on overcast days. Cleaning a live system without switching it off first is a serious safety risk. Contact with water and live components can cause electric shocks with severe consequences.
  • Treating bird droppings like regular dust: Bird droppings are acidic and concentrated. Unlike dust, they do not wash off easily and actively degrade the anti-reflective coating underneath. They also block concentrated clusters of cells, creating hotspots that damage panels from the inside.
  • Cleaning without proper safety gear: Panel cleaning without a safety harness, electrical hand gloves, and non-slip footwear is dangerous. A slip on a wet roof or accidental contact with a live connector can cause serious injury. Safety gear is not optional.
  • Only cleaning panels and skipping inspections: Cleaning panels removes dust but does not catch loose connections, corroded mounting structures, inverter faults, or hot spots. A panel that looks clean can still be underperforming due to issues that only a trained technician with the right equipment can detect.

FAQs

Can rain clean solar panels properly?

Rain can wash away light dust, but it cannot clean solar panels properly. Bird droppings, stubborn dust deposits, and pollution residue do not wash off with rain. In fact, as rainwater evaporates, it can leave mineral deposits on the panel surface, reducing light transmission over time.

What is the cheapest way to clean solar panels?

Professional cleaning is the cheapest and also the best solar panel cleaning method when you look at the full picture. DIY panel cleaning on your own costs ~Rs. 15,000 in equipment and safety gear before you clean a single panel. It also puts your warranty at risk and does nothing to detect inverter faults, hotspots, or damage to the mounting structure. On the other hand, a professional 1-year 4-visit cleaning package from Assure by SolarSquare starts at ~Rs. 1,800, translating to ~Rs. 450* per visit for systems up to 3.25 kWp.

*Please note: The solar panel cleaning pricing mentioned above is indicative and based on SolarSquare Assure’s pricing at the time of publishing. It’s specific to the 1-year package plan with 4 visits for small systems up to 3.25 kWp. Prices are subject to change without prior notice and may vary based on your location, system size, site conditions, and the package you choose.

Can I use vinegar to clean solar panels?

No. Vinegar is acidic and permanently strips the anti-reflective coating on solar panels. Once that coating is damaged, your panels lose output that no future cleaning can recover. Baking soda, dishwashing gel, and other household cleaning agents cause the same damage.

Which chemical is used for solar panel cleaning?

Professional solar panel cleaning does not rely on chemicals for routine cleaning. Trained technicians use low-mineral or RO water with a soft-bristle brush for regular dust removal. For stubborn stains that do not wash away with water, special non-abrasive cleaning solutions are used that do not damage the anti-reflective coating.

Does cleaning void the warranty?

DIY cleaning on your own done the wrong way can void your warranty, not professional panel cleaning. Manufacturers specify approved cleaning methods, equipment, and who must carry out the cleaning. Using household chemicals, abrasive tools, or high-pressure water jets are common reasons warranty claims get rejected.
