A tier-1 solar system is designed to generate electricity safely and reliably for 25 years. However, certain faults can appear suddenly and require immediate attention. For example, situations like smoke from components, exposed wiring, or electrical arcing indicate serious faults that threaten safety, property, and the solar installation itself. In such cases, an immediate professional solar repair is required.

Emergency solar repair service providers usually charge a service visitation fee of Rs. 800-1,500 per visit. This fee covers only the technician’s emergency inspection. The actual final solar repair cost depends on the damaged component and the extent of damage. Since the root cause is unknown until inspection is over, the final cost of solar system repair cannot be predicted in advance.

Certain warning signs should always be treated as solar emergencies. If you notice any of the following, your rooftop solar system most likely needs urgent solar repair services:

  • Burning smell, smoke, heating, or electrical arcing from solar components
  • Physical damage to panels or mounting structure after storms
  • Loose, exposed, or damaged wiring around the solar system
  • Inverter breakdown, overheating, or repeated tripping
  • Fire near or around the solar installation
  • Sudden failure of critical solar components

When a serious fault appears, safety should always come first. If you suspect an electrical hazard, the safest step is to cut off the main power supply through the home’s MCB and keep people away from the system. In case of a visible fire, never pour water on solar electrical equipment; call the fire brigade instead.

Homeowners can feel tempted to fix solar panels themselves or call a nearby electrician. However, it can make the situation worse. All types of solar panels generate high-voltage DC electricity and require specialized technical knowledge. Handling the system without proper training can lead to electrical shock, equipment damage, or fire hazards.

That is why contacting a professional solar repair company, such as Assure by SolarSquare, to fix solar panels is always the safest option during an emergency. Trained solar technicians understand system design, inverter diagnostics, and electrical safety protocols, ensuring their safety and the safety of the property and equipment.

What Situations Qualify as Emergencies that Need Urgent Solar Repair?

A solar emergency is any situation in which the PV system poses a risk of fire, electrical shock, or structural damage. In simple terms, if the problem threatens human safety or could damage property, it should be treated as an emergency requiring immediate solar repair service.

Issues like electrical arcing, overheating components, exposed wiring, or fire near the system can turn dangerous within minutes. These are situations where delaying professional solar repair can increase the risk of injury, fire, or severe equipment damage.

Emergency vs Non-Emergency Situations: How to Tell the Difference?

Not every solar system issue requires immediate intervention. Some problems affect system performance but do not pose a safety risk. Others can lead to electrical hazards, fire, or severe equipment damage if ignored. Knowing how to distinguish between these situations helps you decide when to call an emergency solar repair company and when a routine solar system repair appointment is sufficient.

The table below highlights the key differences between emergency and non-emergency solar issues.

When in doubt, it is always safer to consult a professional solar repair company. A quick solar inspection can determine whether the issue is a genuine emergency or a routine maintenance matter.

Why Acting Quickly Can Prevent Fire and Electrical Hazards?

Rooftop solar energy systems generate solar electricity in DC form throughout the day. When a fault develops, the system can continue carrying high-voltage current even if the inverter shuts down. This means issues like damaged wiring, overheating components, or electrical arcing can lead to electrocution if mismanaged.

Calling a solar repair service immediately helps contain the problem before it turns into a fire or major equipment failure.

Acting quickly during a solar emergency can prevent several serious risks:

  • Stops electrical arcing early: Arcing occurs when electricity jumps between loose or damaged connections. This can generate intense heat and set nearby materials on fire.
  • Prevents fire hazards: Overheated cables, faulty connectors, or damaged components can trigger fires if the system continues operating.
  • Reduces damage to expensive components: Early solar repair can protect inverters, wiring, and other critical equipment from failures.
  • Protects your property: Faults in rooftop systems can spread heat to roof materials, increasing the risk of structural damage.
  • Improves safety for occupants: Immediate action reduces the chance of electric shock or injury to anyone near the system.
  • Limits downtime of the solar plant: Quick diagnosis and solar system repair can restore operation faster. Which means you won’t lose on generation and solar savings in the long term.

Top 7 Solar Emergencies That Require Immediate Solar Repair Service

Most solar systems operate safely for decades, but certain faults require urgent attention because they create fire, electrical, or structural risks. Recognizing warning signs early can prevent serious damage to your solar plant, property, and the people around it.

If any of the following situations occur, it is important to contact a professional solar repair company immediately instead of attempting a quick solar panel fix yourself.

#1. Burning Smell/ Heating/ Arcing/ Smoke from Solar Components

A burning smell or visible smoke from any part of the solar system is one of the most serious warning signs. They usually indicate overheating, electrical arcing, or component failure inside the system.

Since solar installations carry high-voltage DC electricity, such faults can increase the risk of fire. If you notice heat, smoke, or sparking from panels, cables, connectors, or the inverter area, shut down the main power supply immediately and contact a professional solar repair service.

Here are the common reasons behind these issues:

  • Loose or poorly connected cables that create heat buildup
  • Damaged MC4 connectors or junction boxes causing arcing
  • Overheating inverter components due to internal faults or poor ventilation
  • Melted insulation on solar wiring from prolonged exposure to heat
  • Short circuits within modules or electrical connections
  • Faulty installation or poor-quality connectors that fail over time

#2. Physical Damage to Structure or Modules

Physical damage to solar panels or the mounting structure is another situation that requires immediate solar repair. Solar systems are designed to withstand outdoor conditions, but extreme weather events can still damage panels, frames, or mounting structures.

Here are the common causes of structural or module damage:

  • Storm damage that loosens mounting structures or shifts panel alignment
  • Hail impact that cracks or shatters the solar panel glass surface
  • Strong winds that dislodge panels or weaken the mounting system
  • Falling debris, such as tree branches, hitting the solar modules
  • Roof structure movement or damage that affects panel stability
  • Visible cracks or broken glass on solar panels

If panels appear cracked, misaligned, or physically unstable, avoid touching the system and call a professional solar repair company immediately.

#3. Wiring-related Issues

Solar systems rely on a network of DC and AC wiring that connects panels, inverters, and safety components. If any part of this wiring becomes damaged, loose, or exposed, it can create serious electrical hazards.

Faulty wiring can lead to short circuits, system shutdowns, overheating, or electrical shocks. In such cases, immediate solar repair service is required.

Here are some common wiring-related emergencies:

  • Exposed or damaged cables around the solar panels or inverter
  • Loose electrical connections that cause sparking or overheating
  • Chewed wires caused by rodents or birds
  • Burnt or melted insulation on solar cables
  • Disconnected grounding wires that affect electrical safety
  • Visible sparks caused by wiring faults

Do not try to repair solar components yourself if any of these issues occur. Only trained professionals have the gear and knowledge to safely inspect the wiring, identify the fault, and carry out the required solar system repair without posing a risk to the equipment or human life.

#4. Inverter Issues/Breakdown/Tripping

A solar inverter is the central component that converts DC electricity into usable AC power for your home. When the inverter starts malfunctioning, the entire PV system can stop producing electricity. Frequent shutdowns, error codes, overheating, or repeated breaker trips are strong signs that the system needs an immediate solar repair.

In many cases, inverters display error codes indicating internal issues, grid faults, or communication failures. Only trained technicians can understand what these codes mean and determine the exact cause before performing the required solar system repair.

Here are the most common inverter-related emergency signs you must not ignore:

  • Inverter displaying error codes or warning alerts
  • Complete inverter shutdown with no power output
  • Repeated tripping of circuit breakers connected to the solar system
  • Inverter overheating or producing unusual heat
  • Flashing red warning lights on the inverter display
  • Unusual noises coming from the inverter unit

If your inverter stops functioning or repeatedly trips, avoid attempting a quick solar panel fix yourself. A professional solar repair company should inspect the system safely to identify whether the issue lies in the inverter, wiring, or grid connection.

#5. Fire Near or Around the Solar System

Any fire near a solar installation should be treated as a serious emergency. Solar panels and their associated wiring carry DC during daylight hours. It increases the danger tenfold during a fire.

Heat, flames, or smoke around the system can damage electrical components and create a high risk of short circuits or electrical shock. In such cases, immediate solar repair and professional inspection are essential.

Here are the situations that require urgent solar repair:

  • Fire on the roof near solar panels
  • Electrical fire near the inverter or distribution board
  • Fire caused by nearby appliances or wiring spreading toward the solar system
  • Burnt solar connectors or cables after a fire incident
  • Visible heat damage to panels, mounting structures, or electrical components

If a fire occurs near the solar installation, prioritize safety first. Switch off the main power supply if it is safe to do so and evacuate the area. Never attempt to extinguish an electrical fire with water; instead, call a fire brigade immediately.

After the fire brigade gets the situation under control, a professional solar repair company should inspect the system before it is restarted.

#6. Solar Component Failure

Solar systems consist of several critical components, including panels, inverters, junction boxes, connectors, isolators, and protection devices. If any of these components fail suddenly, the system can stop functioning or develop electrical risks.

Component failure almost always requires immediate solar repair service, especially if it affects electrical stability or safety.

Here are some of the most common solar component failures that need professional intervention:

  • Failure of DC isolators or protection switches
  • Burnt connectors or junction boxes
  • Damaged surge protection devices
  • Faulty combiner boxes affecting panel connections
  • Internal failure of inverter components
  • Breakdown of safety components that protect the system from electrical faults

When a critical component stops working, the system should not be restarted until a qualified solar repair company inspects it. Operating a solar plant with failed components can increase the risk of electrical hazards and further equipment damage.

#7. Monitoring Failure If the Plant Goes Offline

Solar monitoring systems track how much electricity your plant is producing and whether the system is operating normally. If the monitoring platform suddenly shows the entire plant offline, it indicates a technical issue that requires inspection.

Sometimes the issue may only be a monitoring or internet connection error. But if the system stops showing data and power generation also drops, it may mean the system needs immediate solar repair service.

Here are some situations that indicate a monitoring-related emergency:

  • The monitoring platform shows the plant is completely offline
  • No energy generation data appears for an extended period
  • Inverter status showing faults while monitoring is inactive
  • Sudden drop to zero generation during normal sunlight hours
  • Communication devices connected to the inverter fail to transmit data

If the plant appears offline, the issue is not related to internet connectivity, and the grid is operational, a professional solar repair company should inspect the system.

What to do Before a Solar Repair Company Arrives?

When a solar emergency occurs, the first few actions you take can significantly reduce risk. Before a solar repair company arrives, the priority should always be safety for everyone in the home and preventing further damage to the system.

Taking these few careful steps can help stabilize the situation until professional solar repair technicians begin inspection and troubleshooting.

  • Switch off the electrical supply through the distribution box
  • Turn off/shut down the solar system properly

How to Safely Shut Down Your Solar System?

If you suspect a serious issue, such as smoke, sparks, or overheating, shutting down the system can help limit further risk.

Follow these basic steps:

  • Turn off the home’s main power supply (MCB) from the distribution board if it is safe to access.
  • Keep people away from the solar installation area, especially the roof, inverter zone, or electrical panels.
  • Empty the immediate premises if there are signs of smoke, fire, or electrical sparking.
  • Avoid touching any solar components, cables, or panels during the emergency.
  • Call a fire brigade immediately if you spot a fire.

Safety Precautions to Avoid Injury

Electrical components in a solar system can remain active even during system faults. Simple precautions can reduce the chance of injury before technicians arrive.

Important safety measures include:

  • Do not use water to extinguish fires near solar equipment because water conducts electricity.
  • Maintain distance from panels, cables, and the inverter area during the emergency.
  • Avoid climbing onto the roof to inspect damaged panels or wiring.
  • Call a fire brigade to extinguish the fire.
  • Wait for trained technicians who are equipped to handle high-voltage solar systems.

These precautions protect both people and property while the solar repair service team prepares to inspect the system.

What Not to Do During a Solar Emergency?

Certain actions can worsen the situation or create additional safety risks. During a solar emergency, avoid the following:

  • Do not touch any solar components with your bare hands, especially cables, connectors, or panels.
  • Do not attempt DIY solar fixing on your own or quick repairs without proper training.
  • Do not call local electricians who are not trained in solar systems, since solar installations require specialized expertise.
  • Do not restart the solar system until a qualified solar repair company has inspected it.

What Makes DIY Solar Panel Fixes (Fixing Panels on Your Own) Dangerous?

Fixing solar panels and other solar components yourself might seem cost-effective at first. However, the hidden cost could be human life. Solar panels and many other solar components carry DC electricity, which can be lethal. Besides, rooftop solar work is done at height. Without proper equipment and a safety harness, you could fall and seriously injure yourself.

Here’s why attempting DIY solar panel fixes on your own is extremely dangerous:

  • High-voltage DC risks most homeowners don’t realize: Solar panels generate direct current (DC) electricity whenever sunlight hits them. DC power continues flowing even if the inverter shuts down. Touching exposed wires or connectors can lead to severe electric shock, electrical burns, and even loss of life.
  • Roof safety and fall hazards: Solar panels are installed on rooftops. Inspecting damaged modules or wiring without proper safety equipment increases the risk of slipping or falling. Even experienced technicians use harnesses and specialized tools to work safely at height.
  • Warranty and insurance risks: Many solar panel manufacturers require trained professionals to perform maintenance and repairs. Attempting DIY solar fixing on your own can void warranties and may affect insurance claims if further damage occurs.
  • Loss of property and life: Improper handling of solar components can cause short circuits, electrical fires, or major system failures. A mistake during DIY solar system repair on your own can put both property and lives at risk.

How Professionals Handle Emergency Solar Repair?

Professional solar repair is safe, and there’s peace of mind that the technicians working on a faulty system pose no risk to the equipment, as well as the safety of your family. A trained technician first identifies the exact problem, confirms the cause, and ensures the fault does not occur again.

Here’s how professionals repair solar issues with safety being the priority always:

  • Troubleshooting to identify the exact issue: The first step is to determine what has gone wrong. Technicians inspect the inverter, panels, wiring, and protection devices to locate the fault. For example, if an inverter displays an error code, a trained solar technician can interpret it and determine the type of fault it represents.
  • Diagnostics and testing to confirm the cause: Once the initial issue is identified, professionals perform technical tests to confirm the source of the fault. For instance, different inverter error codes can have different causes. Technicians analyze system data, test electrical connections, and inspect components to determine which specific issue is responsible. If required, they may use advanced diagnostic tools, such as thermal imaging and an IV curve tracer, to detect issues.
  • Root cause analysis (RCA) to understand why the issue occurred: After finding the immediate problem, technicians investigate the root cause of the failure. Root cause analysis helps determine why the fault occurred in the first place. It may reveal issues such as installation errors, component degradation, environmental damage, or poor electrical connections. Identifying the underlying cause reduces the need for emergency solar repair in the future.

The cost of solar repair cannot be estimated in advance because the exact fault and the extent of the damage become clear only after technicians inspect the system.

For example, a minor issue, such as a loose connection or bolt, may be resolved quickly and at low cost. However, major component failures involving the inverter or solar panels can lead to higher costs, depending on whether the component needs repair or replacement.

The technician’s visit charge can range between Rs. 800* to Rs. 1,500* for a normal inspection to diagnose the problem.

Why Are Emergency Solar Repair Services More Expensive?

Emergency solar repair costs more than scheduled maintenance because it requires immediate attention, specialized expertise and tools, and rapid troubleshooting.

Here’s why emergency solar repair tends to be more expensive:

  • Immediate response and priority scheduling: Emergency requests are handled on priority. Technicians have to adjust existing schedules or respond quickly to urgent calls, which increases service costs.
  • Specialized technical expertise: Solar systems involve high-voltage DC electricity, inverters, and complex wiring networks. Repairing faults safely requires trained solar professionals rather than general electricians.
  • Advanced diagnostic tools and inspection: Identifying the exact cause of a solar fault may sometimes require specialized testing equipment, such as thermal imaging and an IV curve tracer.
  • Risk and safety considerations: Emergency repairs involve dealing with electrical hazards and damaged components. Technicians follow strict safety protocols and use proper protective equipment.
  • Component replacement and availability: In some cases, emergency repairs involve replacing damaged parts such as connectors, isolators, or inverter components. The cost depends on the component type and its availability.

Factors That Affect Solar Repair Pricing

The cost of solar repair can vary depending on the type of issue and the work required to resolve it. After the initial inspection, technicians assess several factors before providing the final repair estimate.

Here are the main factors that affect the final solar repair cost in India.

  • Cost of components: Replacement of parts like connectors, wiring, inverters, or panels can significantly affect repair costs.
  • Experience and expertise of the technician: Qualified solar professionals bring specialized training and experience in handling high-voltage solar systems. Skilled technicians can accurately diagnose problems and carry out safe solar fixing, which is reflected in service pricing.
  • Need for advanced diagnostics: Some faults, such as hotspots on solar panels, are difficult to detect during a basic inspection because they can’t be seen with the naked eye. In such cases, technicians need advanced diagnostic thermal imaging to locate hidden faults or overheating components.
  • Number of visits required: If parts need to be ordered or additional testing is required, technicians need to return to complete the solar repair, which increases the overall cost.

How to Choose the Right Solar Repair Company in an Emergency?

When a solar emergency occurs, choosing the right solar repair company is critical for safety and quick resolution. Hiring an unqualified technician can worsen the issue or create additional safety risks.

Before selecting a provider for solar repair services, it helps to check these key factors:

  • Licensing and certification: Ensure the company employs trained solar technicians who can perform emergency inspections and solar system repair safely.
  • Emergency availability and response time: A reliable provider will offer quick response for emergency calls and be able to inspect the system within a short timeframe.
  • Reviews and local reputation: Check customer reviews and ratings to understand the company’s service quality and reliability.
  • Trained workforce: Confirm that the technicians have proper solar training and experience, rather than general electrical repair experience. Solar systems require specialized technical knowledge.

FAQs

Can a damaged solar panel be repaired?

No, a damaged solar panel cannot be repaired. If the glass surface cracks or internal cells burn due to hotspots, the entire panel has to be replaced. Attempting to fix solar panels in such cases can pose electrical risks and reduce system safety.

How fast should an emergency solar repair service respond?

Response time depends on the type of emergency. If there’s structural damage due to storms and cyclones, the ideal response time from a good solar repair company is 24 hours. In case of a fire in or around the solar system, call the fire brigade first. Once the fire is contained and you’ve shut down the main power supply to your home, schedule an emergency solar repair promptly.

Are solar repair services offered during weekends or at night?

Good solar repair companies will always offer emergency solar repair services at weekends. However, nighttime repair work is not performed because solar systems cannot be properly inspected in the dark.

Is inverter failure an emergency?

Yes, inverter failure should be treated as a solar emergency. The inverter is the central component that converts DC power from solar panels into AC power. If the inverter fails, overheats, or repeatedly trips, the entire solar system may shut down. A professional solar repair technician should inspect the system immediately to determine whether the issue lies with the inverter, wiring, or the grid connection.

Can solar panels catch fire?

Yes, solar panels can catch fire due to short circuits, damaged modules, faulty connectors, or component failure. Electrical arcing, overheated wiring, or poor installation also increases fire risk. If you notice smoke, a burning smell, or sparking near the solar system, switch off the main power supply immediately. If there’s an active fire, call the fire brigade and never pour water on it.
