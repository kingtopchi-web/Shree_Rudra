Do you know about Government solar subsidies in Madhya Pradesh? If not, don’t worry!

The solar panel subsidy in MP is a central subsidy under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana: ₹ 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, capped at ₹ 78,000 for systems of 3 kW and above. It applies to grid-connected residential rooftop solar systems and is credited directly to your bank account after commissioning.

This article covers everything you need to know about the solar subsidy in MP: how much you get, who is eligible, how to apply, and how to calculate what you will actually pay.

Most of us know that solar panel installation matters for society. Unlike fossil fuel electricity, it does not generate carbon dioxide. With the subsidy, the upfront cost drops sharply, and the savings on your electricity bill over the system’s 25-year life are far larger than what you invest.

Solar Panel Yojana MP: What Is the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana?

The solar panel yojana MP homeowners can use today is the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, launched by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) in February 2024. It aims to light up one crore households with rooftop solar by March 2027, and it applies uniformly across Madhya Pradesh, whether you live in Bhopal, Indore, Jabalpur, Gwalior, or a smaller town. Applications are made on the national portal, pmsuryaghar.gov.in.

Solar Subsidy in Madhya Pradesh 2026: Amount by System Size

The first 2 kW earn ₹ 30,000 per kW (₹ 60,000). The third kW earns ₹18,000, which brings the total to ₹ 78,000. Nothing is added beyond 3 kW, so a 3 kW system captures the full subsidy, and larger systems get the same ₹ 78,000.

Who is Eligible for Solar Panel Subsidy in MP?

Before you compare quotes or pick a system size, it’s worth checking whether your home qualifies for a solar panel subsidy in MP. The solar panel subsidy in MP follows the central PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana rules, so eligibility depends on who you are, what you own, and how your rooftop plant is installed. Here’s what the portal and your DISCOM (Distribution Company) will check.

  • You are an Indian citizen and own the house or rooftop where the system will be installed.
  • You have a valid residential electricity connection in your name from an MP DISCOM.
  • You install a grid-connected solar system with made-in-India (DCR) solar panels through a vendor registered on the national portal.
  • You have not already claimed a solar subsidy for the same property.
  • The subsidy is for residential consumers only. Commercial and industrial rooftop solar systems are not covered. Off-grid systems are not covered either.

Documents Required for the Solar System Subsidy in MP

Most subsidy delays come from small paperwork mismatches, not from the scheme itself. A name that differs between your bill and your bank account, or a blurry cheque scan, can stall an application for weeks. Keep the documents below ready before you register, and the process runs much more smoothly.

Which Solar System Size Should You Choose in MP

Bigger isn’t always better. The right system size depends on how many units you use each month, how much shadow-free roof you have, and how the subsidy is structured. Since the subsidy stops growing after 3 kW, size is the decision that most affects your payback. Here’s how to choose. MNRE suggests a rooftop capacity based on your average monthly consumption:

Average monthly unitsSuitable capacity
0-150 units1-2 kW
150-300 units2-3 kW
Above 300 unitsAbove 3 kW

As a rough guide, a 2 kW system generates about 2,800-2,900 units a year and suits a 1-2 BHK home without an air conditioner. If you want to run an AC on solar, you need at least 3 kW. You also need roughly 80 sq. ft. of shadow-free roof per kW. Read our guides on the 2 kW solar panel price in MP and the 3 kW solar panel price in MP for details.

How to Apply for the Solar Panel Subsidy in MP

Now let’s understand what are the steps to follow to apply for a solar panel subsidy in MP effectively.

  1. Register on the national portal: choose Madhya Pradesh, select your DISCOM, and enter your consumer account number.
  2. Fill in the rooftop solar application using your latest electricity bill, and add your bank details.
  3. Select SolarSquare from the list of approved vendors and discuss all the details with SolarSquare Energy (SSE).
  4. Sign the agreement with your installer and upload it to the portal.
  5. A technical officer from the discom will visit your house for inspection and approve the site if it is feasible.
  6. Afterward, the SSE team will start the installation process.
  7. Your DISCOM installs a bi-directional net meter or smart meter and completes the inspection.
  8. Once the installation is complete, commissioning of net metering will start, and the DISCOM issues a commissioning certificate.
  9. Upload the commissioning certificate on the portal to raise your subsidy claim.
  10. After approval, the subsidy is transferred directly to your bank account, typically within about 30 days. This replaces the older process where the discom paid the subsidy to the installer.

Which DISCOM Serves Your City in MP?

Madhya Pradesh has three DISCOMs, and you select yours while registering on the portal: MP Poorv Kshetra Vidyut Vitaran Company (Jabalpur and the east), MP Madhya Kshetra Vidyut Vitaran Company (Bhopal and the centre), and MP Paschim Kshetra Vidyut Vitaran Company (Indore and the west). Pick the one printed on your electricity bill when you register.

Net Metering in Madhya Pradesh

Net metering is what turns your rooftop system into a two-way arrangement with the grid. You use your solar power first, and the extra goes to your DISCOM instead of going to waste. For anyone applying for the solar subsidy in MP, it’s also a required step, because your system must be connected to the grid and verified before you can claim the subsidy.

How it works:
During the day, your panels power your home first. When they generate more than you use, the surplus flows to the grid through a bi-directional (or smart) meter, which records both the units you export and the units you import. Those exported units are credited against your future bills. At night, or on cloudy days, you draw from the grid as usual.

Key rules to know:

  • Who can apply: Consumers with a valid DISCOM connection, including residential, commercial, and industrial users.
  • System size: Up to 500 kW under the latest MPERC regulations (RG-39 of 2024).
  • Settlement: Credits are settled annually, with surplus units valued at the APPC rate.
  • Meter: A bi-directional or smart meter is mandatory.
  • No third-party sale: You can offset your own consumption, but you can’t sell solar power to someone else.
  • Subsidy: Net metering is open to all categories, but the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy applies to residential systems only.

Your installer usually handles the DISCOM coordination, so confirm this before you sign. For the full process, read our detailed guide on net metering in Madhya Pradesh.

Solar Subsidy in MP for Housing Societies, Businesses and Farmers

The subsidy conversation often focuses on individual homeowners, but solar can work for societies, offices, shops, and farms as well. The incentives differ for each, though. Here’s what’s available to each group, and where the PM Surya Ghar subsidy does and doesn’t apply.

Housing societies and RWAs

Group Housing Societies and RWAs get ₹18,000 per kW for common-facility rooftop solar (including EV charging), up to 500 kW at 3 kW per house. This limit includes the individual rooftop plants installed by residents.

Commercial and industrial buildings

There is no PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy for commercial or industrial rooftops, but they can still go solar under net metering and cut their bills.

Farmers

Farmers looking at solar pumps or farm-side solar should look at MNRE’s rel=”nofollow”, which has a different structure from PM Surya Ghar.

Can You Get a Loan for Solar in MP?

If the upfront cost is the only thing holding you back, financing may help. Rooftop solar loans are designed so the savings on your electricity bill offset a large part of the repayment. Combined with the subsidy, this can make going solar manageable without a large lump sum.

What’s available:
PM Surya Ghar portal supports collateral-free loans for rooftop systems up to 3 kW through participating banks, reported at around 7% interest. Rates, tenure, and eligibility depend on the lender, so compare offers before committing.

How to think about it:

  • Subsidy first: The subsidy is credited to your bank account after commissioning, not at the time of purchase. Plan how you’ll bridge that gap, either with your own funds or the loan.
  • Compare the monthly EMI with your current bill: If your EMI is close to or below what you pay for electricity today, the system effectively pays for itself.
  • Check the fine print: Ask about processing fees, prepayment charges, and whether the loan covers the full system cost or only part of it.
  • Ask your installer: Many installers have lending partners and can guide you through the paperwork.

Benefits of Installing Solar Systems in Madhya Pradesh

Before we learn about solar rooftop Madhya Pradesh subsidies, let’s understand why you need to install solar systems in the first place. There are mainly three reasons why you should.

  • It reduces carbon emissions: Solar panels absorb energy from the renewable energy source, the Sun. Therefore, there is no involvement of fossil fuels in generating electricity. Hence, no carbon dioxide gets emitted. When you switch to solar power, your carbon footprint drops significantly.
  • It decreases the electricity bill: With everything getting costlier, the least we can do is save money on electricity consumption. When the rays fall on the solar panel’s surface, it converts the sunlight into reusable energy. So, the need for grid electricity is very low; thus, the electricity bill also gets reduced. On-grid systems can cut electricity bills by 90% or more.
  • It pays back through the subsidy and savings: The subsidy lowers your upfront cost, and a 2 kW system in Bhopal is estimated to save about ~₹ 7.75 lakh over 25 years (indicative; see sourcing note above).

Common Mistakes That Delay the Solar Subsidy in MP

Most subsidy applications don’t fail because of big issues. They stall because of small, avoidable ones. Knowing these in advance can save you weeks of back-and-forth with your DISCOM and the portal.

  • Choosing an installer who is not registered on the national portal.
  • Using panels that are not DCR-compliant.
  • Mismatched names or consumer details between the bill, Aadhaar, and bank account.
  • Forgetting to upload the commissioning certificate after the net meter is installed.

Conclusion

Going solar in Madhya Pradesh comes down to a few clear steps. Check that you’re eligible, choose a system size that matches your usage, keep your documents ready, and apply on the national portal through a registered installer. Once your net meter is in and your commissioning certificate is uploaded, the subsidy lands in your bank account.

The numbers work in your favour. The solar panel subsidy in MP gives you ₹ 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, up to ₹ 78,000. Deduct that from your system price, and you have your real cost. A 3 kW system in Bhopal, for instance, is estimated to save around ~₹ 11.63 lakh over 25 years. Add a collateral-free loan if you need one, and the upfront cost becomes far easier to manage.

The PM Surya Ghar scheme targets households up to March 2027, so the sooner you start, the sooner your bills drop. FAQs

Does the MP government give an extra subsidy on top of the central one?

The central PM Surya Ghar subsidy is the main benefit you can get in Madhya Pradesh. There is no state solar subsidy in MP as of now, but state schemes can change, so check the state scheme section on the national portal after logging in.

Who is eligible for the solar panel yojana in MP?

Indian citizens who own their home or rooftop, hold a valid residential electricity connection and have not claimed a solar subsidy for the property before. The system must be grid-connected, use made-in-India (DCR) panels and be installed by a vendor registered on the national portal.

Can tenants or people living in rented homes get the subsidy?

Generally no, because the applicant must own the rooftop where the system is installed. If you rent, ask the property owner to apply with the installer.

How long does it take to get the solar subsidy in MP?

After your system is commissioned and you upload the certificate, the subsidy is transferred to your bank account once the claim is approved. This typically takes about 30-45 days.

Can I get the subsidy if I install solar before applying?

It is safest to register and get DISCOM approval before installation, because the approval and inspection steps are part of the claim process. If you have already installed, check with your DISCOM first.

Are solar panel rates in MP fixed?

No. Prices vary with panel type, efficiency, brand, inverter, mounting structure and city, so compare detailed quotes rather than just the per-kW rate.

Is net metering mandatory for the solar subsidy in MP?

Yes. Your system must be connected to the grid with a bi-directional or smart meter, and the DISCOM must issue a commissioning certificate before you can raise the subsidy claim.
