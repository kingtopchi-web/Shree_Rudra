What is a State Nodal Agency (SNA) for Renewable Energy? And What is MEDA?

Every time a homeowner in Maharashtra applies for a solar subsidy, a farmer in Rajasthan gets a PM KUSUM solar pump sanctioned, or a housing society in Karnataka gets its rooftop project cleared, there’s a state government agency working quietly in the background. That agency is the State Nodal Agency, or SNA.

The scale of what these agencies now handle is significant. PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana has reached a major milestone. By August 2026, India had crossed 50 lakh households benefiting from rooftop solar under the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, with ₹28,024 crore in subsidies transferred directly to beneficiaries. The scheme depends on coordination between MNRE, the National Portal, state governments, DISCOMs, vendors, and other implementing agencies. The exact role of a State Nodal Agency varies by state and scheme.

If you’ve ever applied for a solar subsidy and wondered which government office actually processes it, this is the one. Here’s what an SNA does, why every state has its own, and a closer look at Maharashtra’s version- MEDA (Maharashtra Energy Development Agency).

What Is a State Nodal Agency (SNA)?

A State Nodal Agency is a state government body, such as a corporation, society, or statutory authority, designated to implement MNRE (Ministry of New and Renewable Energy) schemes within the state.

Think of MNRE as the policy-maker in Delhi, setting national targets and scheme guidelines, and the SNA as the state-level execution arm that turns those guidelines into an actual solar connection on your rooftop. SNAs are typically structured as a society, a state corporation, or a government undertaking; never a private entity, and each one reports to its state’s energy department while staying in lockstep with MNRE’s central framework.

An SNA’s exact responsibilities depend on:

  • The renewable-energy scheme
  • The state
  • The authority appointed as the implementing agency
  • Whether the project is residential, agricultural, commercial, or utility-scale

For PM Surya Ghar, the National Portal and the consumer’s DISCOM handle most of the customer-facing process. The official MNRE page says that rooftop installations are verified by the State DISCOM and the CFA is transferred to the consumer’s account.

Why Does Every State Have Its Own SNA? 

Electricity is a Concurrent List subject in India, so both the central and state governments have a role in the sector. Solar renewable energy policies are framed centrally but implemented through state-level agencies.

This isn’t just a constitutional technicality; it plays out in three practical ways:

  • Local knowledge: Land records, state subsidy top-ups, DISCOM (Distribution Company) billing structures, and net-metering rules differ from state to state; a Delhi-based body simply can’t administer all of that efficiently.
  • State subsidy administration: Several states layer their own subsidy on top of the central PM Surya Ghar or PM KUSUM Yojana support, and only a state agency can manage that disbursal.
  • Vendor empanelment is state-specific: Installer and vendor lists are approved, audited, and blacklisted at the state level, not centrally by MNRE. This is not universally true. PM Surya Ghar now has a national vendor-registration and rating framework, even though vendors operate through particular DISCOMs and states.

What Does a State Nodal Agency Do?

Depending on the scheme and the state government’s notification, an SNA may implement renewable-energy programs, conduct resource assessments, manage tenders, support project development, run awareness programs, empanel vendors, or coordinate subsidies. Some responsibilities may instead sit with the DISCOM, state utility, regulator, SLDC (State Load Despatch Center), or another designated implementing agency.

The mandate is broader than most applicants realize. A typical SNA may handle:

  • Implement renewable-energy programs: Roll out central and state schemes related to solar, wind, biomass, small hydro, bioenergy, and other renewable-energy sources.
  • Support renewable-energy project development: Help identify suitable sites, assess project potential, and coordinate with relevant government departments and electricity utilities.
  • Assess renewable-energy resources: Study and map the state’s solar, wind, biomass, and small-hydro potential.
  • Manage tenders and demonstration projects: Invite bids and oversee renewable-energy installations for government buildings, public institutions, and state programs.
  • Promote energy conservation: Function as the State Designated Agency under the Energy Conservation Act, where assigned, and implement energy-efficiency programs.
  • Conduct training and awareness programmes: Train technicians and other stakeholders while informing consumers about renewable-energy technologies and government schemes.
  • Advise the state government: Provide technical inputs for renewable-energy policies, targets, programs, and project planning.

How Do MNRE, the SNA, the DISCOM and the State Regulator Work Together?

No single body owns the entire process; MNRE sets policy, the SNA or state implementing agency leads specific programs, the DISCOM handles the physical connection, and the SERC sets the rules everyone else operates within.

Does Every Renewable-Energy Scheme Go Through the SNA?

No. The implementing authority depends on the scheme and the state. An SNA may lead some programs, while a DISCOM or another department may implement others. Applicants should check the scheme guidelines and national portal rather than assume that every application must be submitted to the SNA.

A few examples make this clearer:

  • PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana: National Portal, registered vendor, and DISCOM-led consumer workflow.
  • PM-KUSUM: State Implementing Agency, which varies by state and component.
  • State-specific schemes: SNA, DISCOM, agriculture department, or another notified agency.

What Is MEDA?

MEDA (Maharashtra Energy Development Agency) is Maharashtra’s State Nodal Agency for renewable energy; the state body responsible for implementing solar, wind, and bio-energy schemes across Maharashtra.

Maharashtra is currently the second-highest state among the top solar states in India by PM Surya Ghar installations nationally, trailing Gujarat, with lakhs of households already benefiting from rooftop solar subsidies routed through MEDA. Here’s what the agency actually is and does.

MEDA Full Form and Overview

MEDA stands for Maharashtra Energy Development Agency, a Maharashtra government institution working with the central government to promote solar, bio-energy, and wind energy, and to drive energy conservation across the state.

MEDA acts as the State Nodal Agency under Maharashtra’s Energy Department. It serves as a key point of reference for homeowners, farmers, and developers seeking to access central renewable energy schemes in the state.

MEDA’s Dual Role

MEDA has two key roles in Maharashtra. It serves as the State Nodal Agency for renewable energy and the State Designated Agency for energy conservation.

  1. As the SNA: MEDA implements MNRE schemes across Maharashtra covering solar, wind, biomass, small hydro, and off-grid renewable energy.
  2. As the SDA: MEDA implements the Energy Conservation Act, 2001, under the guidance of the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), Ministry of Power, focused on efficiency rather than generation.

What MEDA Does in Practice for Maharashtra

Beyond subsidy processing, MEDA runs on-ground programs across solar, wind, biomass, and bagasse co-generation, and supports off-grid and biofuel projects statewide.

Concretely, MEDA:

  • Runs programs across solar, wind, biomass, small hydro, and bagasse co-generation
  • Manages off-grid solutions, including solar home systems and standalone bioenergy projects
  • Facilitates rooftop solar installations on government buildings and public institutions
  • Supports the development of second- and third-generation biofuels

Taken together, these functions are what make MEDA the default first stop for almost any renewable energy query in Maharashtra, residential, agricultural, or industrial.

State Nodal Agencies Across India

Every Indian state and union territory has its own SNA, coordinated centrally through MNRE and formalized via the Association of Renewable Energy Agencies of States (AREAS).

MNRE implements many of its programs in coordination with State Nodal Agencies (SNAs) across India. This coordination is supported by AREAS, a society registered in 2014 that brings SNAs together to share best practices and work with MNRE on technology, policy, and IT systems.

Why SNAs Matter to You

Whether you’re a homeowner, farmer, or developer, the SNA is your state’s point of contact for subsidy status, vendor approvals, and project clearances. It can also be an important escalation point when your application or project gets stuck.

For a Homeowner Applying Under PM Surya Ghar

The State Nodal Agency (SNA) helps coordinate implementation of PM Surya Ghar at the state level, while the DISCOM handles key processes such as technical feasibility, inspection, commissioning, and net-metering.

If your installation, net-metering, or subsidy process is delayed, the appropriate escalation route depends on the nature of the issue. Vendor-related complaints generally move through the DISCOM and the scheme’s designated grievance mechanism, while issues involving the National Portal or central scheme administration are handled through MNRE.

For a Farmer Applying Under PM KUSUM Yojana

PM-KUSUM is implemented through state-designated implementing agencies, and the responsible authority can vary by state and by scheme component. Depending on the state, this may be an SNA, DISCOM, agriculture department, irrigation department, or another department appointed by the state government.

For example, different states have designated agencies such as BREDA in Bihar, RRECL in Rajasthan, UPNEDA in Uttar Pradesh, and MEDA in Maharashtra.

The agency responsible for your application may handle activities such as:

  • Verifying eligibility and processing applications
  • Issuing sanctions or demand letters, where applicable
  • Selecting or allotting empanelled vendors, depending on the state’s implementation process
  • Coordinating installation and verification
  • Processing or facilitating subsidy/CFA-related payments according to the applicable state and component guidelines

The exact process is not identical across India. MNRE’s guidelines allow states to determine their implementation arrangements within the scheme framework, and the current national portal directs applicants to the relevant state implementing agency for information on how to apply.

So, before applying, check your state’s designated PM-KUSUM implementing agency and its latest application guidelines. The required documents, vendor-selection process, payment mechanism, and processing timelines may differ from one state or component to another.

For a Business or Developer

For utility-scale or commercial projects, the SNA coordinates land aggregation, grid evacuation planning with the DISCOM, and state-level regulatory clearances.

For a developer planning a large solar park in Maharashtra, MEDA helps with land aggregation, coordinates with MSEDCL (Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company Limited) on evacuation planning, and supports the project through state-level approvals. These are responsibilities that a central agency cannot handle in place of the SNA.

Who Should You Contact When a Solar Application Is Delayed?

Because PM Surya Ghar, PM KUSUM, and state-specific schemes are each led by a different body, the right point of contact for a delay depends on where in the process it’s stuck:

  • Net-metering, inspection, or commissioning delays: Contact your DISCOM directly; this stage sits with them regardless of which scheme you applied under.
  • PM Surya Ghar subsidy or National Portal issues: Raise it through the National Portal’s grievance mechanism or MNRE, since the central scheme is administered there rather than by the SNA.
  • PM KUSUM or other state-scheme delays (demand letter, vendor allotment, subsidy release): Contact your state’s SNA; MEDA in Maharashtra, BREDA in Bihar, and so on, since these steps sit entirely within the SNA’s workflow.
  • Vendor-related complaints (installation quality, timelines): Route these through the DISCOM or the scheme’s designated grievance mechanism, not the SNA directly.

Identifying which of these four buckets your issue falls into before you reach out usually saves a round of being redirected between offices.

How to Check Your Application or Subsidy Status

Central PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy status is tracked on the national portal for rooftop solar, while MEDA-specific state schemes (like PM KUSUM in Maharashtra) are tracked directly through the MEDA/Mahaurja portal. Knowing which one applies to your query saves a lot of back-and-forth.

This is one of the most common points of confusion for applicants, largely because the subsidy and the connection are tracked in two different places:

  • For PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (central subsidy): Log in at pmsuryaghar.gov.in using Consumer Login, enter your registered mobile number, and verify with the OTP/CAPTCHA. Once logged in, you can check DISCOM approval status, installation progress, and subsidy disbursal status against your application. This is the same portal you originally registered on.
  • For MEDA-administered schemes (like PM KUSUM Yojana in Maharashtra): Applicants track demand letters, vendor allotment, and subsidy release directly through mahaurja.com, under the relevant scheme section (KUSUM, off-grid solar, etc.).
  • If your status hasn’t updated in a while: Reach out to your DISCOM first for connection-related delays (net meter, inspection), and to MEDA for subsidy- or vendor-related delays. This generally resolves faster than raising a generic complaint with either office.

How to Contact MEDA and Other SNAs

MEDA can be reached via mahaurja.com or its Pune office; other major state SNAs each maintain their own official portal, and the master list is always kept current on the MNRE website.

State Nodal Agencies play a key role in implementing renewable energy schemes such as the PM KUSUM Yojana. But knowing your SNA is only one part of the process. You still need the right system design, an MNRE-approved registered vendor, and proper handling of subsidy and net-metering paperwork.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a State Nodal Agency (SNA) for renewable energy?

A State Nodal Agency (SNA) is a state government body, such as a society, corporation, or statutory undertaking, that implements MNRE’s renewable energy schemes within the state. It does not make policy; instead, it handles the on-ground execution of schemes like PM KUSUM, including application approvals, vendor empanelment, and subsidy disbursal.

What is the difference between MEDA, MSEDCL, and MERC in Maharashtra?

If you’re applying for solar in Maharashtra, it’s important to know which government body handles what. MEDA, MSEDCL, and MERC (Maharashtra Electricity Regulatory Commission) have different roles, so approaching the right one can save you time and confusion.

  • MEDA (State Nodal Agency): Handles renewable energy schemes, including subsidy processing, vendor empanelment, and scheme applications for solar, wind, and bio-energy.
  • MSEDCL (DISCOM): Handles your electricity connection, net metering, meter installation, and monthly billing.
  • MERC (Regulator): Sets electricity tariffs and regulatory policies, including net-metering rules, that MEDA and MSEDCL follow.

Which state nodal agency handles PM-KUSUM solar pumps in different states?

There is no single central office that handles PM-KUSUM applications across India. The process is managed by the State Nodal Agency (SNA) in the state where the pump or plant is being installed.

  • Bihar: BREDA
  • Rajasthan: RRECL
  • Uttar Pradesh: UPNEDA
  • Maharashtra: MEDA

The SNA typically issues the demand letter, assigns an empanelled vendor, and processes the subsidy after installation is verified. As a result, the process and timelines can vary by state. Check the table above to find the correct SNA before applying.

Is MEDA the same as MAHAURJA?

Yes. Mahaurja.com is MEDA’s official website, not a separate organisation or private portal. The name “Mahaurja” refers to Maharashtra Energy and is used by MEDA for its online services, including scheme information, application tracking, and vendor details.

What is the difference between a State Nodal Agency (SNA) and a DISCOM?

An SNA and a DISCOM have different roles in the solar installation process:

  • SNA (State Nodal Agency): Handles scheme implementation, including application approval, vendor empanelment, and subsidy disbursal.
  • DISCOM: Handles the electricity connection, including site inspection, net-meter installation, and adjustment of exported solar power in your bill.
  • For example: MEDA is the SNA in Maharashtra, while MSEDCL is the DISCOM.
  • In simple terms: A delayed subsidy is generally an SNA-related issue, while a delayed net meter is generally a DISCOM-related issue.

Is an SNA the same as a State Implementing Agency?

Yes, in most official MNRE documentation, the terms are used interchangeably. Both refer to the state-level body responsible for implementing central renewable energy schemes on the ground. Some states may use one term in their official notifications and the other in public communication, but they refer to the same agency, for example, MEDA in Maharashtra or UPNEDA in Uttar Pradesh.

Does a homeowner need to visit the SNA office for PM Surya Ghar?

No, physical visits are generally not required. The PM Surya Ghar application, vendor selection, and subsidy tracking process is designed to be completed online through the national portal. The SNA’s role happens mostly in the background, such as empanelling vendors and processing subsidy disbursal, once the DISCOM has verified the installation. You would only need to contact the SNA directly if there’s a delay or discrepancy that isn’t resolved through the portal.

Can an SNA approve a net-meter connection?

No. Net-meter approval and installation fall under the DISCOM’s responsibility, not the SNA’s. The SNA handles scheme-side approvals, such as application eligibility and subsidy processing, while the DISCOM handles the technical side, including site inspection, net-meter installation, and billing adjustments for exported solar power. If your net meter is delayed, that’s a DISCOM issue to follow up on, not an SNA one.
