What’s the Solar Subsidy in India for Grid-Tied Solar Systems?

The central government of India offers homeowners nationwide a maximum subsidy of Rs. 78,000 for grid-tied rooftop solar power systems with a capacity of 3 kW or higher under the PM Surya Ghar scheme. In addition to this central subsidy, the Delhi and Uttar Pradesh state governments provide separate state-level subsidies for eligible homeowners within their respective states.

Here are the subsidy breakdown details:

  • PM Surya Ghar solar subsidy in India for 1-2 kW rooftop solar power systems: Rs. 30,000 per kilowatt
  • PM Surya Ghar solar subsidy in India for additional capacity over and above 2 kW up to 3 kW: Rs. 18,000
  • PM Surya Ghar solar subsidy in India for 3 kW or higher capacity: Rs. 78,000
  • State subsidy in Uttar Pradesh for 1-2 kW sun panels on roof: Rs. 15,000/ kW 
  • State subsidy in Uttar Pradesh for systems >=2 kW: Rs. 30,000
  • State subsidy in Delhi for residential rooftop solar panels: Rs. 10,000/ kW. The maximum state subsidy is capped at Rs. 30,000 in Delhi.

How Long Does it Take to Install Residential Solar Power Systems at Homes in India?

At SolarSquare, the full timeline for installing residential solar power systems, from your first call to a fully commissioned rooftop solar panel system, is 40* to 45 days* for an on-grid residential installation. 

  • The first solar site visit happens within 2 days of your first call. 
  • System design, proposal, and finalization of specifications take another 2 to 3 days. 
  • Once you place the order with us, the physical installation of panels, inverter, and other components is completed in 1 week. 

After that, the file is sent to the local DISCOM for inspection, net meter installation, and final system commissioning, which takes about 1 month and is the longest leg of the timeline because it depends entirely on the utility company. 

This 40 to 45-day window is what we deliver at SolarSquare. Timelines with other installers can run longer, since DISCOM liaisoning, design turnaround, and installation scheduling vary widely across companies.

*Please note: The 40 to 45-day estimate assumes your electricity connection is ready to accept a solar system without any prior corrections. The timeline can extend when the home needs a load enhancement to match the proposed solar capacity, a phase change from single-phase to three-phase supply, a name change on the electricity connection, or any other DISCOM liaisoning to clear pending dues, fix address mismatches, or resolve documentation gaps. Rooftop access constraints, monsoon delays, DISCOM workload in your area, and the time it takes you to finalize design choices can also add days or weeks. We handle liaisoning with the DISCOM end-to-end, but the approval clock rests with the utility. 

Who is Eligible for the PM Surya Ghar Yojana?

As a homeowner in India, you can receive subsidy assistance under the PM Surya Ghar scheme if you meet the eligibility conditions listed below: 

  • You’re an Indian citizen installing a grid-tied house solar system with made-in-India solar panels.
  • You legally own the property/roof where the solar system will be installed.
  • You have an electricity connection in your name with your local DISCOM.
  • You have never received a solar subsidy under any scheme for systems up to 3 kW.

How to Apply for a Solar Subsidy in India?

Indian homeowners must register an account on the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana National portal to receive the subsidy amount straight into their bank accounts. 

You can follow these steps to receive the PM Surya Ghar subsidy for installing residential solar power systems: 

  • Step 1 – Visit the national solar portal: Go to the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana National Portal and choose the option to apply for a rooftop solar system.
  • Step 2 – Register as a consumer: Select your state, district, and electricity distribution company (DISCOM). Enter your electricity consumer number to create your account on the portal.
  • Step 3 – Enter applicant and electricity details: Enter your personal information and provide the details exactly as they appear on your latest electricity bill to begin the application process.
  • Step 4 – Upload documents and bank information: Submit the required documents listed on the portal, enter your bank account details, and upload a cancelled cheque for subsidy credit.
  • Step 5 – Choose a DISCOM-approved solar installer: Select an authorized solar vendor approved by your local DISCOM to carry out the rooftop solar installation at your property.
  • Step 6 – Upload the installer agreement: Once you finalize the installer, upload the signed installation agreement to the portal for verification.
  • Step 7 – Net meter installation: After the solar system is installed, the DISCOM replaces your existing meter with a bi-directional net meter or smart meter to track electricity import and export.
  • Step 8 – System inspection and commissioning: The DISCOM inspects the installation and issues a Solar Commissioning Certificate confirming that the system is live and compliant.
  • Step 9 – Apply for subsidy release: Log back into the portal and upload the commissioning certificate to formally request the release of your eligible solar subsidy.
  • Step 10 – Subsidy credit: Once approved, the sanctioned subsidy amount is transferred directly to your bank account within 30 working days.

On-Grid vs Off-Grid Solar Electric Panels for Homes: Which is Better?

You must weigh the grid reliability in your area when choosing between an on-grid and off-grid solar system.

  • An on-grid solar system is considered best for areas with a very reliable grid supply, making battery backup unnecessary. 
  • An off-grid solar system is highly recommended for areas without grid power or with unreliable grid service, making a battery backup essential for continuous power. 

Let’s understand how these systems work and how they differ in detail. 

What is an On-Grid Solar System for a House and How Does it Work?

An on-grid solar system is connected to the utility grid. Since the DISCOM does not cap unit exchange between the grid and the solar system, a lead-acid or lithium battery is not needed to store solar energy. This makes on-grid residential solar power systems cheaper than off-grid systems. 

Here’s a step-by-step guide on how they work:

  • Solar cells in solar panels for homes capture photons: Photons are energy packets that, when absorbed, transfer their energy to electrons within the solar cells. Energized electrons leave their place and begin to flow in a single direction, resulting in the formation of direct current (DC) power.
  • A solar inverter converts DC power to AC power: Indian homes do not run on direct current, as it can be lethal. Hence, DC power from the solar panels is fed to a solar inverter, which converts it to alternating current (AC) power.
  • Solar net metering for billing: The bi-directional meter or smart meter installed by the DISCOM tracks the electricity exchange happening between the grid and your home PV panels. When the solar system sends more power to the grid than what it imports, the DISCOM gives you credits because you’re supplying electricity to the grid. However, when the solar system draws more power from the grid than what is exported, you pay the DISCOM for the additional grid units used in your home.

What is an Off-Grid Solar System for a Home and How Does it Work?

An off-grid solar system, also known as a standalone solar system, is connected to lead-acid or lithium batteries rather than the grid. Although the use of batteries and planning for complete autonomy make off-grid systems costlier, they’re essential in areas where grid power is unreliable or unavailable. 

Since there’s no grid to fall back on in off-grid PV systems, the system is designed for complete autonomy. Additional panels and a large battery backup are included so the system can power the entire home for at least 2 days, even in the complete absence of solar production. This is what makes off-grid residential solar power systems the most expensive.

Here’s the step-by-step working process:

  • Solar cells in solar panels for homes capture photons: They produce DC power the same way as generated in on-grid sun panels on roofs.
  • Excess DC power is stored in the batteries: Electricity generated by household solar panels that is not used in real time is used to charge the battery bank. This stored power is used at night or whenever needed.
