    How Does an On-Grid Rooftop Solar System in Delhi NCR Work?

    An on-grid solar system in NCR works the same way it works everywhere else in the country, i.e., through the photovoltaic effect. Solar panels capture sunlight and convert it into electricity.

    Here’s the step-by-step breakdown of the entire working process:

    • Solar cells in solar panels capture photons from sunlight: The absorbed photons transfer their energy to electrons within the solar cells, causing them to flow in one direction. This unidirectional flow of electrons is direct current (DC) power.
    • DC from the panels is converted to alternating current (AC): A solar inverter converts DC to AC before sending power to homes because homes in India are not supplied with DC, as it can be lethal.
    • Solar net metering to track energy exchange: The bidirectional net meter, or smart meter, installed by the DISCOM at your property after the solar system is installed, tracks the electricity exchange between the grid and the PV system. When the system sends more units to the grid than it imports, the DISCOM gives you credits. When the system imports more units from the grid than it exports, you pay the DISCOM for those additional units at standard electricity tariffs in your city.

    On-Grid vs Off-Grid Solar Systems for Homes in NCR: Which is Better?

    Solar systems for homes in NCR generally fall into two categories, and the right choice depends entirely on how dependable your local electricity supply is.

    • On-grid solar systems operate in sync with the utility grid and do not rely on lead-acid or lithium batteries. In most NCR locations, where power availability is consistent and outages are limited, this setup works well. Because there is no battery storage involved, installation costs remain lower, making on-grid systems a practical option for urban homes.
    • Off-grid solar systems function independently of the utility grid and store surplus daytime electricity in lead-acid or lithium batteries for later use. This setup is suitable for areas where grid access is absent or unreliable. The added storage increases the system cost but ensures electricity availability during extended outages or when the grid supply is unavailable.
