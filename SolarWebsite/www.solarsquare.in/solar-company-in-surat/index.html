Eligible homeowners in Surat can receive subsidy support from both the central and Gujarat governments.

From 22 October 2026, Gujarat also provides an additional state subsidy for eligible residential rooftop solar systems of up to 2.18 kWp.

The state subsidy is calculated using a government benchmark cost of ₹ 50,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp and ₹ 45,000 per kWp for capacity above 2 kWp up to 2.18 kWp. For general-category homes, the household bears 10% of this benchmark cost. The central CFA is deducted first, and the Gujarat government covers the remaining eligible amount.

How Does an On-Grid Rooftop Solar System in Surat Work?

An on-grid rooftop solar system in Surat works the same way it works in any other city. Solar panels absorb solar radiation and convert it into DC electricity.

Here’s a step-by-step breakdown of the entire working and billing process:

  • Sunlight initiates electricity generation at the panel level: When solar radiation strikes rooftop solar panels, the photovoltaic cells inside them respond instantly. Photons in sunlight are absorbed, exciting electrons in the solar cells and causing them to move. This controlled movement of electrons produces electrical power in the form of direct current (DC).
  • The solar inverter converts DC power into usable electricity: Electricity generated by the panels flows to the solar inverter, which converts direct current (DC) into alternating current (AC). Once converted, AC power runs your lights, appliances, and other household loads.
  • Solar net metering for billing: A bidirectional net meter or smart meter installed by the DISCOM records the amount of electricity flowing between your solar system and the grid. Any surplus solar power sent to the grid earns you credits. If your household consumes more grid electricity than it exports during a billing cycle, you are charged for those additional units.

On-Grid vs Off-grid Solar Systems in Surat: Which is Better?

An on-grid solar system is connected to the grid, not lead-acid or lithium batteries, making it the more affordable option of the two. Since it does not store solar energy in batteries, an on-grid solar system is a better investment for homes in areas with a stable grid supply and rare power cuts. That’s because these areas will not require power backup, as power outages will be infrequent.

On the other hand, off-grid solar systems are connected to lead-acid or lithium batteries, not the grid. Excess solar energy from the panels is stored in the battery bank, which can be used at night or during periods of low solar output to keep the load running. It’s costlier than an on-grid PV system but highly recommended in areas with an unstable or unavailable grid supply, as in both cases, backup power will be required.

How do they work?Solar panels produce electricity during the day. Any power not used in real time is sent to the grid. At night, the system draws power from the grid to keep appliances running.The electricity generated by the solar panels during the day is used in two ways:

  • It keeps the load running in real time.
  • Excess energy is used to charge the lead-acid or lithium batteries through a solar panel charge controller.

Electricity stored in the battery bank keeps the load running at night.
